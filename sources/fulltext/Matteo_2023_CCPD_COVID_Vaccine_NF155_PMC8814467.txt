Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination with Anti-Neurofascin155 IgG Following COVID-19 Vaccination

Authors: Eleonora Matteo, Michele Romoli, Calogero Calabro, et al.
Journal: Canadian Journal of Neurological Sciences, 2023;50(1):141-143
PMID: 35107062 | PMCID: PMC8814467 | DOI: 10.1017/cjn.2021.256

CASE REPORT

A 52-year-old male presented 11 days after ChAdOx1 nCoV-19 vaccination with sudden-onset tingling in fingers and toes with reduced hand dexterity.

CLINICAL COURSE:
- Day 0: Cranial nerve VII involvement, speech difficulties, bilateral hand weakness (MRC 4/5)
- Day 2: Rapid deterioration with double vision, facial weakness, swallowing difficulty

DIAGNOSTICS:
- EMG: Severe, distally predominant demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy
- Spine MRI: Contrast enhancement of cauda equina nerve roots
- Brain MRI: Contrast-enhancing multifocal bilateral demyelinating lesions
- Serum: Positive for anti-NF155 IgG autoantibodies
- Negative for anti-contactin-1, anti-CNTN-1/CASPR-1 complex

TREATMENT:
- IVIg (0.4 g/kg x 5 days) starting Day 3: modest improvement
- Day 4: Hypercapnic respiratory failure requiring ICU
- Day 30: High-dose IV corticosteroids (1g x 5 days): complete clinical recovery
- Discharge on oral prednisone, stable at 2-month follow-up

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
- PNS involvement preceded CNS complications
- Anti-NF155 autoantibodies present
- Intrathecal IL-8 elevation indicated localized CNS autoimmune process
- Temporal relationship with vaccination suggested autoimmune mechanism
- Recent vaccination/infection triggered two-thirds of CCPD cases in large series
