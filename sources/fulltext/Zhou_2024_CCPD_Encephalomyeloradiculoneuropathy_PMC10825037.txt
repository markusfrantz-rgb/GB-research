Combined central and peripheral demyelination: a case report resembling encephalomyeloradiculoneuropathy
Frontiers in Neurology 2024;14:1288546
Authors: Xuan Zhou, Ali Peng, Chuan Li, Lin Li, Dan Yao, Yunfeng Hao, Chao Zhao, Qi Yan, Ying Li, Juntong Liu, Shuyu Liu, Wenping Zhu, Ying Du, Wei Zhang
PMID: 38292033 | PMCID: PMC10825037 | DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2023.1288546

=== ABSTRACT ===

A 14-year-old teenager initially presented with symptoms of acute myelitis (AM), followed by GBS manifestations. Hepatitis E virus infection appeared to trigger the GBS component. The patient responded well to corticosteroids, IVIg, and Rituximab, demonstrating the potential responsiveness and reversibility of CCPD.

=== CLINICAL PRESENTATION ===

Patient: 14-year-old male
Preceding event: Hepatitis E virus (HEV) infection

Symptoms:
- Weakness and tingling in lower extremities
- Urinary retention
- Progressed to tetraplegia

=== DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS ===

Imaging:
- Spinal cord edema without obvious enhancement
- Normal brain MRI initially

CSF:
- Protein-cytological dissociation

NCS:
- Demyelinating polyneuropathy

Laboratory:
- Elevated transaminases (ALT 205 U/L, AST 60 U/L)
- HEV infection confirmed
- Anti-AQP4: Negative
- Anti-MOG: Negative

=== TREATMENT COURSE ===

Phase 1: IV methylprednisolone
- Partial improvement only

Phase 2: IVIg added
- Better response
- Full strength (5/5) recovered in all upper extremity muscle groups

Phase 3: Rituximab infusions added
- Sustained improvement at 4-month follow-up

=== DISCUSSION ===

Key points:

1. No standardized criteria exist for diagnosing CCPD
2. Distinction between CCPD and encephalomyeloradiculoneuropathy (EMRN) is unclear
3. HEV-GBS association: HEV infection responsible for 5-11% of sporadic GBS cases in developed countries
4. Molecular mimicry and direct neuronal infection are potential pathways

Treatment hierarchy for CCPD:
1. First-line: IV methylprednisolone (for CNS component)
2. Add IVIg if incomplete response (for PNS component)
3. Rituximab as secondary treatment for poor responders
4. Aggressive immunomodulation when standard therapies insufficient

=== CLINICAL RELEVANCE ===

This case demonstrates:
1. Stepwise escalation of immunotherapy is effective
2. Corticosteroids alone may provide only partial benefit in CCPD
3. IVIg addresses the PNS component that steroids alone cannot
4. Rituximab offers a safety net for sustained immunosuppression
5. Complete recovery is possible with aggressive combined treatment
6. HEV should be tested as a triggering infection
