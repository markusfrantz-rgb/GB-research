Spinal MRI Findings of Guillain-Barre Syndrome

Authors: Ozlem Alkan, Tulin Yildirim, Naime Tokmak, Meliha Tan
Journal: Journal of Radiology Case Reports
Year: 2009
PMID: 22470650
PMCID: PMC3303301
DOI: 10.3941/jrcr.v3i3.153

KEY SPINAL MRI FINDINGS

Enhancement Patterns:
Marked contrast enhancement of anterior and posterior nerve roots in the conus medullaris and cauda equina on contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MR imaging. Precontrast imaging appeared normal.

Gadolinium Findings:
Marked enhancement of nerve roots indicates a breakdown of the blood-nerve barrier, reflecting inflammatory infiltration characteristic of GBS.

Diagnostic Significance:
Enhancement is nonspecific - also appears in neoplastic and other inflammatory conditions. However, it serves as a supplementary diagnostic tool, especially when clinical and electrophysiologic findings are equivocal.

Enhancement solely of the anterior roots is strongly suggestive of GBS.
Enhancement of cauda equina nerve roots with gadolinium is 83% sensitive for acute GBS and was present in 95% of typical cases.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS FOR NERVE ROOT ENHANCEMENT

- Leptomeningeal carcinomatosis
- Sarcoidosis
- Lymphoma
- CIDP (Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculopathy)
- Arachnoiditis
- Disc herniation and radiculitis

Clinical Outcome:
Patient responded to plasmapheresis with near-complete symptom resolution within 6 weeks.
