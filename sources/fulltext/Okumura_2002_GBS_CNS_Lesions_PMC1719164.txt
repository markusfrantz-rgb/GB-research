Guillain-Barre Syndrome Associated with Central Nervous System Lesions

Authors: A Okumura, H Ushida, K Maruyama, K Itomi, Y Ishiguro, M Takahashi, A Osuga, T Negoro, K Watanabe
Journal: Archives of Disease in Childhood
Year: 2002 (April), Volume 86, Issue 4, Pages 304-306
DOI: 10.1136/adc.86.4.304
PMID: 11919115
PMCID: PMC1719164

=== FULL TEXT ===

ABSTRACT

This case report describes a pediatric patient with an unusual presentation combining Guillain-Barre syndrome with central nervous system involvement. During immunoglobulin treatment, the patient developed "headache with meningism." Brain imaging revealed focal lesions in the frontal and right occipital lobes.

KEY FINDINGS

MRI Results:
- High intensity areas in the deep white matter in the bilateral frontal lobes
- Abnormal signal in the cortex and subcortex in the right occipital lobe
- Follow-up imaging showed cavity formation in the deep white matter
- Atrophic changes in the right occipital lobes documented one year post-discharge

CSF Analysis:
- Raised protein concentration accompanied by mild pleocytosis

Clinical Course:
- Recovery occurred within two months
- Permanent imaging changes (cavity formation, atrophy) despite clinical recovery

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

1. GBS can be associated with true CNS white matter lesions
2. These lesions can occur in frontal and occipital lobes
3. CNS involvement may present during IVIg treatment (though causality is uncertain)
4. Permanent structural brain changes can persist even after clinical recovery
5. The combination of peripheral neuropathy and CNS lesions suggests shared pathogenic mechanisms
6. Mild pleocytosis (unusual for GBS) may indicate CNS inflammatory involvement
