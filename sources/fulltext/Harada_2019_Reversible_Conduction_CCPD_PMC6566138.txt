Reversible Conduction Failure in Anti-lactosylceramide-antibody-positive Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination

Authors: Masaya Harada, Shiroh Miura, Hiroshi Kida, et al.
Journal: Frontiers in Neurology, 2019;10:600
PMID: 31231307 | PMCID: PMC6566138 | DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2019.00600

CASE REPORT

A 60-year-old woman with CCPD positive for serum anti-lactosylceramide antibody exhibited reversible conduction failure (RCF) on nerve conduction studies.

PRESENTATION:
- Progressive lower limb weakness and sensory disturbances below chest level
- Girdle sensation in right abdominal region
- Absent deep tendon reflexes with bilateral Babinski responses

DIAGNOSTICS:
- Serum anti-lactosylceramide antibody (IgG): positive
- Negative for anti-AQP4, anti-MOG, and various anti-glycolipid antibodies
- CSF: pleocytosis with elevated protein, myelin basic protein elevated (818 pg/mL), oligoclonal bands positive
- MRI: multiple abnormal signal intensities in medulla and spinal cord
- NCS: reduced CMAPs, conduction block in right tibial nerve, absent F waves

TREATMENT:
- Responded to methylprednisolone pulse therapy and plasma exchange
- CNS lesions showed relatively poor response compared to PNS improvement
- Relapse at ~2 months with similar symptoms
- Sustained remission with maintenance oral corticosteroid (20 mg/day)

UNIQUE ASPECTS:
- Reversible conduction failure during both initial presentation and relapse
- Relapsing-remitting pattern (differs from typical CCPD)
- Anti-lactosylceramide antibodies cause reversible physiological blocking similar to axonal GBS
- Links to encephalomyeloradiculoneuropathy (EMRN)
