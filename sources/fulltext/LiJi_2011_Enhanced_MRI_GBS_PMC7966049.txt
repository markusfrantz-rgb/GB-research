The Diagnostic, Prognostic, and Differential Value of Enhanced MR Imaging in Guillain-Barre Syndrome

Authors: H-f Li, X-j Ji
Journal: AJNR American Journal of Neuroradiology
Year: 2011
PMID: 21596802
PMCID: PMC7966049
DOI: 10.3174/ajnr.A2620

KEY FINDINGS

Diagnostic Value:
- 70.6% of GBS patients had elevated CSF protein
- 85.7% had positive electrophysiologic findings
- Asymptomatic enhancement of nerve roots had no clinical association
- Questions additional diagnostic benefit beyond clinical evaluation, electrophysiology, and CSF

Enhancement Patterns:
Nerve root enhancement occurs due to blood-nerve barrier breakdown during inflammatory phase. Grading: absent, present, strongly present.

Prognostic Value:
- Prominent nerve root enhancement accompanied increased leg weakness severity
- Correlation between intracranial findings and clinical outcomes poorly established
- Outcome assessment requires adjustment for baseline severity, electrophysiologic classification, CSF protein, immunotherapy, and timing

Differential Diagnosis (GBS vs CIDP):
Enhanced MRI may help differentiate GBS from CIDP during recovery phase (6-8 weeks post-onset), when acute inflammation subsides in GBS while persisting in CIDP.
