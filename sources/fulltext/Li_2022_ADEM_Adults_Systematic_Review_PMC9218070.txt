Clinical Presentation and Outcomes of Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis in Adults Worldwide: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Frontiers in Immunology 2022;13:870867
Authors: Kunyi Li, Maolin Li, Lan Wen, Qiancheng Wang, Xin Ding, Jian Wang
PMID: 35757742 | PMCID: PMC9218070 | DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2022.870867

=== ABSTRACT ===

This meta-analysis examined clinical features and functional outcomes in 437 adults with ADEM across 12 studies (1990-2021). Common diagnostic features included white matter lesions (87.1%), polyfocal onset (80.5%), and pyramidal signs (68.7%). Mortality reached 7.8%, with 47.5% experiencing residual deficits, indicating worse outcomes than children.

=== METHODS ===

- 12 studies (11 retrospective, 1 prospective) published 2000-2020
- Locations: Europe (4), Asia (4), Oceania (1), North America (1), South America (1), multi-regional (1)
- Total: 437 adult patients
- Mean age: 37.1 years; 41.7% male

=== CLINICAL PRESENTATION ===

Antecedent events:
- Preceding infections in ~50% of cases
- Mean interval to onset: 12.5 ± 21.7 days
- Upper respiratory tract infections most common (25.7%)
- Vaccination-related: Less common than in pediatric cases

Neurological symptoms (prevalence):
- Polyfocal presentation: 80.5%
- Pyramidal signs: 68.7%
- Motor deficits: 63.4%
- Gait abnormalities: 52.0%
- Sensory symptoms: 47.3%
- Cognitive changes: 46.2%
- Bladder dysfunction: 38.1%
- Visual symptoms: 33.2%
- Cerebellar signs: 29.1%

=== MRI CHARACTERISTICS ===

- Abnormal brain imaging: 91.6%
- White matter involvement: 87.1%
- Gadolinium enhancement: 58.0%
- Spinal cord abnormalities: 41.6%
- Lesion characteristics: Diffuse, poorly demarcated, large (>1-2 cm)
- Predominantly involving cerebral white matter

=== CSF FINDINGS ===

- Abnormal in 70% of patients
- Pleocytosis: 51.8%
- Elevated protein: 39.1%
- Positive CSF OCBs: Only ~20% (vs. >95% in MS)

=== TREATMENT ===

Standard management:
- Corticosteroids: 95.2% of patients
- Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg): 16.4%
- Plasma exchange (PLEX): 7.3%
- ICU admission required: 39.7%
- Mean hospitalization: 23.1 days

KEY FINDING: Corticosteroids are the universal first-line treatment for adult ADEM, used in >95% of cases. IVIg and PLEX are reserved for steroid-resistant or severe cases.

=== OUTCOMES ===

Mortality: 7.8%
Residual deficits: 47.5%
ADEM recurrence: 7.2%
Mean follow-up: 2.8 ± 3.6 years

Regional mortality differences:
- Asia: 14.5%
- Europe: 5.5%
- Americas: 0.4-5.0%

=== ADULT vs PEDIATRIC ADEM ===

Key differences:
1. Adults have WORSE outcomes (mortality 7.8% vs <5% in children)
2. Adults more often need ICU admission (39.7%)
3. Residual deficits more common in adults (47.5% vs <25%)
4. OCB positivity higher in adults (~20% vs <5%)
5. More frequent pyramidal signs in adults
6. Pediatric IPMSSG criteria unsuitable for ~50% of adult presentations

=== TREATMENT IMPLICATIONS ===

1. No randomized controlled trials exist for ADEM treatment (any age)
2. All treatment evidence is based on case series and retrospective data
3. IV methylprednisolone remains first-line despite limited evidence
4. Adult-specific diagnostic and treatment guidelines are urgently needed
5. Early and aggressive treatment is associated with better outcomes

=== RELEVANCE FOR ADEM-GBS OVERLAP ===

- Adults with ADEM already have worse outcomes than children
- When ADEM co-occurs with GBS, the disease burden is compounded
- Early combined immunotherapy (steroids + IVIg) is critical
- ICU support is frequently required (~40% even for ADEM alone)
- Long-term follow-up needed (7.2% recurrence rate)
