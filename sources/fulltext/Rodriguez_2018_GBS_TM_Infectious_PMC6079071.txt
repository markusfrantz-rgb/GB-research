Guillain-Barre Syndrome, Transverse Myelitis and Infectious Diseases

Authors: Yhojan Rodriguez, Manuel Rojas, Yovana Pacheco, Yeny Acosta-Ampudia, Carolina Ramirez-Santana, Diana M Monsalve, M Eric Gershwin, Juan-Manuel Anaya
Journal: Cellular & Molecular Immunology, 2018;15(6):547-562
PMID: 29375121 | PMCID: PMC6079071 | DOI: 10.1038/cmi.2017.142

COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW

GBS is an acute inflammatory immune-mediated polyradiculoneuropathy affecting 0.8-1.9 per 100,000 annually. About 75% of GBS patients have a history of preceding infection within 6 weeks.

BACTERIAL TRIGGERS:
- Campylobacter jejuni: Most significant (30-40% of GBS cases), risk of 1.17 per 1,000 infected
- Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, various hepatitis viruses

VIRAL ASSOCIATIONS:
- CMV: Second most frequent infectious etiology
- Herpes simplex virus, Zika virus, Dengue, Chikungunya, EBV

PATHOGENIC MECHANISMS:
1. Molecular mimicry: Bacterial lipopolysaccharides structurally resemble human nerve gangliosides
2. Humoral responses: Anti-ganglioside antibodies
3. Cellular responses: T-lymphocyte activation
4. Innate immune mechanisms

TRANSVERSE MYELITIS:
- Inflammatory disorder of the spinal cord
- Similar infectious triggers and pathogenic mechanisms
- Immune complex deposition and molecular mimicry

HOST FACTORS:
- Genetic predisposition via HLA alleles
- Only some individuals develop GBS/TM after infection
- Complex host-pathogen interactions

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS:
Preventive measures and therapeutic interventions require simultaneous evaluation of microbial and human genetic factors.
