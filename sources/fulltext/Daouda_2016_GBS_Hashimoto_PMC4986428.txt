Guillain-Barre syndrome and Hashimoto's thyroiditis
Toudou Daouda M, Obenda NS, Maazou L, Camara D, Souirti Z, Elmidaoui A, Belahsen MF
QJM: An International Journal of Medicine. 2016;109(8):547-548
PMID: 26792854 | PMCID: PMC4986428 | DOI: 10.1093/qjmed/hcw007

CASE PRESENTATION
44-year-old woman without prior medical history presented with limb weakness starting two days earlier. Symptoms progressed from lower limb numbness and paresthesias to progressive lower limb weakness extending to the upper limbs in 24 hours. Physical examination: flaccid quadriplegia with absent reflexes.

DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS
- Brain and spinal MRI: normal
- CSF: albuminocytologic dissociation with protein level of 128 mg/l
- Electroneuromyography: acute demyelinating polyneuropathy
- Infectious serologies negative (CMV, EBV, Mycoplasma, others)
- Anti-thyroperoxidase antibody: 229.76 IU/ml (normal <5.6)

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Plasma membranes of thyrocytes and neuronal cells are rich in gangliosides. This shared molecular target may trigger autoantibodies causing both conditions through immunological cross-reactivity rather than coincidence.

The association of GBS with Hashimoto's thyroiditis is a rare clinical situation. While GBS associates with various autoimmune diseases, pairing with Hashimoto's is uncommon.

TREATMENT
Immunoglobulin therapy for five days with favorable clinical response.

SIGNIFICANCE
Autoantibodies against thyrocytes may cross-react with neuronal cells, suggesting shared pathophysiological mechanism. Importance of screening for concurrent autoimmune conditions in GBS patients.
