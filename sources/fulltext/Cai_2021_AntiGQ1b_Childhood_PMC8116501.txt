Clinical Characterization of Anti-GQ1b Antibody Syndrome in Childhood

Authors: Lianhong Cai, Zhanqi Hu, Jianxiang Liao, Siqi Hong, Lingyu Kong, Li Chen, Yetao Luo, Tingsong Li, Li Jiang
Journal: Frontiers in Pediatrics
Year: 2021
PMID: 33996691
PMCID: PMC8116501
DOI: 10.3389/fped.2021.649053

STUDY OVERVIEW

Examined 78 children with anti-GQ1b antibody syndrome (12 new cases + 66 published cases). Median onset age 10 years (range 2-18).

CLINICAL SPECTRUM

Most Common Phenotypes:
- Acute ophthalmoparesis: 32% (25 cases)
- Miller Fisher syndrome: 15% (12 cases)
- Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis: 12% (9 cases)

Onset Symptoms:
- External ophthalmoplegia: 48% (most frequent)
- Sensory disturbance: 9%
- Bulbar palsy: 9%

MRI FINDINGS

Brain or spinal MRI abnormalities in 18% (12/68 cases):
- Cerebral white matter, cerebellum, and brainstem lesions: 7 cases
- Cranial nerve involvement: 3 cases
- Myelitis-like demonstration: 1 case
- Enhanced meningeal signal: 1 case
- Additional: white matter, basal ganglia, and periaqueductal areas

LABORATORY FINDINGS

CSF: Albuminocytologic dissociation in 34% (23/68) - significantly lower than classic GBS (77%)
NCS: Abnormal in 60% (27/45 cases)

Anti-GQ1b Antibodies:
- Serum-positive: 92% (72/78)
- Anti-GT1a most common concurrent antibody (55%)

TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS

- IVIG alone or combined with steroids/PE: 58% (42/72)
- Complete recovery within 1 year: 81% (57/70)
- Favorable prognosis regardless of onset age or phenotype
