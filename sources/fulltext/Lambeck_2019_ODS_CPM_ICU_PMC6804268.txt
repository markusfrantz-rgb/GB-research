Central Pontine Myelinosis and Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome

Authors: Johann Lambeck, Maren Hieber, Andrea Dressing, Wolf-Dirk Niesen
Journal: Deutsches Arzteblatt International
Year: 2019
PMID: 31587708
PMCID: PMC6804268
DOI: 10.3238/arztebl.2019.0600

OVERVIEW

ODS is a secondary neurological condition caused by primary underlying diseases, characterized by myelin loss in specific brain regions. Encompasses both central pontine (CPM) and extrapontine manifestations.

RISK FACTORS IN ICU PATIENTS

ICU incidence: 2.5%
Key vulnerabilities:
- Severe electrolyte imbalances, particularly hypokalemia (up to 41% of cases)
- Rapid sodium correction in chronic hyponatremia
- Concurrent hypokalemia with hyponatremia
- Renal failure or hemodialysis

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

Clinical features: Biphasic clinical course, initial electrolyte imbalance or other risk factors, appearance of pontine or extrapyramidal symptoms after 1-14 days.

MRI FINDINGS

- Hyperintensity in T2 and T2-FLAIR sequences
- Hypointensity in T1 sequences
- Characteristic "trident sign" in pontine region
- Positive findings may appear within first 24 hours post-symptom onset
- Early inconspicuous MRI warrants follow-up at 1-2 weeks
- DWI, T2, and T2-FLAIR considered equivalent for detection
- CT sensitivity only 25-28.5% vs MRI

PROGNOSIS

- 33-50% achieve favorable outcomes
- 24-39% reach complete functional recovery
- 33-55% require ongoing care or die

RELEVANCE TO GBS ICU PATIENTS

SIADH/hyponatremia affects ~50% of GBS patients. If sodium is corrected too rapidly, osmotic demyelination can occur, producing white matter lesions that may be confused with other CNS pathology. This is a critical differential for ICU patients with GBS who develop new brain MRI abnormalities.
