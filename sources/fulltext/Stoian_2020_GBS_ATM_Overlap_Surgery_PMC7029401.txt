Guillain-Barre and Acute Transverse Myelitis Overlap Syndrome Following Obstetric Surgery

Authors: Adina Stoian, Anca Motataianu, Zoltan Bajko, Adrian Balasa
Journal: Journal of Critical Care Medicine, 2020;6(1):74-79
PMID: 32104735 | PMCID: PMC7029401 | DOI: 10.2478/jccm-2020-0008

CASE REPORT

A 16-year-old pregnant patient developed concurrent GBS and acute transverse myelitis (ATM) following therapeutic pregnancy termination under general anesthesia.

PRESENTATION:
- Two weeks post-procedure: paraesthesia ascending from lower to upper extremities
- Motor deficits: 2/5 upper limbs, 0/5 lower limbs
- Absent deep tendon reflexes
- Acute urine retention and fecal incontinence
- Sensory level at D1

DIAGNOSTICS:
- NCS: AMAN subtype GBS (axonal motor, >70% CMAP reduction, absent F waves)
- Spinal MRI: T2 hyperintense lesion (no enhancement due to concurrent steroids)
- CSF: Normal
- Extensive infectious, autoimmune, paraneoplastic workup negative

TREATMENT:
- 5 plasma exchange sessions
- IV methylprednisolone 1g x 5 days
- IVIg 0.4 g/kg x 5 days
- Result: Modest upper limb improvement (0/5 -> 3/5), minimal lower limb recovery
- Discharged on corticosteroids + azathioprine

LITERATURE REVIEW:
- As of 2019: 24 cases of GBS-ATM overlap documented
- Predominantly pediatric (mean age 21.3 years)
- Female predominance
- TM most common acquired CNS demyelinating syndrome in GBS overlap (32%)
- 85% of overlap identified within 4 weeks of initial diagnosis
- No established first-choice therapy guidelines

PROGNOSIS:
- AMSAN/AMAN subtypes carry worse prognosis
- ATM combined with GBS portends poorer outcomes than either alone
- All 5 identified ATM-AMAN overlap cases had poor short-term prognosis
