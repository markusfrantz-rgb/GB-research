Concurrent Acute Sensorimotor Axonal Neuropathy and Disseminated Encephalitis Associated with Chlamydia pneumoniae in an Adult Patient with Anti-MOG and Anti-Sulfatide Antibodies: A Case Report

Authors: Michail Papantoniou, Grigorios Panagopoulos
Journal: Therapeutic Advances in Neurological Disorders, 2024;17:17562864241237850
PMID: 38495363 | PMCID: PMC10944586 | DOI: 10.1177/17562864241237850

CASE REPORT

A 60-year-old male developed simultaneous brain and peripheral nerve inflammation following an upper respiratory infection.

PRESENTATION:
- Irritability, asymmetric weakness, sensory loss, absent reflexes
- Symptoms 3 weeks post-respiratory infection

DIAGNOSTICS:
- Brain MRI: Abnormal lesions in hemispheres, brainstem, cerebellum with peripheral enhancement
- Nerve conduction studies: Reduced sensory and motor amplitudes (axonal neuropathy)
- Positive C. pneumoniae serology
- Anti-MOG IgG (1:80 titer)
- Anti-sulfatide IgG (1:1000 titer)

TREATMENT:
- IVIg 30g daily x 5 days + methylprednisolone 1000mg x 3 days
- Oral prednisone taper
- At 3 months: remarkable clinical and radiological improvement
- Antibodies became seronegative

SIGNIFICANCE:
First case report with concurrent AMSAN and ADEM with both anti-sulfatide and anti-MOG antibodies associated with C. pneumoniae.
