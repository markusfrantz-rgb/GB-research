Coexistence of Myositis, Transverse Myelitis, and Guillain Barre Syndrome Following Mycoplasma pneumoniae Infection in an Adolescent

Authors: Yasemin Topcu, Erhan Bayram, Pakize Karaoglu, Uluc Yis, Handan Guleryuz, Semra Hiz Kurul
Journal: Journal of Pediatric Neurosciences, 2013;8(1):59-63
PMID: 23772249 | PMCID: PMC3680901 | DOI: 10.4103/1817-1745.111428

CASE REPORT

A 14-year-old female presented with numbness and weakness of all limbs, preceded by 9 days of back pain. Upper respiratory infection 10 days before symptom onset.

CLINICAL FINDINGS:
- Proximal weakness 3/5, distal 4/5 in all extremities
- Elevated CK (2190 U/L)
- Positive M. pneumoniae IgM and rising IgG titers
- Spinal MRI: Hyperintense lesions C4-T3 (anterior cervicothoracic spinal cord)
- NCS: Acute motor polyneuropathy (reduced amplitudes, prolonged F latencies)
- CSF: Normal protein and glucose, negative for infections

DIAGNOSES: Concurrent myositis + transverse myelitis + GBS (acute motor polyneuropathy) post-M. pneumoniae

TREATMENT:
- IVIg 1 g/kg x 2 days + clarithromycin 15 mg/kg x 2 weeks
- Day 15: Muscle strength improved to 4-5/5 proximally, 5/5 distally
- Discharged walking independently

PATHOGENESIS:
- Neurological complications in 0.01-4.8% of M. pneumoniae infections
- Autoimmune mechanism: antibodies against galactocerebroside-C, GM1, GM2, Gt1b, GQ1b
- Common epitope hypothesis: shared pathological epitope between CNS and PNS myelin
- Prior to this case, only one pediatric case of M. pneumoniae-associated GBS with TM documented
