Overlapping Central and Peripheral Nervous System Syndromes in MOG Antibody-Associated Disorders

Authors: Simon Rinaldi, Alexander Davies, Janev Fehmi, et al.; Australian and New Zealand MOG Study Group
Journal: Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation, 2021;8(1):e924
PMID: 33272955 | PMCID: PMC7803332 | DOI: 10.1212/NXI.0000000000000924

STUDY SUMMARY

From 271 MOG seropositive adults (2013-2018), 19 patients with peripheral nervous system involvement were identified and characterized.

KEY FINDINGS:
- All 19 had CNS involvement (optic neuritis 83% of episodes)
- Peripheral manifestations included: AIDP, myeloradiculitis, multifocal motor neuropathy, brachial neuritis, migrant sensory neuritis, and radicular pain
- MRI spine was normal in 16/19 at peripheral symptom onset
- Immunotherapy produced partial or complete symptom resolution in 80% (12/15 treated)
- 4 patients had serum antibodies against NF155, CASPR2, or GM1

PNS SYNDROME CLASSIFICATION:
- Confirmed diagnoses (6): AIDP (1), myeloradiculitis (2), multifocal motor neuropathy (1), brachial neuritis (2)
- Less defined syndromes (13): distal limb pain/paresthesia (5), radicular pain (3), combined (2), migrant sensory neuritis (3)

ANTIBODY FINDINGS:
- Patient 5: NF155 positive (1:6,400), IgG1/2/4 binding
- Patient 8: CASPR2 positive (up to 1:12,800)
- Patients 7 & 17: Low-titer GM1 IgM positive
- No patients positive for NF186, contactin-1, CASPR-1, LGI1, GQ1b, or sulfatide

TREATMENT OUTCOMES (15/19 treated):
- Complete resolution: 3 patients
- Partial resolution: 9 patients
- No response: 3 patients
- 5 patients had peripheral symptom recurrence

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS:
- MOG antibody testing should be incorporated into workups for patients with peripheral nerve root enhancement, myeloradiculitis, and CCPD
- Myeloradiculitis, CCPD, and inflammatory neuropathies may be associated with MOGAD
- These presentations are often immunotherapy responsive
