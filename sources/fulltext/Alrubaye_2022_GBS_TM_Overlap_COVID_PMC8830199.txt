A Possible Guillain-Barre Syndrome/Transverse Myelitis Overlap Syndrome After Recent COVID-19

Authors: Riyadh Alrubaye, Vijayamala Bondugula, Vidya Baleguli, Rosemary Chofor
Journal: BMJ Case Reports, 2022;15(2):e246967
PMID: 35140089 | PMCID: PMC8830199 | DOI: 10.1136/bcr-2021-246967

CASE REPORT

A 72-year-old patient developed concurrent GBS and transverse myelitis following COVID-19 infection.

PRESENTATION:
- Acute bilateral lower extremity weakness 5 days after COVID-19 diagnosis
- Severe back and leg pain, urinary retention, constipation, falls
- Proximal lower limb weakness 1/5 bilaterally, absent lower extremity reflexes

DIAGNOSTICS:
- CSF: Albumin-cytological dissociation (protein 211 mg/dL, WBC 2/L)
- Thoracic MRI: T2-hyperintense signals T7-T12 (transverse myelitis)
- NCS: Decreased velocities and amplitudes (acute motor axonal neuropathy)

TREATMENT:
- IVIg insufficient alone
- IV corticosteroids 1000mg x 5 days + 5 sessions plasmapheresis: remarkable recovery
- Functional independence within 1 week of discharge

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Third documented GBS/TM overlap post-COVID-19. Severe back pain + motor weakness + areflexia + autonomic dysfunction should trigger investigation.
