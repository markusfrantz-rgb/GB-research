Optic Nerve Demyelination in IgG4 Anti-Neurofascin 155 Antibody-Positive Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination Syndrome

Authors: Alice Verghese, Dhayalan Krishnan, Yuen Kang Chia, Luis Querol, Fu Liong Hiew
Journal: Journal of Central Nervous System Disease, 2021;13:11795735211039913
PMID: 34899003 | PMCID: PMC8655830 | DOI: 10.1177/11795735211039913

CASE REPORTS

Two patients with anti-NF155 CIDP demonstrated bilateral optic nerve demyelination.

CASE 1: 25-year-old male
- Progressive distal weakness, areflexia, sensory ataxia, finger tremors post-yellow fever vaccination
- NCS: Definite CIDP criteria met
- Anti-NF155 IgG4 positive (titre 1:24,300)
- VEP: Prolonged P100 latencies (R 124.0 ms, L 132.0 ms) - bilateral optic nerve demyelination
- Brain/spine MRI: Normal
- IVIg + corticosteroids ineffective over 18 months
- Stabilized on rituximab maintenance

CASE 2: 26-year-old male
- Progressive numbness, weakness, hand tremors post-febrile illness
- NCS: Diffused demyelinating changes
- Anti-NF155 IgG4 positive (titre 1:8,100)
- VEP: Prolonged P100 bilaterally (R 118.2 ms, L 119.4 ms)
- OCT: Moderate-to-severe RNFL thinning
- Brain/spine MRI: Normal
- Oral corticosteroids + azathioprine x 3 years: no improvement
- IVIg: no response
- Stabilized on plasma exchange + rituximab

KEY FINDINGS:
- Subclinical optic nerve demyelination in anti-NF155 CIDP = unrecognized CCPD
- Studies show 47-77% of CCPD patients have abnormal VEP
- Most are asymptomatic for visual complaints
- Anti-NF155 antibodies found in up to 45% of Japanese CCPD cohorts
- IVIg consistently poor response in NF155+ patients
- Rituximab effective
