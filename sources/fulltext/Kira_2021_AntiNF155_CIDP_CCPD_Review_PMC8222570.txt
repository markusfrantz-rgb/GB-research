Anti-Neurofascin 155 Antibody-Positive Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy/Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination: Strategies for Diagnosis and Treatment Based on the Disease Mechanism

Author: Jun-ichi Kira
Journal: Frontiers in Neurology, 2021;12:665136
PMID: 34177770 | PMCID: PMC8222570 | DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2021.665136

COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW

This review examines anti-neurofascin 155 (NF155) antibody-positive chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP) and combined central and peripheral demyelination (CCPD).

DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:
- NF155+ CIDP shows younger age at onset (average around 25 years old)
- Significantly higher frequencies of tremor, drop foot, and gait disturbance
- IgG4 is the predominant antibody subclass
- IgG4 autoantibodies can merely block protein-protein interactions without causing full-blown inflammation

NEUROIMAGING:
- Patients demonstrate remarkable hypertrophy of the cervical and lumbar spinal roots
- Cranial nerve involvement common
- Nerve diameters correlate positively with disease duration

TREATMENT RESPONSE:
- 80% (20/25) of NF155+ CIDP patients had poor response to IVIg
- Alternative approaches using corticosteroids and immunosuppressants needed
- Rituximab shows promise

GENETIC ASSOCIATION:
- All Japanese NF155+ CIDP patients carried specific HLA haplotypes
- Predominantly HLA-DRB1*15:01-DQB1*06:02

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS:
Early recognition of NF155+ CIDP through anti-NF155 antibody testing is essential for appropriate management, as conventional intravenous immunoglobulin therapy proves ineffective in most cases.
