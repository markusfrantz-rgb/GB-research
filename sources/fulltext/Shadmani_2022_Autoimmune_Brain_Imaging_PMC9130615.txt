COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW OF AUTOIMMUNE BRAIN DISEASES AND THEIR IMAGING PATTERNS

1. DEMYELINATING DISEASES

Multiple Sclerosis (MS):
- Dissemination in space (DIS) and time (DIT)
- Periventricular lesions (>=3 mm), juxtacortical/cortical, infratentorial, spinal cord
- Central vein sign (CVS) in >80% of lesions
- Ovoid morphology with peripheral discontinuous enhancement

MS Variants:
- Tumefactive demyelinating lesions (TDL): >2 cm, peripheral discontinuous enhancement, peripheral restriction on DWI
- Balo's concentric sclerosis: Alternating isointense and hyperintense rings on T2
- Marburg variant MS: Fulminant, confluent T2/FLAIR hyperintensities

ADEM (Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis):
- Bilateral but asymmetric, diffuse, large (>1-2 cm) FLAIR/T2-hyperintensities with indistinct borders
- 50% periventricular distribution vs >90% in MS
- Enhancement varies: nodular, diffuse, complete or partial ring
- KEY DIFFERENTIATOR: ADEM lesions larger, less well-defined than MS

AHLE (Acute Hemorrhagic Leukoencephalitis):
- Rare hyperacute variant of ADEM
- Multiple confluent large hemorrhagic lesions
- T2*/SWI essential for diagnosis

NMOSD and MOG-Associated Disease:
- Deep and subcortical white matter lesions in ~50% NMOSD
- "Cloud-like enhancement" with blurred margins
- Diencephalic lesions in bilateral hypothalamus
- LETM involving >=3 vertebral segments

Susac Syndrome:
- T2/FLAIR hyperintense "snowball" lesions in middle layer of corpus callosum
- Sparing of callososeptal interface

2. NON-DEMYELINATING PARENCHYMAL DISEASES

Autoimmune Encephalitis (NMDAr):
- 89% have no neuroimaging abnormalities initially
- When present: T2/FLAIR hyperintensities in medial temporal lobes
- Mild transient cortical enhancement without restricted diffusion

Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH):
- Extensive confluent T2/FLAIR hyperintensities in cerebral and cerebellar white matter
- Diffuse leptomeningeal and perivascular enhancement
- Symmetrical bilateral putamen involvement

Hashimoto Encephalitis:
- MRI normal in ~50% of patients
- Non-specific T2/FLAIR changes in subcortical white matter
- Relative sparing of occipital lobes

SLE (Systemic Lupus Erythematosus):
- 50% NPSLE patients have no brain abnormalities on conventional MRI
- Small subcortical and deep white matter hyperintensities
- Bilateral, symmetrical, paramedian, posterior predominance

3. MENINGEAL-BASED DISEASES

Sarcoidosis: Cranial nerve enhancement with basal leptomeningeal thickening
IgG4-Related Disease: Homogeneous hypertrophic nodular meningeal thickening
GPA: Moderately to markedly hypointense pachymeninges with avid enhancement

4. BRAINSTEM AND POSTERIOR FOSSA DISEASES

CLIPPERS: Punctate <3 mm curvilinear T1 post-gadolinium enhancement centered in pons/cerebellum. T2-FLAIR may be relatively unrevealing.

Neuro-Behcet Disease: T2/FLAIR hyperintense lesions in brainstem, pons, midbrain, diencephalon with cytotoxic edema.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS FRAMEWORK

MS vs Mimics:
- Migraines: Subcortical WM lesions <3 mm, minimal periventricular
- Age-related small-vessel disease: Non-enhancing, spared subcortical U-fibers, symmetrical frontal horn caps
- CADASIL: Confluent WM changes with microhemorrhage and lacunar infarcts

Key features to analyze across conditions:
- Signal change characteristics
- Topography and distribution
- Lesion length and width
- Gadolinium enhancement pattern
- Evolution over time
