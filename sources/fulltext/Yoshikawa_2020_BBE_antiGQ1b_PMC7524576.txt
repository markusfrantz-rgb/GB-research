Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis with or without anti-GQ1b antibody

Authors: Keisuke Yoshikawa, Motoi Kuwahara, Miyuki Morikawa, Susumu Kusunoki
Journal: Neurology Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation
Year: 2020
PMID: 32934014
PMCID: PMC7524576
DOI: 10.1212/NXI.0000000000000889

STUDY OVERVIEW

Compared 73 anti-GQ1b antibody-positive BBE cases with 10 antibody-negative cases, collecting clinical data from Japanese hospitals (2014-2017).

KEY FINDINGS

Distinctive Features of Antibody-Positive Cases:
- Upper respiratory infection and sensory disturbance were more common in seropositive patients
- Lower cerebrospinal fluid cell counts and protein levels
- Fewer abnormal brain MRI findings
- Earlier consciousness disturbance resolution (10 days vs 23 days)

MRI FINDINGS BY ANTIBODY STATUS

Antibody-negative cases: High-intensity T2/FLAIR abnormalities in:
- Midbrain/medulla oblongata
- Corpus callosum
- Periventricular regions
- Temporal poles

Antibody-positive cases: Abnormalities in:
- Deep white matter
- Left thalamus
- Bilateral pyramidal tracts

CRITICAL FINDING: Brain MRI abnormalities occur in only 8% of anti-GQ1b-positive BBE, compared to 50% in antibody-negative cases.

TREATMENT

Among antibody-positive cases, 86% received IVIG either alone or combined with corticosteroids, compared to 50% of seronegative patients.

Favorable treatment outcomes ranged from 75-91% across IVIG-based regimens in seropositive patients.
