Efficacy of Rituximab as Third-Line Therapy in Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination

Authors: Salvatore Savasta, Thomas Foiadelli, Elisa Vegezzi, Andrea Cortese, Alessandro Lozza, Anna Pichiecchio, Diego Franciotta, Enrico Marchioni
Journal: Neurology: Clinical Practice, 2017;7(6):534-537
PMID: 29431175 | PMCID: PMC5800713 | DOI: 10.1212/CPJ.0000000000000388

CASE REPORT

A 14-year-old boy presented with ascending paresthesia, ataxic gait, and areflexia following a flu-like illness. Diagnostic findings included:
- Multiple brain white matter lesions with spinal cord involvement
- Demyelinating neuropathy confirmed by nerve conduction studies
- Negative for anti-MOG, anti-AQP4 antibodies

Initial treatments proved insufficient: methylprednisolone and immunoglobulin therapy produced minimal benefit over 30 months. Natalizumab provided temporary improvement but ultimately worsened peripheral nervous system function.

RITUXIMAB TREATMENT:
Rituximab (500 mg weekly for four weeks) yielded dramatic results:
- Clinical: Disability score improved from EDSS 7 to EDSS 1
- Imaging: Brain and spine lesion burden decreased substantially
- Electrophysiology: Near-normalization of motor conduction velocities
- Durability: 28-month follow-up showed sustained improvement without relapses

The only adverse effect was transient low immunoglobulin levels requiring temporary replacement therapy.

CONCLUSION: Rituximab should be considered as an effective therapy for the treatment of steroid- and IVIg-resistant CCPD.
