Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis
Handbook of Clinical Neurology 2014;123:543-564
Authors: Adil Javed, Omar Khan
PMID: 25015513 | PMCID: PMC7151857 | DOI: 10.1016/B978-0-444-53488-0.00035-3

=== OVERVIEW ===

Comprehensive review of ADEM as an acute multifocal demyelinating disease of the central nervous system that typically follows an infectious illness. While typically monophasic, relapsing variants occasionally occur.

=== ETIOLOGY ===

ADEM results from para- or postinfectious autoimmune complications:
- Various viral agents (measles, influenza, varicella, EBV, CMV)
- Bacterial agents (Mycoplasma, Campylobacter)
- Vaccinations
- No consistent infectious agent found in CNS → immune-mediated, not direct infection

=== PATHOGENESIS ===

T-cell-mediated mechanism:
- Similar to experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE)
- Molecular mimicry between infectious epitopes and myelin antigens
- Triggers autoimmune response against CNS myelin
- Perivenular demyelination with inflammation
- Both white and grey matter can be affected

=== CLINICAL PRESENTATION ===

Symptoms range from mild malaise to fulminant encephalopathy:
- Hemiplegia: 76%
- Ataxia: 18-65%
- Seizures: 13-35%
- Antecedent infection: 70-77% (pediatric cases)
- Onset typically 1-4 weeks after triggering event

=== IMAGING ===

MRI characteristics:
- Multifocal lesions in white and grey matter
- Contrast enhancement: 30-100% of patients
- Follow-up: Lesion resolution without new lesions (monophasic)
- Distinguishing from MS: Larger lesions, less periventricular, cortical involvement

=== TREATMENT ===

First-line: High-dose glucocorticoids
- IV methylprednisolone 20-30 mg/kg/day (max 1 g) x 3-5 days
- Followed by oral taper over 4-6 weeks

Second-line (steroid-refractory):
- Plasma exchange
- IVIg (2 g/kg over 2-5 days)

Third-line (severe/fulminant):
- Cyclophosphamide
- Rituximab (emerging)

=== PROGNOSIS ===

- Full recovery: 70-90% improvement
- Cognitive deficits may persist despite physical recovery
- Pediatric outcomes better than adult
- Relapse rate low in true monophasic ADEM

=== RELEVANCE ===

This comprehensive review confirms:
1. ADEM treatment is primarily steroid-based
2. IVIg is second-line, not competing with steroids
3. When ADEM coexists with GBS, both treatments are needed
4. The immunological mechanism (T-cell mediated, molecular mimicry) can theoretically affect both CNS and PNS
