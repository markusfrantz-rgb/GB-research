An Adult Case of Recurrent Guillain-Barré Syndrome with Anti-galactocerebroside Antibodies
Internal Medicine 2018;57(3):409-413
Authors: Hisashi Takahashi, Tadashi Kimura, Natsuko Yuki, Akira Yoshioka
PMID: 29093388 | PMCID: PMC5827325 | DOI: 10.2169/internalmedicine.8941-17

=== ABSTRACT ===

A 79-year-old woman with previous GBS history (2003) developed somnolence and tetraparesis after pneumonia in 2016. Testing revealed acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (AIDP) and acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM). Anti-galactocerebroside (Gal-C) IgG antibodies were detected, making this extremely rare among recurrent GBS cases. The antibodies likely contributed to both CNS and PNS involvement.

=== CLINICAL HISTORY ===

FIRST EPISODE (2003):
- 66-year-old woman
- Preceding: Upper respiratory tract infection
- Presentation: Progressive weakness
- NCS: Demyelinating features consistent with AIDP
- Treatment: IVIg 400 mg/kg/day for 5 days
- Outcome: Good response, complete recovery with mild residual facial weakness

SECOND EPISODE (2016 — 13 years later):
- 79-year-old (same patient)
- Preceding: Pneumonia
- Presentation: Consciousness disturbance, generalized weakness, sensory loss, meningeal signs

=== DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS (2016 episode) ===

MRI Brain:
- White matter lesions consistent with CNS demyelination
- Features compatible with ADEM

NCS:
- Demyelinating features consistent with AIDP (again)

CSF:
- Elevated protein
- Pleocytosis

Anti-ganglioside antibodies:
- Anti-galactocerebroside (Gal-C) IgG: POSITIVE
- Other anti-ganglioside antibodies: Variable

=== TREATMENT AND OUTCOME ===

Both episodes treated with IVIg (400 mg/kg/day for 5 consecutive days)
- Good response in both episodes
- Demonstrates stereotypic treatment responsiveness

Key observation: IVIg was effective for BOTH the GBS and ADEM components in this case.
No corticosteroids were reported as primary treatment.

=== SIGNIFICANCE ===

1. Anti-Gal-C antibodies as a shared epitope:
   - Galactocerebroside is present in BOTH CNS and PNS myelin
   - Anti-Gal-C antibodies can attack both central and peripheral myelin
   - This explains simultaneous AIDP + ADEM

2. Recurrent GBS is rare (<5% of GBS cases)
   - Recurrence with ADEM is extremely rare
   - Same antibody profile in both episodes suggests a consistent autoimmune mechanism

3. Treatment implications:
   - IVIg effective for combined CNS + PNS demyelination
   - Anti-Gal-C-mediated disease may have specific treatment responsiveness
   - The shared epitope hypothesis has direct therapeutic implications

=== RELEVANCE FOR ADEM-GBS OVERLAP ===

This case demonstrates:
1. A molecular mechanism (anti-Gal-C) explaining combined CNS + PNS disease
2. IVIg can be effective for both components simultaneously
3. Not all ADEM-GBS overlap requires corticosteroids
4. The specific antibody profile may predict treatment response
5. Testing for anti-galactocerebroside and other anti-glycolipid antibodies is important
