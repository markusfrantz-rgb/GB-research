Central nervous system pathology in patients with the Guillain-Barre syndrome

Authors: H Maier, M Schmidbauer, B Pfausler, E Schmutzhard, H Budka
Journal: Brain
Year: 1997
PMID: 9126057
DOI: 10.1093/brain/120.3.451

AUTOPSY STUDY - 13 PATIENTS

This landmark autopsy study examined brain and spinal cord changes alongside peripheral nervous system involvement in 13 GBS patients.

KEY FINDINGS

CNS pathology is frequent in patients with Guillain-Barre syndrome:

- Mononuclear infiltrates in spinal cord: 8/13 cases (62%)
- Mononuclear infiltrates in brainstem structures
- Microglial activation: 11/13 cases (85%)
- Myelin loss in PNS: 12/13 cases (92%)

Distribution of CNS changes:
- Spinal cord
- Medulla oblongata
- Pons
- Subependymal rims along ventricular walls

T lymphocyte composition: Both CD4+ and CD8+ cells in similar proportions.

CRITICAL CONCLUSION

Inflammatory reactions in the central nervous system resemble those in the peripheral nervous system, HOWEVER "primary demyelination is not found in the CNS."

This means:
1. CNS inflammation occurs in GBS but does not cause demyelination
2. CNS MRI abnormalities in GBS may reflect inflammation/microglial activation without demyelination
3. This is fundamentally different from conditions like ADEM or MS where CNS demyelination is the primary pathology
