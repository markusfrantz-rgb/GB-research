Central and peripheral demyelination

Authors: Man Mohan Mehndiratta, Natasha Singh Gulati
Journal: J Neurosci Rural Pract. 2014 Jan;5(1):84-86
DOI: 10.4103/0976-3147.127887
PMID: 24741263 | PMCID: PMC3985372

OVERVIEW

Review of combined central and peripheral demyelination (CCPD) - a rare but
documented phenomenon where both CNS and PNS demyelination occur simultaneously.

KEY FINDINGS

Prevalence and Documentation:
Combined central and peripheral demyelination is rare but various case reports
document both nervous system regions being affected. Documented combinations include:
- Acute combined central and peripheral inflammatory demyelination
- Multiple sclerosis with idiopathic inflammatory-demyelinating neuropathy
- Simultaneous GBS and ADEM in pediatric populations
- Combined CIDP and central pontine myelinolysis in chronic autoimmune hepatitis

Shared Epitope Mechanism:
Molecular mimicry in GBS: epitopes on the surface of peripheral nerves are similar
to epitopes on the surface of infectious agents. This mechanism may extend to
explain central-peripheral overlap when shared antigens exist in both PNS and CNS
myelin.

Proposed Mechanisms:
Immune-mediated processes may affect both CNS and PNS independently or through
a unified mechanism targeting shared myelin epitopes.

Diagnostic Approach:
MRI of brain, spinal roots, brachial plexus, lumbosacral plexus combined with
electrodiagnostic testing and CSF analysis support diagnosis when combined
involvement occurs.

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS

When encountering a patient with both peripheral neuropathy AND brain white matter
lesions, consider:
1. GBS + ADEM overlap
2. CCPD as a distinct entity
3. MOG-antibody associated disease
4. Coincidental conditions

Treatment often requires combined immunotherapy addressing both PNS and CNS
components.
