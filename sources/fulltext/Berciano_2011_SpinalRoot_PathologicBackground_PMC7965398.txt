Thickening and Contrast Enhancement of Spinal Roots on MR Imaging in
Guillain-Barre Syndrome: Thoughts on Pathologic Background

Author: J Berciano
Journal: AJNR Am J Neuroradiol. 2011;32(9):E179
DOI: 10.3174/ajnr.A2754
PMID: 21903912 | PMCID: PMC7965398

OVERVIEW

This editorial/letter discusses the pathologic mechanisms underlying spinal root
thickening and contrast enhancement observed on MRI in GBS patients.

KEY FINDINGS

1. SOURCE OF THICKENING AND ENHANCEMENT

Clinicopathologic studies identified constant but variable radicular lesions
ranging from widespread inflammatory demyelination to scattered inflammatory
infiltrates. These inflammatory changes directly cause the radiologic findings
observed on MRI.

2. CRITICAL ROLE OF THE EPI-PERINEURIUM

The epi-perineurium is THE critical anatomic factor explaining why spinal roots
(and cranial nerves in their subarachnoid trajectory) become visibly thickened,
but peripheral nerves do not.

KEY ANATOMIC PRINCIPLE:
- SPINAL ROOTS and CRANIAL NERVES in the subarachnoid space LACK an
  epi-perineurium
- This absence allows inflammatory swelling to occur freely, producing
  visible thickening on MRI
- PERIPHERAL NERVE TRUNKS possess an epi-perineurium that is relatively
  inelastic, thus NOT allowing any significant increase of their diameters
- This explains why enhancement and thickening are seen in roots/subarachnoid
  cranial nerves but NOT in peripheral nerve trunks

3. SUBARACHNOID ANATOMY AND ENHANCEMENT

Without the constraining epi-perineurium, inflammatory swelling in the
subarachnoid space produces:
- Visible thickening of roots on MRI
- Gadolinium enhancement due to blood-nerve barrier breakdown
- Predominant ventral root involvement (motor roots more affected)

4. MECHANISM OF ENHANCEMENT

Inflammatory demyelination increases endoneurial fluid pressure, potentially
causing endoneurial ischemia and wallerian-like degeneration. This explains
both the enhancement and the prominence of ventral root involvement in GBS.

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS

This anatomic explanation applies equally to:
- Spinal nerve roots in the cauda equina
- Cranial nerves in their cisternal/subarachnoid segments
- Both show enhancement because both lack epi-perineurium in these segments

The same nerve trunk peripheral to the intervertebral foramen (or skull base
foramen) would NOT show thickening due to the constraining epi-perineurium.

This is why GBS enhancement is characteristically seen:
- In the cauda equina (intrathecal spinal roots)
- In cranial nerves at their cisternal segments
- NOT in peripheral nerve trunks
