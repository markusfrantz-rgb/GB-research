CLIPPERS: chronic lymphocytic inflammation with pontine perivascular enhancement responsive to steroids. Review of an increasingly recognized entity within the spectrum of inflammatory central nervous system disorders

Authors: A Dudesek, F Rimmele, S Tesar, S Kolbaske, P S Rommer, R Benecke, U K Zettl
Journal: Clinical and Experimental Immunology
Year: 2014
PMID: 24028073
PMCID: PMC3927899
DOI: 10.1111/cei.12204

OVERVIEW

CLIPPERS is a rare inflammatory CNS disorder first described in 2010 by Pittock et al. Presents with distinctive brainstem involvement and characteristic imaging patterns responsive to glucocorticosteroid therapy.

MRI FINDINGS

Hallmark: Multiple punctate and/or curvilinear gadolinium enhancement "peppering" the pons with potential extension to adjacent hindbrain structures.

Lesion characteristics:
- Focus on pons and peripontine regions
- May extend to cerebellar peduncles, medulla, midbrain, supratentorial structures
- Minimal mass effect and vasogenic edema
- Decreased enhancement with immunosuppressive response
- T2-FLAIR may be relatively unrevealing despite significant clinical disability
- Enhancement faint on T2 but clearly visible on contrast-enhanced scans
- "Salt-and-pepper" appearance

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (Simon et al.)

Clinical: Subacute progressive ataxia, diplopia, brainstem-related manifestations
Radiological: Punctate or nodular enhancing lesions bilaterally in >=2 pontine locations
Steroid responsiveness: Prompt clinical and radiological improvement
Histopathology: White matter perivascular lymphohistiocytic infiltrate (CD3, CD4 predominant)

Absent features: Restricted diffusion, marked T2 hyperintensity, abnormal angiography

CLINICAL CHARACTERISTICS

- Gait ataxia, dysarthria, diplopia, nystagmus, facial sensory changes
- Age 13-86 years, slight male predominance
- Subacute onset over weeks, relapsing-remitting

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES TO EXCLUDE

- Neurosarcoidosis
- Neuro-Behcet's disease
- Multiple sclerosis and ADEM
- Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis
- CNS vasculitis (PACNS)
- Autoimmune encephalitides
- CNS infections, lymphoma, glioma
- Paraneoplastic syndromes

RED FLAGS for alternative diagnosis:
- Lack of steroid response
- Unusual systemic manifestations
- Necrotic lesions, marked mass effects
- Marked pleocytosis (>100/uL)
- Malignant CSF cells

TREATMENT

Initial: High-dose IV methylprednisolone (1g x 5 days) then oral taper
Maintenance: Long-term immunosuppression required (relapse below 10-20 mg/day)
Steroid-sparing agents: Methotrexate, azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, rituximab
