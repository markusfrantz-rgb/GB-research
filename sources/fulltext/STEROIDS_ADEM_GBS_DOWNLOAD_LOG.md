## Summary of Evidence

### Key Conclusions

1. **Pure GBS:** Corticosteroids alone are NOT beneficial (Cochrane review, 6 trials, 587 patients). Oral steroids may actually WORSEN outcomes. IV methylprednisolone + IVIg combination shows modest short-term benefit in one trial (van Koningsveld 2004).

2. **Pure ADEM:** IV methylprednisolone is first-line treatment (10-30 mg/kg/day x 3-5 days). Recovery in 50-80% of patients. Used in >95% of adult ADEM cases.

3. **ADEM-GBS overlap:** COMBINED therapy (IV methylprednisolone + IVIg) is the standard approach. Case reports consistently show good outcomes with dual immunotherapy. Steroids alone are insufficient for the GBS component; IVIg alone is insufficient for the ADEM component.

4. **CCPD (combined central-peripheral demyelination):** Variable response. Two-thirds show unsatisfactory responses to steroids + IVIg. Plasma exchange and rituximab are alternatives.

5. **BBE-GBS overlap:** IVIg is primary treatment. Steroids are debated but used. Combined IVIg + methylprednisolone reported effective in case reports.

6. **Mechanism of steroid harm in GBS:** Steroids suppress macrophage recruitment needed for debris clearance and nerve regeneration (Wang 2015).

### Dosing Protocols from Literature

**For ADEM component:**
- IV methylprednisolone: 1 g/day (adults) or 20-30 mg/kg/day (children) x 3-5 days
- Followed by oral prednisolone taper: 1 mg/kg/day, reducing over 4-6 weeks

**For GBS component:**
- IVIg: 0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days (total 2 g/kg)
- OR Plasma exchange: 5 sessions over 7-14 days

**Combined protocol (ADEM-GBS overlap):**
1. Start IVIg 0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days simultaneously with
2. IV methylprednisolone 1 g/day x 5 days
3. Then oral prednisolone taper x 4-6 weeks
4. Monitor glucose (steroid side effect)
5. If inadequate response: consider PE, rituximab
