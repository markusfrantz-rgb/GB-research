## Abstract (from PubMed)

This review addresses the understanding, detection, and stimulation of consciousness recovery in the intensive care unit. The article covers:

1. **Understanding consciousness**: Frameworks for understanding disorders of consciousness (DOC) in ICU patients
2. **Detecting awareness**: Methods to identify covert awareness in patients who appear unresponsive, including behavioral assessments and advanced neuroimaging/neurophysiology techniques
3. **Stimulating recovery**: Therapeutic approaches to promote consciousness recovery in critically ill patients

---

## Key Points

- Comprehensive review of consciousness assessment and recovery in ICU settings
- Relevant to severe GBS patients who may appear unresponsive but retain awareness
- Covers EEG, fMRI, and other neurophysiological tools for detecting covert consciousness
- Discusses therapeutic interventions to promote arousal and recovery
- Published in Acta Neurochirurgica, a leading neurosurgery journal
- 20-page review (pp. 809-828)

---

## Relevance to GBS

- Severe GBS with cranial nerve paralysis and tetraplegia can mimic disorders of consciousness
- Patients may be fully aware ("locked-in") despite appearing unresponsive
- Tools described in this review (EEG, ERPs, fMRI) can differentiate true consciousness disorders from peripheral paralysis mimicking unresponsiveness
- Critical for appropriate communication strategies and family counseling in ICU
