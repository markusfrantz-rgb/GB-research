The use of intravenous immunoglobulin in the treatment of Hashimoto's encephalopathy: case based review
Sorodoc V, Constantin M, Asaftei A, Lionte C, Ceasovschih A, Sirbu O, Haliga RE, Sorodoc L
Frontiers in Neurology. 2023;14:1243787
PMID: 37745658 | PMCID: PMC10513043 | DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2023.1243787

ABSTRACT
Systematic review of 14 case reports examining IVIG treatment efficacy in Hashimoto's encephalopathy (HE), particularly in steroid-resistant cases.

KEY FINDINGS

IVIG Efficacy:
- IVIG was associated with good outcome, sometimes with dramatic improvements in patient status
- Multiple cases demonstrated rapid clinical improvement within 24-48 hours of IVIG administration
- IVIG therapy proved utility in HE treatment as a well tolerated therapy

Case Presentation:
59-year-old male patient initially deteriorated despite steroid therapy. Following IVIG treatment at 400 mg/kg daily for 5 days, achieved complete recovery with sustained remission during one-year follow-up.

Review of 14 Cases:
- Selected from over 1,365 initial database entries
- IVIG effective as both rescue therapy and alternative first-line in selected cases

TREATMENT CONTEXT
- Corticosteroids remain standard first-line therapy for HE
- IVIG shows promise as second-line therapy or potentially initial treatment
- May be used alone or combined with steroids
- Especially relevant when steroid administration presents clinical risks

CONCLUSIONS
Further research needed to establish optimal IVIG protocols for HE. IVIG represents a viable therapeutic option particularly for steroid-resistant or steroid-contraindicated cases.
