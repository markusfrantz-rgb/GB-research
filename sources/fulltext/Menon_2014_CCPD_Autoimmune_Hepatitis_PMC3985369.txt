Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination
Journal of Neurosciences in Rural Practice 2014;5(1):78-80
Authors: Bindu Menon, Saranjeet Singh Bedi, G Uma Maheshwar Rao
PMID: 24741261 | PMCID: PMC3985369 | DOI: 10.4103/0976-3147.127884

=== ABSTRACT ===

A rare presentation of simultaneous central and peripheral demyelination in a 52-year-old male with chronic autoimmune hepatitis. High-dose steroid therapy proved effective. This represents the first case of central and peripheral demyelination in a patient with CAH.

=== CASE PRESENTATION ===

Patient: 52-year-old male carpenter
History: Chronic autoimmune hepatitis diagnosed 15 months prior, on prednisolone 5 mg daily

Presenting symptoms:
- Progressive quadriparesis (4 months)
- Recent onset dysarthria and drowsiness

Examination:
- Flaccid weakness: Proximal 2-3/5, distal 4/5
- Bilateral eye closure weakness
- Neck flexor involvement
- Preserved sphincter function

=== DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS ===

Laboratory:
- Mild bilirubin elevation (1.7 mg/dl)
- Positive smooth muscle antibodies
- Positive anti-nuclear antibodies
- Elevated IgG (2507)
- Mild thrombocytopenia (100,000/cumm)

MRI Brain:
- Hyperintense signals in basis pontis on T2 sequences
- Consistent with central pontine myelinolysis/demyelination

NCS:
- Prolonged distal latencies
- Decreased motor velocities (median, tibial, peroneal nerves)
- Consistent with CIDP

=== TREATMENT AND OUTCOME ===

High-dose IV methylprednisolone:
- Sensorium improved within 10 HOURS
- Motor power improved within 5 DAYS
- At discharge: Ambulatory with minimal assistance
- 8-month follow-up: Stable, maintained improvement

=== DISCUSSION ===

Key points:
1. Autoimmune hepatitis may trigger both central and peripheral myelin involvement
2. Dysimmune mechanisms (molecular mimicry) explain combined involvement
3. Most previous cases showed subclinical involvement of one neuroaxis
4. This case had clinically significant involvement of BOTH systems

Treatment principle: In this CHRONIC combined demyelination case, steroids were highly effective — contrasting with ACUTE GBS where steroids are harmful.

Distinction: This case involved CIDP (chronic) + central demyelination, NOT acute GBS + ADEM. The chronic nature of the PNS disease may explain steroid responsiveness.

=== RELEVANCE ===

Demonstrates that:
1. Combined CNS-PNS demyelination can occur in various autoimmune contexts
2. Steroids can be effective when the PNS component is CHRONIC (CIDP-like)
3. The distinction between acute (GBS) and chronic (CIDP) PNS involvement matters for treatment
4. Autoimmune comorbidities increase risk of combined demyelination
5. Early diagnosis and prompt treatment prevents progression
