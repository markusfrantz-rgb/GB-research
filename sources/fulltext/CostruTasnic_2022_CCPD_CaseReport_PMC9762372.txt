Combined central and peripheral demyelination: a case report
Journal of Medicine and Life 2022;15(11):1442-1448
Authors: Elena Costru-Tasnic, Elena Manole, Vitalie Lisnic
PMID: 36567843 | PMCID: PMC9762372 | DOI: 10.25122/jml-2022-0010

=== ABSTRACT ===

Combined central and peripheral demyelination (CCPD) is a rare condition involving demyelinating lesions of both the central nervous system (CNS) and peripheral nervous system (PNS). Specific and widely available immunological targets are necessary for diagnosing CCPD cases appropriately.

=== CASE PRESENTATION ===

A 27-year-old previously healthy male:

October 2018: Initial symptoms — brief episodes of hand numbness and leg heaviness.

February 2019: Acute deterioration — weakness progressing to tetraplegia within days, alongside diplopia.

Initial Findings:
- Brain MRI: Demyelinating lesions in corpus callosum and periventricular regions
- Cervical MRI: Myelopathy at C1-C2
- Nerve conduction studies: Demyelinating polyneuropathy
- CSF: Elevated protein (2.04 g/l) without pleocytosis
- Anti-aquaporin 4 IgG: Negative
- Anti-NF155: Not tested initially

=== TREATMENT COURSE ===

FIRST EPISODE:
1. Methylprednisolone pulse therapy (1000 mg/day x 5 days) → INEFFECTIVE
2. Plasma exchange → Limited benefit, partial improvement

SIX MONTHS LATER — RELAPSE:
- Recurrent weakness progressing to tetraplegia
- Brain MRI: New acute lesions in periventricular regions

REVISED TREATMENT:
1. Six plasma exchange sessions
2. Dexamethasone 16 mg/day for 3 days
3. Prednisolone 60 mg/day
4. Maintenance: Azathioprine 2 mg/kg/day

Response began day 5 with progressive motor strength recovery.

TWO-YEAR FOLLOW-UP:
- Relapse-free
- Patient able to walk with unilateral assistance (walking crutch)
- Persistent moderate distal paresis
- Final diagnosis: Atypical CIDP with CNS demyelination (CCPD)

=== DISCUSSION ===

Key points:
1. Misdiagnosis occurs in up to 50% of patients referred with CIDP diagnosis
2. Distinguishing AIDP from CIDP is challenging, especially with rapid onset
3. CCPD presents heterogeneous features with generally worse outcomes than isolated pathologies
4. Available treatments: steroids, IVIg, plasmapheresis, and emerging evidence for Rituximab
5. Two-thirds of CCPD patients show unsatisfactory response to high-dose corticosteroids and IVIg

TREATMENT PRINCIPLES FOR CCPD:
- Combination immunotherapy often required
- Steroids may be ineffective as monotherapy
- Plasma exchange may be more effective than steroids
- Maintenance immunosuppression (azathioprine, rituximab) often needed
- Response to treatment is unpredictable
