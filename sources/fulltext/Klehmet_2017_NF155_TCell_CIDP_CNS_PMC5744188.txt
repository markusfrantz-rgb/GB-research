Neurofascin (NF)155- and NF186-Specific T Cell Response in a Patient Developing a Central Pontocerebellar Demyelination after 10 Years of CIDP

Authors: Juliane Klehmet, Max Staudt, Jan-Markus Diederich, Eberhard Siebert, Edgar Meinl, Lutz Harms, Andreas Meisel
Journal: Frontiers in Neurology, 2017;8:724
PMID: 29312139 | PMCID: PMC5744188 | DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2017.00724

CASE REPORT

A 56-year-old patient with CIDP since 1999 developed brainstem symptoms after 10 years of stable disease on maintenance immunoglobulin therapy.

PRESENTATION: Subacute brainstem syndrome with progressive vertigo, ataxia, and gait disturbance. MRI revealed right-sided pontocerebellar lesion with partial enhancement.

KEY IMMUNOLOGICAL FINDINGS:
- ELISPOT assays detected T cell responses against NF155 and NF186 BEFORE clinical manifestation
- Elevated interferon-gamma responses against these myelin antigens
- NF155 and NF186 antibodies were NEGATIVE (seronegative)
- This suggests T cell-mediated rather than antibody-mediated pathology

TREATMENT: Escalating IVIg to 2 g/kg led to clinical remission with resolution of NF-specific T cell responses on follow-up ELISPOT.

SIGNIFICANCE:
- May represent a novel CIDP subtype with delayed CNS involvement
- T cell responses to neurofascin possible without detectable antibodies
- Demonstrates that CCPD can develop years after isolated PNS disease
- Highlights importance of monitoring for CNS involvement in long-standing CIDP
