Case report: Incidence and prognostic value of brain MRI lesions and elevated
cerebrospinal fluid protein in children with Guillain-Barre syndrome

Authors: Francesco Pizzo, Alessandra Di Nora, Alessia Di Mari, Giuseppe Costanza,
Elisabetta Testa, Marianna Strazzieri, Filippo Greco, Tiziana Timpanaro,
Antonio Basile, Giuseppe Belfiore, Andrea Giugno, Roberta Rocca,
Martino Ruggieri, Agata Fiumara, Piero Pavone

Journal: Frontiers in Neurology. 2022 Oct 21;13:885897
DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2022.885897
PMID: 36341115 | PMCID: PMC9635623

ABSTRACT

Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS) is an acute immune-mediated disorder affecting
peripheral nerves and nerve roots with variable clinical course and outcome. This
study clarifies which clinical features serve prognostic purposes by evaluating
correlations between: (1) brain MRI lesions and disability grade; (2) brain MRI
lesions and elevated CSF protein; and (3) increased CSF protein levels and
disability grade.

Twelve pediatric patients meeting Brighton criteria for GBS were enrolled.
Clinical evaluation at disease onset and 2 weeks using GBS disability score.

Pearson correlation analysis:
- MRI lesions vs disability grade: r = 0.92 (R2 = 0.8573)
- CSF protein vs disability grade: r = 0.85 (R2 = 0.7342)
- MRI lesions vs CSF protein: r = 0.87

Conclusion: MRI and CSF laboratory parameters can be important tools for the
clinician not only for diagnosis but also to evaluate possible worsening.

INTRODUCTION

GBS manifests as acute flaccid paralysis with symmetrical limb weakness,
hyporeflexia, or areflexia developing fully within 4 weeks. Among pediatric
patients at disease zenith: 75% unable to walk unsupported, 30% quadriplegic,
35-50% show cranial nerve involvement, 15-20% have respiratory failure.

In children aged 0-9 years, GBS incidence is 0.62 cases per 100,000 person-years.
Diagnosis relies on family history, assessment, electrodiagnostics, and CSF
analysis revealing classical albumin-cytologic dissociation.

METHODS

Literature Review: Cochrane, EMBASE, MEDLINE. Found no publications evaluating
correlation among clinical severity, imaging, and CSF proteins combined.

GBS Disability Score (0-6 scale):
0 = Asymptomatic
1 = Movement reduction but capable of manual work
2 = Able to walk but unable to work with hands
3 = Needs walking support
4 = Bedridden
5 = Requiring assisted ventilation
6 = Dead

Brain MRI Classification (Yikilmaz et al.):
Type I: No enhancement
Type II: Anterior roots > posterior roots
Type III: Anterior roots = posterior roots
Type IV: Only anterior roots enhance

Study Population: 12 children, ages 1-14 years (mean 6.8), 7 boys/5 girls.
All met Brighton criteria. All received IVIg 1 g/kg daily for 2 days.

RESULTS

Two children required intubation. Three showed cranial nerve involvement.
Children with respiratory disturbances involving cranial nerves had worse
clinical course.

Trigger events: gastrointestinal infections (3), airway infections (8),
vaccination (1). Mean admission time: 4 days post-onset. Sensory disturbance
in 2 patients. Hyporeflexia/areflexia in 10 patients.

Strong correlations found between all three parameters (MRI, CSF protein,
disability), with r-values approaching unity indicating parallel trends.

DISCUSSION

Currently, prognostic scores are based exclusively on clinical parameters.
There is no clinical-prognostic score that takes into consideration radiological
or laboratory parameters.

Involvement of anterior and posterior nerve root is associated with severe
clinical conditions and worse prognosis. MRI should be considered an essential
exam in diagnostic and prognostic evaluation of GBS.

The best prognostic indicator among CSF elements was high level of CSF protein.

MRI should be performed routinely in children with GBS to estimate possible
evolution of clinical condition and establish early need for ventilatory support.

This is the first study using laboratory, imaging, and clinical parameters
to stage GBS together.
