Minimally symptomatic posterior reversible encephalopathy in Guillain-Barre syndrome

Authors: Giovanni Piccolo, Giuseppina Borutti, Diego Franciotta, Carla Arbasino, Carlo Dallocchio, Claudia Camana, Giada Gola, Maria Grazia Egitto
Journal: Functional Neurology
Year: 2015
PMID: 26214029
PMCID: PMC4520675

CASE PRESENTATION

58-year-old woman with progressive distal leg paresthesia and lower limb weakness, alongside autonomic dysfunction. Developed excessive daytime somnolence and non-painful sense of head heaviness - minimal CNS involvement despite significant neuroimaging abnormalities.

MRI FINDINGS

Brain imaging revealed:
- White matter hyperintensities on FLAIR and T2-weighted images bilaterally involving occipital and parietal lobes
- Cortical hyperintensities in occipital lobes related to vasogenic edema on ADC mapping
- DWI did NOT show areas of restricted signal (key distinguishing feature from cytotoxic edema/infarction)
- No contrast enhancement

CLINICAL CONTEXT

Marked autonomic dysregulation:
- Hypertension: 220/110 mmHg
- Tachycardia: 110 bpm
- Paralytic ileus

CSF: Elevated protein (109 mg/dL) with normal cell counts

RESOLUTION

Follow-up MRI demonstrated nearly complete disappearance of white matter hyperintensities within two weeks, confirming PRES's reversible nature.

KEY CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS

1. PRES can occur in GBS patients due to autonomic dysregulation causing severe hypertension
2. White matter hyperintensities in posterior brain regions should raise suspicion for PRES
3. Absence of restricted diffusion and contrast enhancement helps distinguish PRES from other white matter pathology
4. PRES is reversible - follow-up imaging confirms resolution
5. Almost all GBS-associated PRES cases occur in women over 55 years
