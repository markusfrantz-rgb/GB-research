Differences in Thyroid Function and Autoantibodies in the Development of Guillain-Barre Syndrome vs. Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyradiculoneuropathy
Tu Y, Gong X, Zeng G, Zhuo W, Li Z, Yu X
Frontiers in Neurology. 2020;11:1018
PMID: 33013669 | PMCID: PMC7511860 | DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2020.01018

KEY FINDINGS
Thyroid autoantibody levels were markedly elevated in GBS patients compared to CIDP:
- GBS: 70.10% positive for thyroid autoantibodies
- CIDP: 14.10% positive

Diagnostic thresholds:
- Elevated antibody values >133 IU/mL: indicated GBS with 98% specificity
- Elevated antibody values >261.1 IU/mL: indicated GBS with 99% specificity

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS
Thyroid autoantibodies may function as biomarkers for differential diagnosis of GBS vs CIDP. This has potential clinical applications in early disease differentiation.

SIGNIFICANCE FOR HASHIMOTO/GBS OVERLAP
The striking finding that 70% of GBS patients have elevated thyroid autoantibodies supports the hypothesis of shared autoimmune mechanisms between thyroid disease and peripheral neuropathy, particularly GBS. This is highly relevant to understanding the GBS-Hashimoto overlap.
