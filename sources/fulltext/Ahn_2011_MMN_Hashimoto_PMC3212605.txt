Concurrence of Multifocal Motor Neuropathy and Hashimoto's Thyroiditis
Ahn SW, Kim SH, Park BS, Cha JI, Kim SM, Sung JJ, Lee KW
Journal of Clinical Neurology. 2011;7:168-172
PMID: 22087213 | PMCID: PMC3212605 | DOI: 10.3988/jcn.2011.7.3.168

CASE REPORT
37-year-old woman with progressive asymmetric upper-limb weakness over 5 years. Concurrent diagnosis of Hashimoto's thyroiditis via thyroid biopsy showing diffuse lymphocyte infiltration.

CLINICAL FINDINGS
- Progressive distal upper-limb weakness, asymmetric involvement
- No sensory symptoms
- Preserved cranial nerve function
- Severe motor weakness with muscle atrophy in hand musculature
- Normal lower extremity strength

ELECTROPHYSIOLOGY
Motor nerve conduction: conduction blocks in median, ulnar, and radial nerves without sensory involvement (hallmark of MMN).

LABORATORY
- Negative for antiganglioside antibodies (despite clear MMN)
- Antithyroglobulin: 794 U/mL (elevated)
- Antimicrosomal antibodies: 26,030 U/mL (markedly elevated)

TREATMENT
IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days: significant clinical improvement with reduced hand deformity and improved conduction blocks.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL LINK
Gangliosides are sialic acids containing glycosphingolipids that are particularly abundant in neural membranes and also present in thyroid tissue. This suggests a potential immunological connection between MMN and Hashimoto's thyroiditis despite negative antiganglioside antibodies.

SIGNIFICANCE
Demonstrates possible link and pathophysiological mechanism between MMN (a peripheral demyelinating neuropathy) and Hashimoto's thyroiditis. Supports shared autoimmune susceptibility between thyroid and neurological autoimmune conditions.
