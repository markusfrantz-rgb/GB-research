Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis: Treatment guidelines
Annals of Indian Academy of Neurology 2011;14(Suppl1):S60-S64
Authors: M Alexander, J M K Murthy
PMID: 21847331 | PMCID: PMC3152158 | DOI: 10.4103/0972-2327.83095

=== ABSTRACT ===

Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM) is a monophasic, postinfectious or postvaccineal acute inflammatory demyelinating disorder of central nervous system. Incidence is approximately 0.4-0.8 per 100,000 annually, predominantly affecting children and young adults.

=== CLINICAL PRESENTATION ===

Neurologic deficits typically emerge 3-6 weeks following an antecedent event. Manifestations include:
- Altered mental status
- Pyramidal dysfunction
- Cerebellar ataxia
- Brainstem syndromes
- Optic neuritis
- Myelitis
- Seizures
- Encephalitic presentation (more common in children under 3)

=== DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA ===

CSF analysis:
- Abnormalities in approximately two-thirds of patients
- Moderate pleocytosis with raised proteins
- Oligoclonal bands typically ABSENT (distinguishing from MS)

MRI (modality of choice):
- Features differentiating ADEM from MS include:
  * Solitary lesion
  * Unilateral large lesion
  * Cortical lesions
  * Subcortical grey matter involvement

=== TREATMENT PROTOCOLS ===

FIRST-LINE THERAPY: Intravenous methylprednisolone
- Dose: 10-30 mg/kg/day (maximum 1 g/day)
- Duration: 3-5 days
- Followed by: Oral corticosteroid taper over 4-6 weeks
- Outcome: Full recovery in 50%-80% of patients

MECHANISM OF ACTION:
Corticosteroids in ADEM work through:
1. Reducing inflammation
2. Stabilizing the blood-brain barrier
3. Decreasing cerebral edema
4. Suppressing the autoimmune attack on myelin

SECOND-LINE THERAPY (if corticosteroids fail):

Option A: Plasma exchange (PE)
- 4-6 sessions
- Moderate to marked and sustained improvement demonstrated
- Particularly useful in fulminant or steroid-refractory cases

Option B: Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg)
- Dose: 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days (total 2 g/kg)
- OR: 1 g/kg/day for 2 days
- Improvement evident within 2-3 days
- Useful when corticosteroids contraindicated

SEVERE/FULMINANT CASES:
- Cyclophosphamide: Has shown success in fulminant presentations
- Hypothermia: Adjunctive in refractory cerebral edema
- Decompressive hemicraniectomy: Life-saving for massive cerebral edema

SUPPORTIVE CARE:
- Airway protection
- Mechanical ventilation when necessary
- Antiseizure medications
- Electrolyte correction
- Prophylactic anticoagulation for high-risk patients
- Physical rehabilitation

=== ORAL STEROID TAPER ===

Critical point: Oral corticosteroid tapering must continue for 4-6 weeks after IV pulse therapy to:
1. Prevent relapse (relapse rate higher with abrupt discontinuation)
2. Allow gradual immune system normalization

Typical taper:
- Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day initially
- Gradual reduction over 4-6 weeks

=== PROGNOSIS ===

- Full recovery: 50-75% of patients, within 1-6 months
- Postmeasles cases: Higher mortality
- Poor prognostic indicators:
  * Hyperacute onset
  * Severe neurologic deficits
  * Steroid unresponsiveness
  * Lack of treatment response within first week

=== RELAPSE ===

- Monophasic ADEM: By definition, single episode
- Multiphasic ADEM: Rare; occurs >3 months after first event
- If relapse occurs: Consider MS or NMOSD as alternative diagnosis

=== CLINICAL RELEVANCE FOR ADEM-GBS OVERLAP ===

When ADEM co-occurs with GBS:
1. IV methylprednisolone is ESSENTIAL for the ADEM component
2. IVIg is ESSENTIAL for the GBS component
3. Combined therapy (IVIg + IV MP) addresses both simultaneously
4. Corticosteroid taper should continue even after IVIg course
5. Monitor for both CNS and PNS improvement independently
