Hashimoto Encephalopathy -- Still More Questions than Answers
Waliszewska-Prosol M, Ejma M
Cells. 2022;11(18):2873
PMID: 36139446 | PMCID: PMC9496753 | DOI: 10.3390/cells11182873

OVERVIEW
Comprehensive review examining the controversies surrounding Hashimoto encephalopathy (HE), questioning whether it represents a genuine disease entity.

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- Prevalence: ~2.1 per 100,000 adults
- Female predominance 4:1
- Typical emergence in 4th-6th decades of life

CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS
Two presentation types:
- Type 1: Stroke-like episodes with cognitive dysfunction (relapsing-remitting)
- Type 2: Progressive with seizures, psychiatric symptoms, dementia

Cognitive dysfunction described as initial symptom in >80% of patients.

CONTROVERSIES
Skeptics argue:
- Anti-TPO antibodies appear in up to 13% of general population
- Many reported cases may represent misdiagnosis
- No correlation between antibody titers and disease severity
- Immunosuppressive treatment carries substantial side effects
- A 2020 Mayo Clinic study found 73% of initially diagnosed HE patients received alternative diagnoses after comprehensive evaluation

Core question: Does HE constitute a genuine disease entity or merely coincidental finding of autoimmune thyroiditis in patients with encephalopathy from other causes?

PATHOGENESIS
Proposed mechanisms:
- Autoimmune CNS vasculitis
- Cross-reactive antigens between thyroid and brain tissue
- Demyelination
- Potential TRH toxicity
- IgG4-related inflammation in aggressive cases

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (Graus et al.)
- Subacute cognitive or seizure onset
- Euthyroid or mild hypothyroidism
- Elevated TPO antibodies (>200 IU/mL)
- Absent neuronal antibodies
- Exclusion of alternative causes

TREATMENT
- First-line: Corticosteroids (methylprednisolone IV then oral prednisone)
- Only ~31% fully respond to steroids (contradicting "steroid-responsive" designation)
- Alternative: azathioprine, rituximab, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil
- IVIG and plasma exchange for resistant cases

CONCLUSIONS
Despite decades of observation, fundamental questions about HE etiology and optimal management remain unresolved.
