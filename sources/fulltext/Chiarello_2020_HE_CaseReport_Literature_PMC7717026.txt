Hashimoto encephalopathy: a case report and a short revision of current literature
Chiarello P, Talarico V, Nicoletti A, Rodio B, Arcuri PP, Bosco D, Gigliotti F, Galati MC, Raiola G
Acta Biomed (Acta Bio Medica: Atenei Parmensis). 2020;91(3):e2020087
PMID: 32921783 | PMCID: PMC7717026 | DOI: 10.23750/abm.v91i3.10157

OVERVIEW
HE is a rare but controversial entity encompassing a variety of neuropsychological presentations in the setting of autoimmune thyroid disease. Predominantly affects females (4:1 ratio). Pediatric cases are rare (~60 documented cases, median onset age 14 years).

CASE PRESENTATION
12-year-old girl with acute neurological symptoms: headache, vomiting, tremors, speech difficulties, disorientation, lower limb weakness. Parents noted mood worsening with alternating phases of depression and irritability weeks before.

DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS
- Standard infectious workups negative
- Markedly elevated anti-TPO levels
- Brain MRI: hyperintense spots in white matter of frontal-parietal lobe, bilateral

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (Graus et al., 2016)
1. Encephalopathy with seizures, myoclonus, hallucinations, or stroke-like episodes
2. Thyroid disease (typically subclinical or mild hypothyroidism)
3. Normal or non-specific brain MRI abnormalities
4. Elevated serum TPO or TG antibodies
5. Absence of other neuronal antibodies
6. Reasonable exclusion of alternative causes

TREATMENT
IV methylprednisolone (1g daily x 3 days) then oral prednisone. Rapid improvement: tremors and speech difficulties resolved within days. 5-year follow-up: asymptomatic.

PEDIATRIC CLINICAL PRESENTATIONS
- Seizures: 69% of pediatric cases
- Altered consciousness: 60%
- Psychosis: 37%
- Behavioral changes: 33%

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Three mechanisms proposed:
1. Vasculitic: brain microvasculature inflammation/edema
2. Autoantibody: antithyroid antibodies cross-react with brain antigens
3. Hormonal: excessive TRH from hypothyroidism

TREATMENT APPROACHES
First-line: corticosteroids (>90% respond)
Resistant cases: IVIG, plasma exchange, methotrexate, azathioprine
HE designated as category II indication for plasma exchange with 2C level of recommendation.

ASSOCIATED CONDITIONS
HE coexists with other autoimmune disorders in ~1/3 of cases: SLE, Sjogren's, myasthenia gravis, autoimmune inner ear disease.
