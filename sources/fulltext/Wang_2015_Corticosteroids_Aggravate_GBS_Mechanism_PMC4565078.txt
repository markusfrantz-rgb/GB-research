Action mechanism of corticosteroids to aggravate Guillain-Barré syndrome
Scientific Reports 2015;5:13931
Authors: Yu-Zhong Wang, Hui Lv, Qi-Guang Shi, Xu-Tao Fan, Lei Li, Anna Hiu Yi Wong, Yan-Lei Hao, Chuan-Ping Si, Cui-Lan Li, Nobuhiro Yuki
PMID: 26355080 | PMCID: PMC4565078 | DOI: 10.1038/srep13931

=== ABSTRACT ===

Corticosteroids are widely used to treat autoimmune diseases, but they have been shown NOT to benefit GBS patients. This study used a rabbit axonal GBS model to investigate WHY corticosteroids fail in GBS and may actually worsen outcomes.

=== KEY FINDINGS ===

EXPERIMENT 1: Effect on macrophage infiltration
- Methylprednisolone significantly REDUCED macrophage infiltration in spinal ventral roots
- Complement deposition was unchanged
- Sodium channel disruption was unchanged
- Conclusion: Steroids suppress macrophage recruitment but do not affect the initial autoimmune attack

EXPERIMENT 2: Effect on clinical outcomes
- 42% of methylprednisolone-treated rabbits DIED within 30 days (vs lower mortality in controls)
- Clinical scores were significantly WORSE in steroid-treated animals
- Higher frequency of AXONAL DEGENERATION in steroid-treated group
- Survival rate was LOWER with steroids

=== PROPOSED MECHANISM ===

Why corticosteroids HARM GBS patients:

1. In GBS, the immune attack damages peripheral nerve myelin/axons
2. After the initial damage, MACROPHAGES are recruited to clean up debris
3. Macrophages act as essential "SCAVENGERS" — removing damaged myelin and axon fragments
4. This debris clearance is CRITICAL for nerve regeneration
5. Corticosteroids SUPPRESS macrophage migration and recruitment
6. Without macrophage scavenging, debris accumulates
7. Accumulated debris BLOCKS nerve regeneration
8. Result: Delayed or prevented recovery

KEY QUOTE: "Corticosteroids may reduce the scavengers that play a crucial role for nerve regeneration, thus delay the recovery."

=== CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS ===

For PURE GBS:
- Corticosteroids are CONTRAINDICATED as monotherapy
- They suppress the very cells needed for recovery (macrophages)
- This explains the Cochrane review finding that oral steroids WORSEN outcomes

For ADEM-GBS OVERLAP:
- The situation is MORE COMPLEX
- ADEM requires corticosteroids (for CNS inflammation)
- GBS may be worsened by corticosteroids (blocked nerve regeneration)
- COMBINED therapy (steroids for ADEM + IVIg for GBS) is the pragmatic approach
- The ADEM component typically drives the steroid decision when both conditions coexist
- Monitor PNS recovery carefully when steroids are used in overlap syndromes

=== DISTINCTION FROM CIDP ===

Important: This mechanism applies to ACUTE GBS, not CIDP.
In CIDP:
- The disease is chronic with ongoing inflammation
- Corticosteroids reduce this ongoing inflammation → beneficial
- Macrophage suppression is less problematic because the inflammatory cycle is different

If a patient with "GBS" responds to steroids, consider:
- May actually be first attack of CIDP
- May have ADEM component driving the improvement
- May have BBE component responding to steroids
