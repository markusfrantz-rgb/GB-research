Sphingolipid Abnormalities in Encephalomyeloradiculoneuropathy (EMRN) are Associated with an Anti-Neutral Glycolipid Antibody

Authors: Tatsuro Mutoh, Akihiro Ueda, Yoshiki Niimi
Journal: FEBS Open Bio, 2023;13(9):1580-1586
PMID: 36807737 | PMCID: PMC10476566 | DOI: 10.1002/2211-5463.13578

REVIEW

EMRN is a newly identified neuroinflammatory disorder affecting brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves.

KEY FINDINGS:
- Anti-neutral glycolipid autoantibodies (especially anti-lactosylceramide/LacCer) detected in sera and CSF at acute phase
- Antibodies disappear after successful immunomodulatory therapy
- Significant upregulation of serum C5a levels and CSF ceramide accumulation
- Suggests abnormal innate immunity activation

SPHINGOLIPID CATEGORIES:
- Ceramide (Cer): Accumulation induces neuronal cell death via oxidative stress
- Glucosylceramide (GlcCer): Associated with lysosomal storage diseases
- Galactosylceramide (GalCer): Produced in oligodendrocytes, essential for myelin
- Lactosylceramide (LacCer): Regulates proinflammatory mediators in astrocytes, influences BBB

MOLECULAR MECHANISMS:
- Anti-glycolipid autoantibodies disrupt lipid rafts (specialized membrane microdomains)
- Anti-GM1 antibodies inhibit neutral sphingomyelinase 2 activity
- Complement activation plays essential role

THERAPEUTIC IMPLICATIONS:
- Anticomplement therapy proposed for fulminant EMRN
- Anticeramide approaches under investigation
- Disease-specific biomarker: anti-lactosylceramide antibody
