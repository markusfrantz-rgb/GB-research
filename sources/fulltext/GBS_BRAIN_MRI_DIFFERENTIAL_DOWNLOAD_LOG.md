## Key Clinical Synthesis for Madeleine's Case

### Differential Diagnosis of Small White Matter Lesions on Brain MRI in Severe GBS

Based on this systematic review, the following conditions should be considered:

#### 1. PRES (Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome) — **HIGH RELEVANCE**
- **Mechanism:** Autonomic dysregulation in GBS causes hypertensive surges → vasogenic edema
- **MRI pattern:** T2/FLAIR hyperintensities in posterior brain (occipital > parietal), NO restricted diffusion, NO enhancement
- **Risk factors:** Woman >55, autonomic dysfunction (Madeleine has both)
- **Key feature:** REVERSIBLE — follow-up MRI shows resolution
- **Action:** Check BP trends, repeat MRI in 2 weeks

#### 2. Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (Central Pontine Myelinolysis) — **MODERATE RELEVANCE**
- **Mechanism:** Rapid sodium correction (SIADH affects ~50% of GBS patients)
- **MRI pattern:** T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in pons ("trident sign") and/or extrapontine locations (basal ganglia, thalamus, cerebral white matter)
- **Timing:** 1-14 days after electrolyte correction
- **Action:** Review sodium correction history carefully

#### 3. Bickerstaff Brainstem Encephalitis (BBE) — **MODERATE RELEVANCE**
- **Mechanism:** Anti-GQ1b mediated CNS inflammation
- **MRI pattern:** T2/FLAIR hyperintensities in brainstem, thalamus, cerebellum, cerebral white matter
- **Frequency:** Only 30% of BBE cases have abnormal MRI; only 8% of anti-GQ1b-positive BBE
- **Key features to check:** Ophthalmoplegia? Hyperreflexia? Altered consciousness?
- **Action:** Test anti-GQ1b antibodies if not already done

#### 4. ADEM (Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis) — **LOW-MODERATE RELEVANCE**
- **Mechanism:** Post-infectious/immune-mediated CNS demyelination
- **MRI pattern:** Large (>1-2 cm), bilateral asymmetric, poorly marginated T2/FLAIR lesions involving white and grey matter
- **Differentiator from GBS:** ADEM lesions typically LARGE and POORLY DEFINED; small punctate lesions less typical
- **Action:** If lesions are large/multifocal, consider ADEM-GBS overlap

#### 5. Incidental/Age-Related White Matter Changes — **MODERATE RELEVANCE**
- **Mechanism:** Small vessel disease, hypertension
- **MRI pattern:** Small, non-enhancing T2/FLAIR hyperintensities; periventricular caps; subcortical dots
- **Key differentiator:** Were these present BEFORE GBS onset? Previous MRI comparison critical

#### 6. CNS Inflammation Intrinsic to Severe GBS — **HIGH RELEVANCE**
- **Evidence (Maier 1997):** Autopsy shows microglial activation in 85% and mononuclear infiltrates in 62% of GBS patients, BUT **no primary CNS demyelination**
- **Implication:** Small T2/FLAIR hyperintensities in GBS may reflect inflammation WITHOUT demyelination
- **This means:** Steroids would NOT be indicated for this mechanism alone

#### 7. IVIG-Related Complications
- **Aseptic meningitis:** 0.5-1% of IVIG-treated patients
- **Thrombotic events:** Variable incidence (1-16%), could cause small infarcts
- **Action:** Review timing of white matter findings relative to IVIG administration

#### 8. SIADH/Metabolic — **HIGH RELEVANCE IN ICU**
- SIADH affects ~50% of GBS patients
- Hyponatremia can cause reversible brain edema
- Hepatic encephalopathy, vitamin deficiencies also possible in prolonged ICU stay

### Decision Framework: Should Steroids Be Given?

**Steroids INDICATED if:**
- ADEM-GBS overlap (large, enhancing, multifocal lesions)
- BBE confirmed (ophthalmoplegia + consciousness changes + anti-GQ1b)
- CLIPPERS (punctate pontine enhancement)

**Steroids NOT indicated for:**
- PRES (treat hypertension instead)
- Osmotic demyelination (supportive care only)
- Intrinsic GBS CNS inflammation (no demyelination to treat)
- Small vessel disease / incidental findings
- IVIG-related aseptic meningitis (self-limiting)

**Key discriminating tests:**
1. Anti-GQ1b antibodies (for BBE/MFS spectrum)
2. Serum sodium trend review (for osmotic demyelination)
3. BP monitoring trends (for PRES)
4. MRI characteristics: enhancement? restricted diffusion? location? size?
5. Repeat MRI in 2 weeks (PRES resolves; ADEM/MS persists or evolves)
