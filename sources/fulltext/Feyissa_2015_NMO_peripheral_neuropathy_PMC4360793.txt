Neuromyelitis Optica Phenotype Associated with Therapy-Responsive Acute Peripheral Neuropathy

Authors: Anteneh M Feyissa, Elena Shanina, Rahul Shah, Robert G Smith
Journal: Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation
Year: 2015; Volume: 2(3): e83
PMID: 25798448 | PMCID: PMC4360793 | DOI: 10.1212/NXI.0000000000000083

CASE SUMMARY

A 51-year-old Hispanic woman with history of Churg-Strauss syndrome, mesangial glomerulonephritis, end-stage renal disease (post-transplant), and treated CMV infection presented with sudden lower extremity numbness. Within 24 hours, symptoms progressed to complete paraplegia, ascending paresis to upper extremities, complete blindness, and high thoracic sensory level.

DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS

Imaging: Bilateral prechiasmatic optic nerve enhancement and T2 hyperintensities involving central spinal cord gray matter with edema.

CSF: Neutrophilic pleocytosis (182/mm3), protein 142 mg/dL, no oligoclonal bands. All cultures negative.

Serology: Negative for AQP4, antineuronal antibodies, ANCA, ganglioside antibodies (GM1, GM2, GD1a, GD1b, GQ1b).

Electrophysiology: Severe reduction in compound motor action potential amplitudes with reduced sensory responses. EMG: severe denervation.

DIAGNOSIS

Combined:
- Clinically definite NMO phenotype (seronegative)
- Acute axonal polyradiculoneuropathy

TREATMENT

Methylprednisolone followed by 5 cycles plasma exchange, then IV rituximab (4 weekly cycles) with significant improvement.

OUTCOMES
- 7-14 months: Normal upper extremity strength, ambulation with walker
- Right eye: complete recovery. Left eye: improved to 20/400
- 7-month NCS/EMG: marked improvement; 14-month studies normal

DISCUSSION

Clinicians should maintain clinical suspicion for concomitant peripheral nerve involvement with NMOSD. Proposed mechanisms include: MOG or other complement-activating antibodies, severe inflammation causing ischemia, genetic predisposition to multiple autoimmune conditions, and immune reconstitution effects.
