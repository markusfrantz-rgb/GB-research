Hashimoto's encephalopathy: an endocrinological point of view
Croce L, Dal Molin M, Teliti M, Rotondi M
Frontiers in Endocrinology. 2024;15:1367817
PMID: 38665262 | PMCID: PMC11044667 | DOI: 10.3389/fendo.2024.1367817

OVERVIEW
HE from an endocrine specialist's perspective. Prevalence ~2.1 per 100,000. Strong female predominance (4-5x). Typical onset ages 45-55.

CLINICAL PHENOTYPES
1. Vasculitic type: stroke-mimicking episodes, relapsing-remitting course
2. Diffuse progressive type: insidious onset with progressive cognitive decline
Up to 30% of HE patients present as euthyroid.

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (Graus revised)
- Subacute cognitive or seizure onset
- Euthyroid status or mild hypothyroidism
- Elevated TPO antibodies (>200 IU/mL)
- Absent neuronal antibodies
- Exclusion of alternative causes

KEY FINDING: Mayo Clinic Study
2020 study revealed 73% of initially diagnosed HE patients received alternative diagnoses after comprehensive evaluation. This highlights low specificity of thyroid antibodies -- over 10% of healthy individuals carry detectable anti-thyroid antibodies.

PATHOGENIC MECHANISMS (proposed)
- Autoimmune CNS vasculitis
- Cross-reactive antigens between thyroid and brain tissue
- Demyelination
- Potential TRH toxicity
- IgG4-related inflammation

TREATMENT
First-line: high-dose IV methylprednisolone (500-1000 mg weekly) then oral prednisone (1-2 mg/kg/day) with gradual taper.
Only 31% of HE patients fully respond to steroids (contradicting "steroid-responsive" designation).
Alternatives: azathioprine, rituximab, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil.
Emerging therapies target B cells, complement cascades, IL-6 pathways.

CRITICAL CONCLUSIONS
1. HE is a neurological disorder, not primarily endocrinological
2. Autoimmune etiology supported by female predominance and antibody presence
3. Thyroid antibody titers show NO correlation with symptom severity -- antibodies are "an epiphenomenon rather than a causative factor"
4. Steroid-resistance in some patients indicates HE encompasses broader pathological spectrum
5. Seronegative autoimmune encephalitis possibilities remain inadequately explored
