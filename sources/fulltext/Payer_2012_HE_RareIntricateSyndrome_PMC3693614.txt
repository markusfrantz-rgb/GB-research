Hashimoto Encephalopathy: A Rare Intricate Syndrome
Payer J, Petrovic T, Lisy L, Langer P
International Journal of Endocrinology and Metabolism. 2012;10(2):506-514
PMID: 23843812 | PMCID: PMC3693614 | DOI: 10.5812/ijem.4174

OVERVIEW
HE described as a rare but very serious illness characterized by neurological dysfunction with elevated thyroid antibodies and responsiveness to glucocorticoid therapy. Approximately 70-80% of patients are female.

KEY FEATURES
Clinical manifestations: unexplained or epilepsy-like seizures resistant to anti-convulsive treatment, confusion, headaches, hallucinations, stroke-like episodes, coma, and cognitive impairment.

Diagnostic markers: High thyroid antibody levels, especially against thyroperoxidase (TPOab) appear in virtually all documented cases. CSF elevated protein in ~78% of cases. Abnormal EEG findings in 98%.

PATHOGENESIS
Brain vasculitis and autoimmunity directed against common brain-thyroid antigens represent the most likely etiologic pathway. Additional research has identified anti-neuronal antibodies and amino-terminal alpha-enolase (NAE) antibodies as contributing factors.

TREATMENT
Corticosteroid treatment usually provides dramatic recovery, establishing HE's autoimmune foundation. Some cases respond to IVIG when steroids prove insufficient.

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Diagnostic challenges due to widely varying symptoms. Early recognition of thyroid antibodies in unexplained encephalopathy is crucial.
