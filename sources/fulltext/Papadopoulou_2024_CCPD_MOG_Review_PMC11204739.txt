Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination (CCPD) Associated with MOG Antibodies: Report of Four New Cases and Narrative Review of the Literature

Authors: Marianna Papadopoulou, Dimitrios Tzanetakos, Christos Moschovos, et al.
Journal: Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2024;13(12):3604
PMID: 38930142 | PMCID: PMC11204739 | DOI: 10.3390/jcm13123604

STUDY OVERVIEW

Four new cases of MOG-IgG seropositive patients with concurrent demyelinating polyneuropathy, plus review of 22 previously published cases since 2018.

KEY FINDINGS:
- Patients aged 7-74 years
- CNS and PNS involvement was concurrent in 10 cases (45%), sequential in others
- All had confirmed demyelinating polyneuropathy on electrodiagnostic criteria
- CSF commonly showed protein elevation without oligoclonal bands
- MRI frequently revealed cauda equina involvement with gadolinium enhancement

TREATMENT:
- Corticosteroids used in all cases
- Sometimes followed by IVIG, plasma exchange, other immunosuppressive therapies
- Response varied from complete resolution to partial improvement with relapses

PATHOGENIC HYPOTHESES:
MOG is exclusively expressed in CNS tissue. Three hypotheses for PNS involvement:
1. Very low, undetectable MOG quantities in peripheral nerve cells
2. Molecular mimicry triggering immune responses against unidentified PNS antigens
3. Antibody leakage from nerve roots near the CNS-PNS transition zone

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:
Anti-MOG-associated CCPD should be considered in the differential diagnosis of demyelinating polyneuropathy, and CNS involvement should be persistently sought.
