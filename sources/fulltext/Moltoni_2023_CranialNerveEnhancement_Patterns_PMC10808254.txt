Extra-axial cranial nerve enhancement: a pattern-based approach

OVERVIEW

This review identifies four distinct cranial nerve enhancement patterns on
gadolinium-enhanced MRI and their differential diagnoses.

FOUR ENHANCEMENT PATTERNS

1. UNILATERAL LINEAR
   - Associated with: demyelinating conditions, viral neuritis
   - Examples: Bell's palsy, herpes zoster, MS

2. BILATERAL LINEAR
   - Associated with: multiple sclerosis, some inflammatory disorders
   - Examples: MS, neurosarcoidosis

3. UNILATERAL THICKENED
   - Associated with: perineural tumor spread, localized infection
   - Examples: schwannoma, perineural invasion of head/neck cancers

4. BILATERAL THICKENED
   - Associated with: neoplastic carcinomatosis, disseminated inflammatory disease
   - Examples: leptomeningeal carcinomatosis, GBS, Lyme, TB

GUILLAIN-BARRE SYNDROME PATTERN

GBS can show cranial nerve involvement with a BILATERAL THICKENED pattern
resulting from an immuno-inflammatory process caused by complement activation.

This pattern is shared with:
- Lyme disease
- Tuberculosis
- Leptomeningeal carcinomatosis

Clinical history, laboratory findings, and imaging characteristics of associated
structures are essential for differentiation.

MECHANISM IN GBS

The enhancement in GBS reflects breakdown of the blood-nerve barrier secondary
to immune-mediated inflammation. Complement activation leads to inflammation and
edema of the nerve, producing the thickened bilateral enhancement pattern.

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Abnormal cranial nerve enhancement on MRI may sometimes be the first or only
indication of an underlying disease process. Pattern recognition helps narrow
differential diagnosis.
