COVID-19-Associated Miller Fisher Syndrome: MRI Findings

Authors: JE Lantos, SB Strauss, E Lin
Journal: AJNR Am J Neuroradiol. 2020 Jul;41(7):1184-1186
DOI: 10.3174/ajnr.A6609
PMID: 32467190 | PMCID: PMC7357656

CASE REPORT

Patient with COVID-19-associated Miller Fisher syndrome showing
striking MRI findings of cranial nerve III involvement.

MRI FINDINGS

Affected cranial nerve: CN III (oculomotor nerve)

Enhancement pattern:
- Striking enlargement of the left CN III
- Prominent enhancement with gadolinium
- T2 hyperintense signal of the left CN III
- Enhancement extended from cavernous sinus through the orbit
- Marked enhancement on postcontrast imaging

Other findings:
- No other cranial nerves showed abnormal signal or enhancement
- Brain MRI otherwise normal
- No cerebellar lesions
- No meningitis
- No encephalitis
- No demyelination
- No infarction

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

1. Isolated CN III enhancement can be the ONLY MRI finding in MFS/GBS variants
2. Enhancement involves the cisternal and cavernous segments
3. Brain parenchyma typically normal in MFS (unlike BBE)
4. COVID-19 is a recognized trigger for MFS/GBS

DIFFERENTIATION

This case illustrates key MRI distinctions:
- MFS: Cranial nerve enhancement WITHOUT brain parenchymal lesions
- BBE: Brainstem parenchymal lesions (midbrain, pons)
- GBS: Typically spinal root enhancement, cranial nerve enhancement possible
- ADEM: Multifocal white matter lesions in brain + possible nerve involvement
