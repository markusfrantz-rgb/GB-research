Guillain-Barre Syndrome and Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome following Spinal Surgery

Authors: Yui Sanpei, Akira Hanazono, Sachiko Kamada, Masashiro Sugawara
Journal: Case Reports in Neurology
Year: 2019
DOI: 10.1159/000502570
PMID: 31607895
PMCID: PMC6787417

=== FULL TEXT ===

ABSTRACT

The case describes a female patient in her 70s who developed ascending paralysis and brain lesions after spinal surgery. Neurological findings included "demyelinating polyneuropathy" with "bilateral subcortical altered signal intensities involving U-fibers of the frontal-parieto-occipital lobes." The patient was diagnosed with both Guillain-Barre syndrome and PRES, with brain lesions resolving after blood pressure management and intravenous immunoglobulin treatment.

KEY CLINICAL POINTS

1. Surgery can trigger GBS (post-surgical GBS)
2. GBS-related autonomic dysfunction may precipitate PRES through hypertension
3. Importance of "treating hypertension before using IVIG when PRES is suspected"
4. Brain imaging should be considered in post-surgical patients with neurological symptoms
5. Women over 55 years appear to be at higher risk for this complication
6. MRI showed bilateral subcortical signal changes involving U-fibers of frontal-parieto-occipital lobes
7. Brain lesions resolved completely after blood pressure control and IVIg treatment

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

This case adds to the growing body of evidence that:
- Post-surgical GBS is a recognized entity
- PRES is a recognized complication of GBS regardless of the trigger
- Blood pressure management should PRECEDE immunotherapy when PRES is present
- The combination of GBS + PRES follows the typical demographic pattern (women > 55 years)
