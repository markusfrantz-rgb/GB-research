Encephalopathy of Autoimmune Origin: Steroid-Responsive Encephalopathy With Associated Thyroiditis
Adithya-Sateesh B, Gousy N, Gogna G, Ayele GM, Michael M, Munir KM
AACE Clinical Case Reports. 2023;9(6):205-208
PMID: 38045791 | PMCID: PMC10690420 | DOI: 10.1016/j.aace.2023.10.001

CASE REPORT
51-year-old woman with long-standing hypothyroidism and history of partial thyroidectomy.

PRESENTATION
- Involuntary muscle jerking, muscle shaking, unprovoked falls
- Forgetfulness, intense episodes of feeling overwhelmed, paranoia, impulsive changes of mind
- Intermittent neuropsychiatric symptoms alternating with normal periods

LABORATORY
- TPO antibody: 2112 IU/mL (normal: <34)
- Partial medication nonadherence affecting thyroid hormone levels

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA FOR SREAT
1. Altered consciousness with changes in cognitive ability
2. New or worsening psychiatric symptoms
3. Elevated serum TPO-Abs levels (>=0.5 U/mL) or other antithyroid antibodies
4. Exclusion of infectious, toxic, metabolic, or tumorous etiologies

TREATMENT
Prednisone 60 mg daily. Symptoms of fatigue, cognitive dysfunction, myoclonus, muscle shaking, and palpitations resolved within a few days.

SIGNIFICANCE
- No prior reports of SREAT developing in patients after partial thyroidectomy
- 91% of SREAT patients respond to glucocorticoids
- Those with severe symptoms (coma) have worse prognosis and more prone to relapsing episodes
- ~23.8% of patients with autoimmune thyroiditis experience depression, ~41.6% experience anxiety disorders
