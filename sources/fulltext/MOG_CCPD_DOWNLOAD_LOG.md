## Key Clinical Insights for Madeleine's Case

### 1. MOG-IgG and Peripheral Neuropathy
- MOG-IgG can be found in patients with combined CNS + PNS demyelination (CCPD)
- **Isolated peripheral neuropathy is NOT a feature of MOGAD** (Gupta 2024)
- PNS involvement in MOGAD = polyradiculopathy at CNS-PNS transition zone, not length-dependent neuropathy
- MOG is expressed at dorsal root entry zone where oligodendrocytes extend into spinal roots
- 22+ published CCPD cases with MOG-IgG since 2018 (Papadopoulou 2024 review)

### 2. CCPD (Combined Central and Peripheral Demyelination)
- Rare condition: simultaneous CNS + PNS demyelinating disease
- Multiple antibody associations: anti-NF155 (86% in CCPD), anti-AQP4 (21%), MOG-IgG (growing)
- Anti-NF155 positive CCPD: young onset, tremor, IgG4 subclass, poor IVIg response, good rituximab response
- CCPD patients often have worse outcomes than isolated CNS or PNS demyelination

### 3. ADEM + GBS Overlap
- Rare but documented: shared autoimmune pathogenesis, myelin injury, post-infectious trigger
- 85% of overlap cases identified within 4 weeks of initial diagnosis (Mao 2014)
- Transverse myelitis most common CNS involvement (32%)
- Favorable prognosis in 74% with treatment

### 4. AQP4 and Peripheral Nerve
- AQP4 expressed at CNS-PNS transition zone in roots and cauda equina
- Radiculopathy may be underreported in NMOSD due to symptom overlap with myelitis
- Peripheral neuropathy can be FIRST manifestation of NMOSD (Chi 2018)

### 5. MOG-IgA -- New Biomarker
- MOG-IgA found in 1.6% of double-seronegative demyelinating disease patients (Gomes 2023, JAMA Neurol)
- Different clinical profile from MOG-IgG: more myelitis (65%), more brainstem (44%), less optic neuritis (27%)
- **Implication for IgA deficiency:** Patients with selective IgA deficiency CANNOT produce MOG-IgA -- this may affect disease phenotype or susceptibility

### 6. IgA Deficiency and Autoimmunity
- No published study specifically links IgA deficiency to MOGAD
- No published study links IgA deficiency to CCPD
- IgA deficiency IS associated with multiple autoimmune diseases (SLE, T1D, celiac, Graves, JRA)
- Shared genetic background: HLA-B8, DR3, DQ2 haplotype; IFIH1, CLEC16A genes
- IgA deficiency prevalence in neurological diseases: increased epilepsy, ASD, tic disorders (Magen 2023)

### 7. Relevance to Madeleine
- **Should MOG-IgG be tested?** If there is ANY suspicion of CNS involvement (MR findings, visual symptoms, brainstem signs), MOG-IgG testing is warranted
- **Should AQP4-IgG be tested?** Same indication -- especially given longitudinal cord/root findings
- **Anti-NF155 should be considered** if CIDP-like features are present with CNS signs
- **The IgA deficiency angle is novel** -- no published literature exists on IgA deficiency + MOGAD or IgA deficiency + CCPD, making Madeleine's case potentially publishable if MOG/AQP4 testing reveals new findings
