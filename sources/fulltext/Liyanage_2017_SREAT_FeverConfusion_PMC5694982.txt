Steroid-Responsive Encephalopathy Associated with Autoimmune Thyroiditis Presenting with Fever and Confusion
Liyanage CK, Munasinghe TMJ, Paramanantham A
Case Reports in Neurological Medicine. 2017;2017:3790741
PMID: 29234548 | PMCID: PMC5694982 | DOI: 10.1155/2017/3790741

CASE REPORT
65-year-old female presenting with fever, headache for three days, and progressively worsening confusion.

CLINICAL FINDINGS
- Loose associations and tangential thoughts
- Mini-Mental State Examination score: 25

LABORATORY
- Elevated WBC (15 x 10^3, 85% neutrophils)
- High inflammatory markers (ESR 65 mm/hr, CRP 25.5 mg/l)
- Anti-TPO: >1000 IU/ml (very high)
- Anti-thyroglobulin: 2471.9 IU/ml
- Euthyroid state (normal thyroid function)
- CSF: 2 lymphocytes/mm3, normal protein/glucose

IMAGING
- EEG: right-sided frontal intermittent rhythmic delta activity (FIRDA)
- MRI: age-related atrophy and periventricular white matter changes

TREATMENT AND OUTCOME
IV methylprednisolone (1g daily x 3 days) then oral prednisolone.
Dramatic improvement within 48 hours: resolution of fever, disorientation, and thought disorders.
Prednisolone gradually tapered over four months without symptom recurrence.

KEY POINTS
- SREAT is fundamentally a diagnosis of exclusion
- Requires elevated thyroid antibodies and steroid responsiveness for confirmation
- Condition demonstrates reversibility when properly diagnosed and treated promptly
- High anti-TPO titers are considered a hallmark of the disease
