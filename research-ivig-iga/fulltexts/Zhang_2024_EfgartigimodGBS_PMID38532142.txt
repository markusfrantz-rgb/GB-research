COMPLETE ABSTRACT (verified from PubMed)
============================================================================

BACKGROUND: Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) is caused by immunoglobulin G (IgG)
autoantibodies. Efgartigimod, a human IgG antibody Fc fragment that acts as a
natural ligand for the FcRn, can increase IgG degradation, which thus may be a
promising therapeutic drug for GBS.

CASE PRESENTATION: The two patients presented with postinfectious and acute
flaccid paralysis. On admission, they were bedridden. Nerve conduction studies
indicated peripheral neuropathy. GBS was suspected and they are treated with
two doses of efgartigimod (10 mg/kg) within 5 days. Their muscle strength
improved gradually and 4 weeks after the initial dose, they could walk
independently. Following the first dose, Patient 1 complaint of muscle
soreness, which subsided the next morning. Patient 2 was intubated due to
respiratory failure the day after the initial dose, and did not report other
adverse effects.

DISCUSSION: In GBS patients, two doses of efgartigimod (10 mg/kg) were
effective in rapidly improving muscle strength, with a satisfactory safety
profile. The findings suggest a potential role for efgartigimod in modifying
the disease process in GBS patients.

CONCLUSION: Efgartigimod seems effective and safe in the treatment of GBS.
This study indicates the potential role of efgartigimod as a novel treatment
option for GBS. Well-designed clinical trials should be conducted.

has been utilized for the treatment of GBS (33-35) (Table 3). The disease
types exhibited variability, with two out of the six cases diagnosed as acute
motor axonal neuropathy. In five out of the six cases, a decrease in IgG
levels was observed. The presence of anti-ganglioside antibodies was confirmed
in three out of six cases. Following the administration of efgartigimod, a
decline in all antibodies was observed."

EFGARTIGIMOD GROUP (n=9):
  Demographics:
  - Mean age: 60.11 ± 14.46 years
  - Sex: 4 male (44.4%), 5 female (55.6%)
  - Days to treatment: 5.22 ± 2.86 days
  - GBS-DS on admission: grade 4 (88.9%), grade 5 (11.1%)
  - MRC sum score: 27.22 ± 11.79
  - Grip strength: 0.69 kg median
  - Subtypes: AIDP 11.1%, AMAN 44.4%, AMSAN 33.3%, unclassified 11.1%
  - Baseline IgG: 12.72 ± 4.91 g/L

  Treatment protocol: 10 mg/kg IV, twice within 5 days

  PRIMARY OUTCOME (GBS-DS ≤2 at 4 weeks):
  - Efgartigimod: 3/9 (33.3%) vs IVIg: 3/12 (25.0%), P=1.000 (NS)

  SECONDARY OUTCOMES - MRC SUM SCORE CHANGES:
  - Week 1:  Efgartigimod +4.00 vs IVIg -2.00, P=0.016 *
  - Week 2:  Efgartigimod +15.00 vs IVIg -2.00, P=0.002 **
  - Week 4:  Efgartigimod +6.00 vs IVIg -0.50, P=0.008 **
  - Week 24: Efgartigimod +12.00 vs IVIg +4.00, P=0.008 **

  BIOMARKERS:
  - IgG reduction at week 1: 65.53%
  - NfL (neurofilament light chain): Stable in efgartigimod group vs
    significant increase in IVIg group (P<0.001 between groups at week 1)
  - Anti-GM1 antibodies: Stable in efgartigimod vs marked elevation in
    IVIg group (P<0.001)
  - Albumin: Stable in efgartigimod group; significant decline in IVIg
    group (-4.97 g/L at week 1, P<0.001)

  ADVERSE EVENTS (efgartigimod group, n=9):
  - Grade 3 lung infections: 3 patients
  - Grade 2-3 elevated aminotransferases: 3 patients
  - Grade 2 myalgia: 1 patient
  - Grade 1 fever: 1 patient
  - Grade 1 increased creatinine: 1 patient
  - No deaths

  ADVERSE EVENTS (IVIg group, n=12):
  - Grade 3 lung infections: 3 patients
  - Grade 2 elevated aminotransferases: 1 patient
  - 1 death: 87-year-old woman from respiratory failure at day 13

KEY CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS (synthesized)
============================================================================

1. FIRST-IN-GBS USE: Zhang 2024 was the first published report of
   efgartigimod in GBS. Prior to this, efgartigimod was approved only for
   generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG).

2. MONOTHERAPY FEASIBILITY: Both original patients received efgartigimod
   monotherapy (no IVIG, no PLEX, no steroids), demonstrating standalone
   efficacy.

3. ACCELERATED DOSING: The 2-dose-in-5-days protocol differs from the
   standard weekly dosing used in MG (4 weekly infusions per cycle).
   Rationale: pathogenic antibodies peak during the first week of GBS.

4. RAPID IgG REDUCTION: Patient 1 showed 67% IgG reduction (20.2→6.71 g/L),
   Patient 2 showed 41% reduction (16.2→9.62 g/L).

5. ANTI-GANGLIOSIDE ANTIBODY CLEARANCE: Both patients showed reduction in
   ganglioside antibodies, supporting the mechanistic rationale (FcRn
   blockade promotes degradation of all IgG including pathogenic antibodies).

6. SAFETY SIGNAL: Patient 2 required intubation the day after the first dose.
   This likely reflects disease progression rather than drug effect, but
   highlights that efgartigimod does not prevent initial deterioration in
   severe cases.

7. FUNCTIONAL RECOVERY: Both patients achieved independent ambulation at
   4 weeks, which is a clinically meaningful outcome.

8. GBS SUBTYPES RESPONSIVE: AMAN and MFS-GBS overlap both responded,
   suggesting efficacy across subtypes.

============================================================================
RELEVANCE TO IgA DEFICIENCY
============================================================================

Efgartigimod targets IgG via FcRn blockade. It does NOT affect IgA levels.
This makes it potentially SAFER than IVIG for patients with IgA deficiency:
  - No risk of anti-IgA anaphylaxis (efgartigimod contains no immunoglobulins)
  - No risk of IgA-mediated complement activation
  - Does not require IgA-depleted product sourcing
  - Mechanism is IgG-specific degradation enhancement

For GBS patients with selective IgA deficiency who cannot safely receive
standard IVIG, efgartigimod represents a mechanistically sound alternative
that avoids IgA-related complications entirely.
