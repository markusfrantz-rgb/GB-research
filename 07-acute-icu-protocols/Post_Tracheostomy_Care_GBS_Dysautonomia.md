## 1. SUCTIONING RISKS AND PROTOCOLS IN DYSAUTONOMIC GBS

### 1.1 Why Suctioning Is Extremely Dangerous in Dysautonomic GBS

Tracheal suctioning is the **single most dangerous routine procedure** in a GBS patient with confirmed autonomic dysfunction. The mechanism involves a **vago-vagal reflex** with both afferent and efferent limbs traveling through the vagus nerve:

1. **Suction catheter stimulates tracheal/carinal mechanoreceptors** (afferent vagus)
2. **Vagal efferent discharge causes profound bradycardia** (can reach asystole within seconds)
3. **In GBS dysautonomia:** the normal sympathetic counter-regulation is impaired, so there is NO compensatory tachycardia or vasoconstriction
4. **Result:** Unopposed vagal stimulation can cause sudden cardiac arrest

**Documented outcomes from suctioning in GBS:**
- Bradycardia progressing to **asystole** (multiple case reports)
- Sudden death attributed to suctioning-triggered cardiac arrest
- Severe hypotension requiring vasopressor rescue
- Hypertensive crisis (sympathetic surge during/after suctioning)

(Zochodne, Muscle Nerve, 1994; Weiss et al., Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2025; Frankel et al., Intensive Care Med, 1975) *[Level 4 — fallrapporter och expertutlåtande]*

### 1.2 Closed-Circuit vs Open Suctioning: Which Is Safer?

**CLOSED SUCTIONING IS STRONGLY RECOMMENDED for GBS patients with dysautonomia.**

| Parameter | Open Suction | Closed Suction | Advantage |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Ventilator disconnection** | Required | Not required | Closed: maintains PEEP, prevents derecruitment |
| **Oxygen desaturation** | More frequent | Less frequent | Closed: fewer hypoxic episodes |
| **Hemodynamic instability** | More pronounced | Less pronounced | Closed: fewer BP/HR swings |
| **Vagal stimulation** | Higher (cough, derecruitment) | Lower (continuous ventilation) | Closed: less reflex triggering |
| **Heart rate changes** | Significant increase (compensatory) | Fewer changes | Closed: more hemodynamic stability |
| **VAP risk** | Slightly lower in some studies | Slightly higher in some studies | Conflicting evidence |
| **PEEP maintenance** | Lost during suction | Maintained | Closed: prevents atelectasis |

**Key evidence for GBS specifically:**

- The hemodynamic instability during open suctioning is primarily driven by hypoxia, which stimulates the adrenergic nervous system. In GBS with dysautonomia, this adrenergic response is **unreliable and exaggerated**, making hemodynamic responses unpredictable.
- Closed suctioning maintains continuous positive pressure ventilation, reducing the magnitude of cardiopulmonary parameter changes.
- A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis *[Level 1]* confirmed that closed tracheal suction systems reduce the incidence of hemodynamic disturbances compared to open systems in mechanically ventilated patients.

> **Citation:** Andrade AL, et al. Comparative analysis of open and closed tracheal suction systems on mechanical ventilation efficiency in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Aust Crit Care*. 2024. DOI: 10.1016/j.aucc.2024.09.005
> **Citation:** Jongerden IP, et al. Closed tracheal suction systems versus open tracheal suction systems for mechanically ventilated adult patients. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*. 2007;4:CD004581.

### 1.3 Prophylactic Atropine Before Suctioning

#### Evidence

Prophylactic atropine before tracheal suctioning is supported by evidence from both GBS and spinal cord injury literature (both conditions share the mechanism of impaired sympathetic function with intact vagal tone): *[Level 4 — fallrapporter, extrapolering från ryggmärksskada]*

- **Frankel et al. (1975):** Demonstrated that atropine administration before tracheal suction prevented bradycardia and cardiac arrest in tetraplegic patients. Recommended "adequate oxygenation and atropine administration before tracheal suction in all artificially ventilated tetraplegic patients."
- **Rosen & Hillard (1987):** Showed that both parenteral (IV) and nebulized atropine prevented bradycardic responses to endotracheal suctioning. Nebulized atropine had a wider margin of safety.
- Multiple case reports in GBS specifically document asystole triggered by suctioning that was prevented by prophylactic atropine.

#### Dosing Protocol

**OPTION A: IV Atropine (Preferred for acute protection)**

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dose** | 0.5-1.0 mg IV push |
| **Timing** | 1-3 minutes before suctioning |
| **Onset** | 1-2 minutes IV |
| **Duration** | 30-60 minutes |
| **Repeat** | Before each suctioning episode |
| **Maximum** | 3 mg total in 24 hours (but rarely needed pre-suctioning) |
| **Side effects** | Tachycardia, dry mouth, urinary retention, ileus worsening |

**OPTION B: Nebulized Atropine (Wider safety margin, topical vagolytic effect)**

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dose** | 0.05 mg/kg nebulized (typically 2.5-5 mg for an adult) |
| **Timing** | 10-15 minutes before suctioning |
| **Onset** | 10-15 minutes |
| **Duration** | 2-4 hours |
| **Advantage** | Provides topical mucosal anesthesia, reducing vagal afferent stimulation directly |
| **Advantage** | Longer duration allows multiple suctioning episodes within the window |
| **Disadvantage** | Slower onset; not suitable for urgent/unplanned suctioning |

> **Citation:** Rosen M, Hillard EK. Prevention of bradycardic responses to endotracheal suctioning by prior administration of nebulized atropine. *Crit Care Med*. 1987;15(11):1009-1011.
> **Citation:** Frankel HL, et al. Bradycardia and cardiac arrest during tracheal suction -- mechanisms in tetraplegic patients. *Intensive Care Med*. 1975;1:147-149.

#### Glycopyrrolate as Alternative

**Glycopyrrolate (glycopyrronium) may be PREFERRED over atropine in GBS dysautonomia:**

| Comparison | Atropine | Glycopyrrolate |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| **Duration** | 30-60 min (IV) | 2-4 hours (IV) |
| **CNS effects** | Crosses BBB; may cause confusion, agitation | Does NOT cross BBB; no CNS effects |
| **Heart rate effect** | May cause initial paradoxical bradycardia at low doses | More predictable chronotropic response |
| **Anti-sialagogue potency** | 1x | 5x more potent (reduces secretions) |
| **Dosing** | 0.5-1.0 mg IV | 0.2-0.4 mg IV |
| **GI effects** | Worsens ileus | Also worsens ileus (similar) |
| **Advantage in GBS** | More evidence, lower cost | Longer duration, no CNS effects, better secretion control |

**Glycopyrrolate dosing for pre-suctioning:**
- **0.2 mg IV** given 2-5 minutes before suctioning
- Duration: 2-4 hours (covers multiple suctioning episodes)
- Does not cross blood-brain barrier (important in a sedated patient where CNS effects are undesirable)

> **Citation:** Glycopyrrolate. StatPearls. NCBI Bookshelf. Updated 2024.

### 1.4 Comprehensive Suctioning Protocol for Dysautonomic GBS

```
PRE-SUCTIONING CHECKLIST (MANDATORY for confirmed dysautonomia):
================================================================

[ ] 1. CONFIRM: Crash cart at bedside
[ ] 2. CONFIRM: Transcutaneous pacing pads applied (or external pacer ready)
[ ] 3. CONFIRM: Epinephrine drawn up (1 mg/10 mL syringe at bedside)
[ ] 4. CONFIRM: Atropine 1 mg IV ready (or glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg)
[ ] 5. CONFIRM: Continuous ECG monitoring visible to person suctioning
[ ] 6. CONFIRM: Arterial line waveform visible

BEFORE SUCTIONING:
==================
[ ] 7. Pre-oxygenate with 100% FiO2 for 30-60 seconds
[ ] 8. Administer vagolytic agent:
       - Glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg IV (preferred), OR
       - Atropine 0.5 mg IV, OR
       - If scheduled nebulized atropine given within last 2 hours: proceed
[ ] 9. Wait 2-3 minutes for vagolytic effect
[ ] 10. Communicate to team: "Suctioning in [patient name], dysautonomic GBS"

DURING SUCTIONING:
==================
[ ] 11. USE CLOSED SUCTION SYSTEM (mandatory)
[ ] 12. Suction duration: MAXIMUM 10 seconds per pass
[ ] 13. Suction pressure: < 120 mmHg (< 16 kPa)
[ ] 14. GENTLE insertion -- do NOT force past resistance
[ ] 15. ONE PERSON watches the monitor continuously during suction
[ ] 16. Limit to 2-3 passes maximum per episode
[ ] 17. Allow 30-60 seconds between passes for recovery
[ ] 18. STOP IMMEDIATELY if:
       - Heart rate drops below 50 bpm
       - Heart rate rises above 150 bpm
       - Systolic BP drops below 80 mmHg
       - Systolic BP rises above 200 mmHg
       - Any arrhythmia on monitor

POST-SUCTIONING:
================
[ ] 19. Return FiO2 to baseline (after 1-2 minutes of 100%)
[ ] 20. Monitor HR and BP for 5 minutes after suctioning
[ ] 21. Document: time, suction character, hemodynamic response
[ ] 22. Note any adverse events for the care team
```

## 2. BLOOD PRESSURE MANAGEMENT DURING TRACHEOSTOMY CARE

### 2.1 The Nature of BP Instability in GBS Dysautonomia

GBS dysautonomia causes **labile blood pressure** that is fundamentally different from standard ICU hypertension/hypotension:

- **Rapid oscillations:** BP can swing from severe hypertension (SBP > 200) to hypotension (SBP < 80) within minutes
- **Stimulus-dependent:** Routine care (suctioning, turning, wound care) triggers exaggerated responses
- **Often self-limited:** Episodes typically resolve within minutes without treatment
- **Iatrogenic overshoot is the greatest risk:** Treating hypertension aggressively causes rebound hypotension, and vice versa
- **Diagnostic criterion:** Systolic BP variation > 85 mmHg within the same day (without confounding cause) suggests severe dysautonomia

(Weiss et al., Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2025; Farkas, EMCrit IBCC)

### 2.2 General Principles

**THE CARDINAL RULE: PRIMUM NON NOCERE (First, do no harm)**

1. **Most BP swings are self-limited** -- wait 5-10 minutes before treating
2. **Remove the trigger first** (stop suctioning, pause turning, treat pain, check bladder)
3. **Use ONLY short-acting agents** (minutes to hours, not days)
4. **AVOID long-acting antihypertensives** (amlodipine, ACE inhibitors, ARBs)
5. **AVOID beta-blockers** (can worsen bradycardia; risk of asystole in dysautonomia)
6. **Titrate cautiously** -- these patients are hypersensitive to vasoactive drugs
7. **Tolerate a wider BP range than normal** (SBP 90-180 may be acceptable)

### 2.3 Hypertension Management

**Treatment threshold:** MAP > 120 mmHg sustained for > 5 minutes, OR evidence of target-organ damage (headache, visual changes, encephalopathy, cardiac ischemia)

**Step 1: Remove stimulus**
- Stop current procedure
- Assess pain (give additional analgesia if needed)
- Check bladder (full bladder is a potent autonomic trigger)
- Reduce environmental stimulation

**Step 2: Short-acting IV vasodilators (if MAP remains > 120 mmHg)**

| Agent | Starting Dose | Titration | Onset | Duration | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-----------|-------|----------|-------|
| **Clevidipine** (Cleviprex) | 1-2 mg/hr IV | Double every 2 min until approaching target, then 5-10 min intervals | ~3 min | ~5-15 min after stop | PREFERRED: ultrashort-acting, easily titratable |
| **Nicardipine** (Cardene) | 5 mg/hr IV | Increase by 2.5 mg/hr every 5-15 min | 5-15 min | 15-30 min after stop | Well-studied; slightly longer acting than clevidipine |
| **Nitroprusside** | 0.3 mcg/kg/min IV | Titrate by 0.5 mcg/kg/min every 5 min | 1-2 min | 1-2 min after stop | Reserve for refractory cases; cyanide toxicity risk |
| **Oral nifedipine XR** | 30 mg PO/NG once | No rapid titration | 30-60 min | 12-24 hrs | Only if sustained hypertension; NOT for acute spikes |

**AVOID in GBS dysautonomia:**

| Agent | Why Avoid |
|-------|----------|
| **Labetalol** | Beta-blocker component can worsen bradycardia to asystole |
| **Esmolol** | Beta-blocker; same risk as labetalol |
| **Enalaprilat IV** | Moderate duration; unpredictable in labile patients |
| **Hydralazine** | Unpredictable onset/duration; causes reflex tachycardia |
| **Clonidine** | Central alpha-2 agonist; can cause severe rebound hypertension and worsens bradycardia risk |

### 2.4 Hypotension Management

**Treatment threshold:** MAP < 65 mmHg sustained for > 5 minutes, OR signs of poor organ perfusion (oliguria, altered mental status, rising lactate)

**Step 1: Positional and volume**
- Ensure flat positioning (if hemodynamically unstable)
- IV fluid bolus: 250-500 mL crystalloid (LR preferred)
- Avoid aggressive fluid resuscitation (risk of pulmonary edema)

**Step 2: Vasopressors (if fluid-unresponsive)**

| Agent | Starting Dose | Notes for GBS |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Norepinephrine** | 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min IV | PREFERRED: alpha-1 predominant, less tachycardia than dopamine |
| **Phenylephrine** | 40-100 mcg IV bolus, or 0.5-2 mcg/kg/min | Pure alpha-1; no chronotropic effect; good for isolated hypotension |
| **Vasopressin** | 0.01-0.04 units/min IV | Non-adrenergic; useful when catecholamine response is abnormal |

**AVOID:**

| Agent | Why Avoid |
|-------|----------|
| **Dopamine** | Unpredictable in dysautonomia; arrhythmogenic |
| **Isoproterenol** | Pure beta-agonist; may cause paradoxical hypotension in GBS |
| **Ephedrine** | Indirect mechanism relies on catecholamine stores (may be depleted in dysautonomia) |

### 2.5 Blood Pressure During Specific Care Activities

| Activity | Expected BP Response | Pre-medication Strategy |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Suctioning** | Initial hypertension, then possible bradycardia/hypotension | Glycopyrrolate/atropine (see Section 1) |
| **Turning/repositioning** | Orthostatic-type hypotension or sympathetic surge | Go slowly; pause between position changes; assess HR/BP between moves |
| **Wound care (tracheostomy)** | Pain-triggered hypertension | Ensure adequate analgesia; consider fentanyl 25-50 mcg IV 5 min before |
| **Chest physiotherapy** | Variable | Time with scheduled analgesia; gentle techniques only |
| **Bathing** | Vagal responses; temperature-related changes | Warm water only; brief exposure; continuous monitoring |

## 3. AUTONOMIC STORM PREVENTION

### 3.1 Definition of Autonomic Storm in GBS

An autonomic storm (also called "autonomic crisis" or "dysautonomic storm") is defined as:
- **Simultaneous or rapidly alternating** severe hypertension AND hypotension
- AND/OR severe tachycardia AND bradycardia
- AND/OR profuse diaphoresis, flushing, or piloerection
- AND/OR cardiac arrhythmias (including sustained SVT, VT, or asystole)
- **With hemodynamic instability requiring active intervention**
- **Often precipitated by an identifiable trigger**

### 3.2 Known Triggers to Avoid

**HIGH-RISK TRIGGERS (strongly associated with autonomic storms):**

| Trigger | Mechanism | Prevention |
|---------|----------|------------|
| **Aggressive tracheal suctioning** | Direct vagal stimulation | Gentle technique; max 10 sec; pre-medicate with vagolytic |
| **Deep suctioning past carina** | Intense mechanoreceptor activation | Stop catheter at first resistance; do NOT advance beyond tracheostomy tube tip + 1 cm |
| **Open suction with ventilator disconnection** | Derecruitment + hypoxia + vagal stimulation | Use closed suction system |
| **Cold saline irrigation/lavage** | Temperature-mediated vagal response | Do NOT use cold saline for tracheostomy care; use room-temperature or warmed saline only |
| **Rapid/forceful patient turning** | Baroreceptor stimulation + abdominal compression | Slow, coordinated turns; pause between steps; monitor HR/BP continuously |
| **Full bladder distension** | Pelvic autonomic afferents (similar to autonomic dysreflexia in SCI) | Maintain Foley catheter; ensure patency; monitor urine output hourly |
| **Bowel distension/constipation** | Visceral autonomic afferents | Aggressive bowel regimen; daily rectal assessment; prokinetics |
| **Pain (undertreated)** | Nociceptive afferent overload | Scheduled analgesia; behavioral pain assessment (BPS/CPOT) |
| **Rapid sedation withdrawal** | Loss of sympathetic/parasympathetic dampening | Gradual sedation titration; NEVER stop sedation abruptly |
| **Hypothermia or hyperthermia** | Temperature dysregulation in dysautonomia | Target normothermia; active warming/cooling as needed |
| **Intubation/extubation maneuvers** | Intense airway stimulation | N/A post-tracheostomy, but relevant for tube changes |

### 3.3 Environmental Modifications

| Modification | Rationale |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Minimize unnecessary stimulation** | Reduce afferent input that triggers autonomic responses |
| **Dim lighting in patient area** | Reduce sympathetic arousal |
| **Limit visitors to 1-2 at a time** | Reduce environmental complexity |
| **Consistent staff assignment** | Familiarity reduces anxiety-mediated autonomic activation |
| **Noise reduction** | Alarms at minimum effective volume; avoid sudden loud noises |
| **Coordinate care activities** | Cluster care but allow recovery time between procedures |
| **Room temperature 20-22 C** | Avoid temperature extremes that trigger sweating/shivering |
| **Elevate HOB 30-45 degrees** | Standard position unless hemodynamically contraindicated |

### 3.4 Role of Sedation Depth in Modulating Autonomic Responses

Sedation provides a **pharmacological buffer** against autonomic storms by dampening both sympathetic and parasympathetic responses to stimulation. This creates a clinical dilemma in GBS:

**Deeper sedation advantages:**
- Reduces autonomic storm frequency and severity
- Prevents patient distress during locked-in-like state
- Enables safe suctioning and care procedures

**Deeper sedation disadvantages:**
- Prevents clinical neurological assessment
- Delays detection of clinical improvement
- Increases delirium risk on emergence
- Respiratory depressant effects complicate weaning assessment
- Prolonged propofol (PRIS risk) or benzodiazepine use problematic

**Recommended approach for dysautonomic GBS post-tracheostomy:**

| Phase | Target Sedation | Agent | Rationale |
|-------|----------------|-------|-----------|
| **0-12 hours post-tracheostomy** | RASS -4 to -3 (deep) | Propofol 1-4 mg/kg/hr + fentanyl 25-100 mcg/hr | Surgical stabilization; prevent autonomic destabilization |
| **12-24 hours** | RASS -3 to -2 (moderate) | Reduce propofol by 25% | Begin lightening; tracheostomy better tolerated than ETT |
| **24-48 hours** | RASS -2 to -1 (light) | Continue reducing; add dexmedetomidine 0.2-0.7 mcg/kg/hr if autonomic lability | Dexmedetomidine specifically reduces autonomic lability |
| **48-72 hours** | RASS -1 to 0 | Dexmedetomidine + gabapentin | Target: awake but calm; neuropathic pain control |
| **72+ hours** | RASS 0 (alert) | Dexmedetomidine PRN; gabapentin scheduled | Clinical assessment possible |

**Dexmedetomidine (Precedex) is the IDEAL sedative for GBS dysautonomia:**
- Central alpha-2 agonist: reduces sympathetic outflow
- Provides anxiolysis and sedation without respiratory depression
- Specifically reduces heart rate and BP lability
- Allows meaningful neurological assessment even during infusion (cooperative sedation)
- Dose: 0.2-1.4 mcg/kg/hr IV (no bolus in dysautonomic patients -- risk of bradycardia)
- **Caution:** Can cause bradycardia; have atropine ready; avoid in patients with baseline HR < 60

> **Citation:** Pandit L, Agrawal A. Neuromuscular disorders in critical illness. *Clin Neurol Neurosurg*. 2006;108:621-627.
> **Citation:** Farkas J. Guillain-Barre Syndrome (GBS). EMCrit IBCC. Updated 2025.

### 3.5 Quantified Mortality Impact of Dysautonomia

A retrospective analysis from Mayo Clinic (2000-2017) provides the largest single-center dataset on the impact of autonomic dysfunction on GBS outcomes. *[Level 3 — retrospektiv analys, n=187]*

**Study population:** 187 GBS patients; 71 (38%) had at least one manifestation of dysautonomia. The cohort was predominantly demyelinating (72% AIDP), and 36% of demyelinating patients had dysautonomia.

**Mortality:** **6% in the dysautonomia group vs 2% in the entire cohort** (p=0.02). This three-fold mortality increase underscores the clinical significance of recognizing and managing autonomic dysfunction.

**Dysautonomia manifestations (n=71 dysautonomic patients):**

| Manifestation | Prevalence |
|---|---|
| Ileus | 42% |
| Hypertension | 39% |
| Hypotension | 37% |
| Autonomic fever | 29% |
| Tachycardia/bradycardia | 27% |
| Urinary retention | 24% |

**Associations with dysautonomia:**
- Quadriparesis, bulbar weakness, neck flexor weakness, and need for mechanical ventilation were all significantly associated with dysautonomia
- Higher GBS disability score and Erasmus GBS Outcome Score (EGOS) in the dysautonomia group
- More cardiogenic complications and SIADH in the dysautonomia group

> **Citation:** Chakraborty T, et al. Autonomic dysfunction in Guillain-Barré syndrome: prevalence, clinical spectrum, and outcomes. *Neurocrit Care*. 2020;32:113-120. PMID: 31297663

### 3.6 Dysautonomia Spectrum: Incidence Rates from Two Large Cohorts

The table below compares autonomic manifestation rates from two of the largest cohort studies, highlighting that reported rates vary substantially depending on how dysautonomia is defined and ascertained:

| Manifestation | Chakraborty 2020 (n=71 dysautonomic) | Bazán-Rodríguez 2023 (n=51 dysautonomic) |
|---|---|---|
| Ileus/enteric dysmotility | 42% | 76.1% |
| Hypertension | 39% | 84.8% |
| Hypotension | 37% | 76.1% |
| Tachycardia/bradycardia | 27% | 76.1% |
| Fever (autonomic) | 29% | — |
| Urinary retention | 24% | — |
| Required vasopressor | — | 58.7% |

The higher rates in Bazán-Rodríguez 2023 likely reflect a more severely affected subgroup (all hospitalized, Mexican tertiary center, n=214 total with 51 [23.8%] meeting dysautonomia criteria). Notably, Bazán-Rodríguez found that involvement of **cranial nerves VII, IX, and X** was significantly associated with dysautonomia (p=0.002), as was the need for **mechanical ventilation** (p=0.0001) and **ICU admission** (p=0.0001). *[Level 3 — retrospektiv kohortstudie, n=214]*

> **Citation:** Bazán-Rodríguez L, et al. Autonomic dysfunction in Guillain-Barré syndrome: frequency and clinical associations. *Auton Neurosci*. 2023;245:103070. PMID: 36634383

### 3.7 Cross-Reference: Ventilator Weaning in Dysautonomic GBS

Autonomic dysfunction is a recognized independent risk factor for failed extubation in GBS. Nguyen et al. (2006) demonstrated that autonomic dysfunction was present in **73% of patients who failed extubation** compared with **27% of those successfully extubated** (p=0.008). This has direct implications for ventilator weaning strategy: all weaning attempts — including spontaneous breathing trials (SBT) — must be coordinated with a period of documented autonomic stability. If an autonomic storm or severe hemodynamic instability occurs during an SBT, the trial should be **immediately aborted** and the patient returned to full ventilatory support. For the complete weaning protocol, including SBT criteria and step-by-step procedures, see companion document: `Ventilator_Weaning_GBS_Protocol.md`.

> **Citation:** Nguyen TN, et al. Factors predicting extubation failure in patients with Guillain-Barré syndrome. *Neurocrit Care*. 2006;5(3):230-234. PMID: 17290093

## 4. POST-TRACHEOSTOMY MONITORING PROTOCOL FOR GBS

### 4.1 First 24 Hours: What to Watch For

**CRITICAL PERIOD:** The first 24 hours after tracheostomy carry the highest risk of:

| Complication | Risk Level | Mechanism |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Tracheostomy tube displacement/obstruction** | HIGH | Fresh surgical site; unsecured tract |
| **Bleeding from surgical site** | MODERATE-HIGH | Especially with PE-related coagulopathy |
| **Autonomic storm during first suctioning** | HIGH | First tracheal stimulation post-surgery |
| **Subcutaneous emphysema** | MODERATE | Air leak from fresh stoma site |
| **Pneumothorax** | LOW-MODERATE | Rare but life-threatening |
| **Aspiration** | MODERATE | Cuff leak or deflation |

### 4.2 Continuous Monitoring Parameters

**MANDATORY continuous monitoring for first 72 hours:**

| Parameter | Method | Display | Alert Thresholds |
|-----------|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **ECG (5-lead)** | Continuous telemetry | Bedside + central station | Any new arrhythmia; HR < 50 or > 130 |
| **Heart rate** | From ECG | Continuous numeric | < 50: STOP all procedures, give atropine 0.5 mg IV |
| | | | > 150: assess for autonomic surge vs SVT |
| **SpO2** | Continuous pulse oximetry | Bedside | < 92%: assess for tube obstruction, secretions, pneumothorax |
| **Blood pressure** | Arterial line (MANDATORY in severe dysautonomia) | Beat-to-beat | SBP < 80: fluid bolus + vasopressor |
| | | | SBP > 200: assess trigger; short-acting vasodilator |
| | | | SBP variation > 85 mmHg/day: severe dysautonomia confirmed |
| **Respiratory rate** | Ventilator + clinical | Continuous | Sudden change suggests tube displacement or secretion plug |
| **EtCO2** | Capnography (via tracheostomy) | Continuous waveform | Flat waveform = tube displacement/obstruction |
| **Temperature** | Continuous (core preferred) | Every 1 hour | > 38.5 C: infection vs autonomic fever (check CRP, procalcitonin, cultures) |
| **Urine output** | Foley catheter + hourly measurement | Hourly | < 0.5 mL/kg/hr: assess volume status; may reflect autonomic-mediated renal vasoconstriction |

### 4.3 Clinical Assessments

| Assessment | Frequency | Performed By |
|------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Tracheostomy site inspection** | Every 2 hours (first 24h), then every 4h | Nurse |
| **Tracheostomy cuff pressure** | Every 4 hours | Nurse/RT |
| **Secretion volume and character** | Each suctioning episode | RT/Nurse |
| **Skin color around stoma** | Every 2 hours | Nurse |
| **Subcutaneous emphysema check** | Every 4 hours (first 24h) | Nurse |
| **Pupil size and reactivity** | Every 4 hours | Nurse |
| **GCS/RASS** | Every 2 hours | Nurse |
| **Pain assessment (BPS/CPOT)** | Every 4 hours, before procedures | Nurse |
| **Bowel sounds and abdominal exam** | Every 8 hours | Nurse |
| **Passive ROM of extremities** | During each assessment | Nurse/PT |

### 4.4 Laboratory Monitoring Schedule (First 72 Hours)

| Test | Day 0 (Post-trach) | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Rationale |
|------|---------------------|-------|-------|-------|-----------|
| **CBC** | X | X | X | X | Bleeding; infection |
| **Coagulation (PT, PTT, fibrinogen)** | X | X | | X | PE-related coagulopathy; bleeding risk |
| **Electrolytes** | X | X | X | X | Dysautonomia-related; refeeding risk |
| **Calcium (ionized)** | X | X | | | Citrate from PE; arrhythmia prevention |
| **Magnesium** | X | X | | X | Arrhythmia prevention |
| **Phosphate** | X | X | X | | Refeeding risk |
| **Glucose** | Q4h | Q4h | Q6h | Q6h | Stress hyperglycemia; autonomic effects |
| **Lactate** | X | If hypotensive | | | Perfusion marker |
| **CRP** | X | | X | | Baseline; distinguish infection from autonomic fever |
| **Procalcitonin** | X | | X | | Distinguish infection from autonomic fever |
| **ABG** | X | PRN | PRN | PRN | Ventilation adequacy; acid-base |
| **Blood cultures** | If temp > 38.5 | If temp > 38.5 | | | Tracheostomy-related bacteremia |
| **Sputum culture** | X (baseline) | | X | | VAP surveillance |

### 4.5 When to Call for Help: Specific Thresholds

**IMMEDIATE (call physician + prepare for resuscitation):**

| Event | Threshold | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Bradycardia** | HR < 40 bpm | Atropine 1 mg IV; prepare transcutaneous pacing |
| **Asystole** | Flatline on telemetry | CPR; atropine 1 mg IV; epinephrine 1 mg IV; transcutaneous pacing |
| **Sustained tachycardia** | HR > 150 for > 2 minutes | 12-lead ECG; if hemodynamically unstable: cardioversion |
| **Severe hypotension** | SBP < 70 mmHg for > 2 minutes | Fluid bolus 500 mL; norepinephrine start; check for bleeding |
| **Severe hypertension** | SBP > 220 mmHg for > 5 minutes | Clevidipine or nicardipine infusion |
| **Tube displacement** | Unable to ventilate via tracheostomy | Attempt oral intubation; call ENT/surgery |
| **Massive hemorrhage from stoma** | Active bright red bleeding > 50 mL | Direct pressure; call surgery; check coagulation; prepare for OR |
| **Pneumothorax** | Sudden SpO2 drop + absent breath sounds + subcutaneous air | Needle decompression; chest tube |
| **Seizure** | New seizure activity | Lorazepam 2-4 mg IV; consider PRES if preceded by severe hypertension |

**URGENT (call physician within 15 minutes):**

| Event | Threshold | Assessment |
|-------|----------|------------|
| **Temperature > 38.5 C** | New or rising | Cultures; CRP/procalcitonin; distinguish autonomic fever from infection |
| **New arrhythmia** | Any rhythm change on telemetry | 12-lead ECG; electrolytes; medication review |
| **Urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for 2 hours** | Consecutive low readings | Volume status; bladder scan; renal perfusion |
| **Increasing secretion volume** | Doubling from baseline | Assess for infection; adjust humidification |
| **New subcutaneous emphysema** | Crepitus around stoma or neck | CXR; assess for pneumothorax or tube displacement |
| **Fibrinogen < 1.5 g/L** | On coagulation check | Hold PE session 4/5 until corrected; replace with cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate |

## 5. FIRST 24-72 HOURS: DETAILED CARE PROTOCOL

### 5.1 Hour-by-Hour Protocol (First 12 Hours)

```
HOUR 0 (Return from tracheostomy procedure):
=============================================
- Confirm tracheostomy tube in correct position (chest X-ray)
- Check cuff pressure (20-25 cmH2O)
- Secure ties/sutures verified
- Establish baseline vital signs
- Resume ventilator settings
- Check for subcutaneous emphysema
- Ensure EtCO2 waveform present
- Draw post-procedure labs (CBC, coagulation, electrolytes)
- Document tracheostomy tube type and size
- Confirm crash cart stocked and defibrillator tested
- Confirm transcutaneous pacing pads in place
- Resume scheduled medications via NG/OG

HOUR 1-2:
=========
- First gentle suctioning IF secretions audible/visible
  --> Follow full pre-suctioning protocol (Section 1.4)
  --> This is the HIGHEST RISK suctioning event
  --> Physician should be present or immediately available
- Inspect stoma for bleeding (q15 min initially)
- Monitor hemodynamic response to first suctioning
- Document character and volume of secretions

HOUR 2-6:
=========
- Vital signs q30 min (including full hemodynamic assessment)
- Suction only PRN (when audible secretions or SpO2 decline)
- Assess tracheostomy site q2h
- Resume enteral feeding if hemodynamically stable (1-2 hours post-return)
- Begin sedation lightening if hemodynamically stable at hour 6

HOUR 6-12:
==========
- Vital signs q1h if stable (q30 min if labile)
- Consider reducing propofol by 25% if hemodynamically stable
- Second tracheostomy site inspection (check ties, stoma, cuff)
- Overnight plan: maintain current monitoring intensity
- Ensure night staff briefed on dysautonomia-specific precautions
```

### 5.2 Day 1 (Hours 12-24)

- Continue closed-circuit suctioning PRN with pre-medication protocol
- Target sedation lightening to RASS -3 to -2
- Begin daily sedation vacation protocol (if hemodynamically stable):
  - Pause sedation at a consistent time daily
  - Assess responsiveness, purposeful movement
  - Resume at lower dose if autonomically stable
- Check first set of post-tracheostomy labs
- Reassess readiness for PE session 4 (coagulation status)
- Physical therapy: passive ROM (gentle, avoiding autonomic triggers)
- Speech therapy consultation (planning for future communication)

### 5.3 Day 2-3 (Hours 24-72)

- Transition to q1h vital signs if hemodynamically stable for > 12 hours
- Continue sedation lightening toward RASS -1 to 0
- Add dexmedetomidine if autonomic lability persists during lightening
- PE session 4 (if coagulation permits): monitor closely for hemodynamic effects
- First tracheostomy dressing change (if indicated by surgical team)
- Begin neurological assessment as sedation allows:
  - Eye opening, tracking
  - Command following (blink, shoulder shrug)
  - Any spontaneous limb movement
  - Corneal and gag reflexes
- PE session 5 (if coagulation permits): complete PE course
- After session 5: begin formal treatment response monitoring plan
- Draw NfL, IgG levels, anti-ganglioside Ab (if not already done)

## 6. EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROTOCOLS

### 6.1 Bradycardia/Asystole During Suctioning

```
IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (within seconds):
====================================
1. STOP suctioning immediately -- withdraw catheter
2. CALL for help
3. Pre-oxygenate with 100% FiO2 via ventilator
4. Check pulse -- if pulseless, begin CPR

IF PULSE PRESENT BUT HR < 40:
5. Atropine 1 mg IV push (can repeat x 2, total 3 mg)
6. If no response to atropine:
   - Activate transcutaneous pacing (rate 60-80, output to capture)
   - Epinephrine 2-10 mcg/min IV infusion
7. Monitor for recovery (typically occurs within 30-120 seconds after stopping suction)
8. Document event
9. MODIFY future suctioning protocol:
   - Increase pre-medication dose
   - Consider glycopyrrolate 0.4 mg (double dose) before next suction
   - Consider scheduled Q4h glycopyrrolate during acute dysautonomia phase

IF ASYSTOLE:
5. Begin CPR (if not already started)
6. Atropine 1 mg IV (every 3-5 minutes, max 3 mg)
7. Epinephrine 1 mg IV (every 3-5 minutes)
8. Transcutaneous pacing
9. Continue ACLS protocol
10. Note: GBS asystole from suctioning often responds rapidly to atropine + cessation of stimulation
    - Do NOT delay resuscitation waiting for spontaneous recovery
    - But be aware that recovery may occur within 30-60 seconds
```

### 6.2 Hypertensive Crisis During Care

```
IF SBP > 200 mmHg during care activities:
==========================================
1. STOP the triggering procedure
2. Ensure patient is not in pain (assess BPS/CPOT)
3. Check bladder distension
4. Wait 5 minutes -- many episodes self-resolve
5. If persistent:
   - Clevidipine 1-2 mg/hr IV, double every 2 min to effect
   - OR Nicardipine 5 mg/hr IV, increase by 2.5 mg/hr every 5-15 min
6. Target: reduce SBP by no more than 25% in first hour
7. AVOID over-treatment (risk of rebound hypotension)
8. Once BP normalizing, actively titrate infusion DOWN
9. Discontinue antihypertensive within 30-60 minutes if trigger removed
```

### 6.3 Autonomic Storm (Combined Instability)

```
IF simultaneous HR/BP instability with diaphoresis:
====================================================
1. STOP all care activities
2. Reduce environmental stimulation (lights, noise, touch)
3. Ensure adequate sedation depth:
   - Propofol bolus 20-50 mg IV if RASS > -2
   - OR Dexmedetomidine bolus 0.5-1 mcg/kg over 10 min (CAUTION: monitor for bradycardia)
4. Manage each component:
   - Bradycardia: atropine 0.5-1 mg IV
   - Tachycardia: assess cause; if SVT, consider adenosine 6 mg IV
   - Hypertension: clevidipine/nicardipine
   - Hypotension: phenylephrine 100 mcg IV bolus or norepinephrine infusion
5. Consider magnesium sulfate 2 g IV over 20 min (membrane stabilizer, reduces autonomic excitability)
6. Monitor continuously until stable for 30 minutes
7. Document triggers and response for future care planning
8. Review and update care plan to prevent recurrence
```
