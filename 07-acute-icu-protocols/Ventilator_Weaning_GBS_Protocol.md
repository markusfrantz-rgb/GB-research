## 1. PREDICTORS OF PROLONGED MECHANICAL VENTILATION

Approximately **30% of all GBS patients** require mechanical ventilation (MV). Of those ventilated, **71% require prolonged MV (>14 days)** and **64% experience prolonged weaning (≥7 days)** (Thille 2025, n=513 GBS). In the Thille 2025 cohort, **43% of ventilated GBS patients had bulbar weakness** and **57% underwent tracheostomy**. Early identification of high-risk patients is essential for tracheostomy timing and family communication. *[Level 2b — prospective cohort; Level 2 — multicenter retrospective cohort]*

> **Citation:** Walgaard C, et al. Tracheostomy or Not: Prediction of Prolonged Mechanical Ventilation in Guillain-Barré Syndrome. *Neurocrit Care*. 2017;26(1):6-13. PMID: 27538676

### 1.1 EGRIS Score (Erasmus GBS Respiratory Insufficiency Score)

The EGRIS score predicts MV need **within the first week**, validated in a large international cohort (n=1500, IGOS). *[Level 2a — validated prediction model, large multicenter cohort]*

| EGRIS Score | Risk of MV | Clinical Implication |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| 0-2 (low) | ~4% | Standard ward monitoring may suffice |
| 3-4 (intermediate) | ~25-40% | ICU admission recommended, prepare for intubation |
| 5-7 (high) | 65-90% | Anticipate intubation, plan early tracheostomy discussion |

**AUC 0.86** (95% CI 0.84-0.89). Components: days from onset to admission (0-2 pts), facial/bulbar weakness (0-1 pt), MRC sum score (0-4 pts).

> **Citation:** Doets AY, et al. Erasmus GBS Respiratory Insufficiency Score: External Validation in the IGOS Consortium. *Neurology*. 2022;98(8):e820-e829. PMID: 35106830

### 1.2 Neuromuscular Predictors of Prolonged Ventilation (>14 Days)

| Predictor | Threshold | OR for Prolonged MV | Sensitivity/Specificity | Source |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------|------------------------|--------|
| **Deltoid MRC grade** | ≤2 bilaterally at day 7 | 9.3 | 95% need >14d MV | Walgaard 2017 |
| **Inexcitable motor nerves** | No CMAP on NCS | — | 96% need >14d; 89% need >28d | Walgaard 2017 |
| **Axonal degeneration on NCS** | Present at day 14 | — | Strong predictor | Walgaard 2017 |

*[Level 2b — prospective cohort, n=552]*

**Clinical implication:** If deltoid MRC ≤2 at day 7 or motor nerves inexcitable → probability of MV >14 days is >95%. Perform tracheostomy early.

## 2. TIMELINE AND DECISION POINTS

- **Median MV duration:** 25 days (IQR 13-51) in the largest cohort (Thille 2025, n=513 GBS) *[Level 2 — multicenter retrospective cohort]*; 28 days (IQR 12-60) in an earlier cohort (Walgaard 2017, n=552) *[Level 2b]*
- **Median ICU stay:** 35 days (IQR 19-65) for ventilated GBS (Thille 2025) *[Level 2 — multicenter retrospective cohort]*
- **Median time to decannulation:** 193 days (range 49-527) (Cheng 2021) *[Level 4]*
- Recovery follows a **non-linear trajectory** — plateaus are common before resumed improvement.

### 2.1 Structured Assessment Timeline

| Timepoint | Assessment | Action |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Day 5-7** | MRC sum score, deltoid strength, FVC, clinical trajectory | First weaning assessment; if deltoid MRC ≤2 → anticipate prolonged MV |
| **Day 14** | Nerve conduction studies + NfL/NfH biomarkers | Inexcitable nerves → prepare for >28d; plan tracheostomy if not yet done |
| **Day 28** | Formal weaning readiness (FVC, NIF, cough, secretions) | First serious SBT attempt if criteria met |
| **Week 8** | Repeat NCS + clinical reassessment | Late responders may begin improving; reassess prognosis |
| **Month 3-6** | Decannulation readiness | Evaluate for decannulation or long-term ventilation plan |

*[Level 5 — expert consensus, synthesized from multiple sources]*

## 3. WEANING READINESS CRITERIA

Thresholds from a prospective study of 44 GBS patients requiring MV (Nguyen 2006). *[Level 2b — prospective cohort]*

| Parameter | Threshold for Weaning | p-value | Notes |
|-----------|----------------------|---------|-------|
| **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC)** | >22 mL/kg | p=0.003 | Single best predictor of extubation success |
| **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF)** | More negative than -50 cmH2O | p=0.0005 | Reflects diaphragmatic and inspiratory muscle recovery |
| **VC improvement** | >4 mL/kg improvement from pre-intubation to pre-extubation | — | 82% sensitivity, 90% PPV for successful extubation |
| **Cough Peak Flow (CPF)** | >160 L/min | — | Required for effective secretion clearance |

> **Citation:** Nguyen TN, et al. Factors predicting extubation outcome in patients with Guillain-Barré syndrome. *Neurocrit Care*. 2006;5(3):230-234. PMID: 17290095

### 3.1 Non-Respiratory Prerequisites

Before attempting weaning, confirm: *[Level 5 — expert consensus]*

- Stable or improving MRC sum score; no active pneumonia or systemic infection
- No suctioning needed for >24 hours; minimal or no sedation required
- Hemodynamically stable without high-dose vasopressors

## 4. SPONTANEOUS BREATHING TRIAL PROTOCOL POST-TRACHEOSTOMY

### 4.1 SBT Methodology

**Method:** T-piece trial or low-level pressure support ventilation (PSV 5-8 cmH2O) via tracheostomy. *[Level 5 — expert consensus, adapted from general ICU guidelines]*

**Duration progression:**
1. Initial trial: **30 minutes**
2. If tolerated on two consecutive days: extend to **60 minutes**
3. Progressive extension to **2 hours** before considering continuous spontaneous breathing
4. Final goal: **24 hours** of unassisted breathing before decannulation assessment

### 4.2 SBT Failure Criteria

Abort the SBT immediately if ANY of the following occur:

| Parameter | Failure Threshold | GBS-Specific Note |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Respiratory rate** | >35 breaths/min | May be masked by intercostal weakness |
| **Heart rate change** | >20% from baseline | In dysautonomic patients, interpret with caution (see Section 6) |
| **SpO2** | <92% | Supplement with ABG if borderline |
| **Systolic BP** | >180 mmHg or <90 mmHg | Dysautonomic patients may have wide baseline swings |
| **Accessory muscle use** | Visible paradoxical breathing | Diaphragmatic fatigue — resume ventilation immediately |
| **Subjective distress** | Agitation, diaphoresis, anxiety | Consider pain, secretion burden, autonomic storm |

**IMPORTANT:** In GBS with dysautonomia, standard BP and HR failure criteria must be interpreted against the patient's baseline autonomic variability. A patient with known labile BP (e.g., swings of 80-200 mmHg) should not have their SBT terminated for a single BP excursion within their known range. Document the patient's autonomic baseline before initiating SBT. *[Level 5 — expert opinion]*

**Cross-reference:** See [Post_Tracheostomy_Care_GBS_Dysautonomia.md](Post_Tracheostomy_Care_GBS_Dysautonomia.md) for detailed autonomic monitoring protocols.

## 5. DECANNULATION CRITERIA AND NIV BRIDGING

### 5.1 Prerequisites for Decannulation

All of the following must be met before decannulation: *[Level 4 — retrospective cohort + expert consensus]*

| Criterion | Threshold | Assessment Method |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| **FVC** | >20 mL/kg sustained over ≥48h | Bedside spirometry via tracheostomy |
| **Effective cough** | Independent secretion clearance | Cough peak flow >160 L/min; clinical observation |
| **Swallowing** | Bedside swallowing assessment passed | FEES or clinical assessment; no aspiration |
| **Secretion burden** | No suctioning needed for >24 hours | Nursing documentation |
| **Spontaneous breathing** | Tolerates ≥24h off ventilator | Continuous monitoring during trial |
| **Level of consciousness** | Alert, cooperative | GCS 15 or patient's baseline |

### 5.2 NIV as a Bridge to Decannulation

- **59%** of weaned GBS patients used NIV during the process (Cheng 2021) *[Level 4 — retrospective cohort, n=29]*
- **14%** required long-term nocturnal NIV after decannulation

**NIV bridge protocol:** Full support → nocturnal-only via tracheostomy → daytime capping trials + nocturnal ventilation → decannulate when daytime capping tolerated ≥48h → nocturnal NIV via face mask if needed.

**Median time to decannulation: 193 days** (range 49-527). Plan for prolonged rehabilitation, speech/swallowing therapy, and transfer to a weaning facility when ICU care no longer required.

> **Citation:** Cheng MCF, et al. Prolonged Ventilatory Support for Patients Recovering From Guillain-Barré Syndrome. *Neurol Clin Pract*. 2021;11(1):18-24. PMID: 33968468

## 6. DYSAUTONOMIA CONSIDERATIONS DURING WEANING

### 6.1 Autonomic Dysfunction as a Barrier to Weaning

Autonomic dysfunction is independently associated with failed extubation in GBS. In a prospective cohort: *[Level 2b — prospective cohort]*

- **73%** of patients who failed extubation had autonomic dysfunction
- **27%** of successfully extubated patients had autonomic dysfunction
- Difference statistically significant: **p=0.008**

> **Citation:** Nguyen TN, et al. *Neurocrit Care*. 2006;5(3):230-234. PMID: 17290095

### 6.2 Sedation Management During Weaning

**Dexmedetomidine is preferred** during weaning trials in dysautonomic GBS: anxiolysis without respiratory depression, attenuates sympathetic surges, allows neurological assessment. *[Level 4 — case series, expert consensus]*

**Protocol:** Transition to dexmedetomidine (0.2-0.7 mcg/kg/h) 24-48h before SBT. Gradually wean as SBT duration increases. **Do NOT abruptly discontinue** — taper over 24-48h to avoid rebound autonomic instability.

### 6.3 SBT Abort Criteria Specific to Dysautonomia

In addition to standard SBT failure criteria (Section 4.2), abort immediately for: *[Level 5 — expert consensus]*

- **Autonomic storm:** Simultaneous hypertension (>200 mmHg systolic), tachycardia (>130 bpm), and diaphoresis
- **Severe BP instability:** Systolic BP oscillation >60 mmHg within 5 minutes not attributable to positional change
- **Sustained bradycardia:** HR <50 bpm for >2 minutes (risk of asystole in dysautonomic patients)
- **New arrhythmia:** Any sustained arrhythmia not present at baseline

**Cross-reference:** See [Post_Tracheostomy_Care_GBS_Dysautonomia.md](Post_Tracheostomy_Care_GBS_Dysautonomia.md) for autonomic storm management protocols.

## 7. REINTUBATION AND WEANING FAILURE

### 7.1 Reintubation Rates and Weaning Outcomes (Thille 2025)

The largest study to date on ventilator weaning in GBS (n=886; 513 GBS, 373 MG; 47 ICUs in France; 10 years, 2014-2023) provides definitive data on weaning difficulty and reintubation risk. *[Level 2 — multicenter retrospective cohort]*

**Key GBS-specific findings:**

| Outcome | GBS (n=513) | MG (n=373) | p value |
|---------|-------------|------------|---------|
| **Prolonged weaning (≥7 days)** | 64% | 35% | <0.001 |
| **Tracheostomy performed** | 57% | 17% | <0.001 |
| **Extubation attempted** | 46% | 88% | <0.001 |
| **Reintubation rate** | 26% | 29% | 0.440 |
| **ICU mortality** | 9.4% | 9.7% | 0.882 |
| **Median MV duration (days)** | 25 [IQR 13-51] | 9 [IQR 6-21] | <0.001 |
| **Median ICU stay (days)** | 35 [IQR 19-65] | 15 [IQR 9-28] | <0.001 |

**Context:** The GBS reintubation rate of 26% is approximately 2-3x higher than the ~10% rate in the general ICU population. However, despite far longer MV duration, GBS mortality remained relatively low (9.4%).

**SBT and extubation patterns in GBS:**
- Only **49%** of GBS patients underwent any SBT (vs 87% MG)
- **77%** of GBS tracheostomies were performed directly without prior SBT
- Tracheostomy was performed earlier in GBS (median 12 days [IQR 6-19]) than MG (18 days [IQR 11-26])
- **56%** of tracheostomized GBS patients were decannulated before ICU discharge
- **76%** of tracheostomized GBS patients were deventilated before ICU discharge

**Cough strength as a key determinant:** The reintubation rate was **61%** in patients (GBS or MG) extubated with ineffective cough (40/66 patients), compared to far lower rates in those with effective cough (p<0.001). This strongly supports requiring effective cough (CPF >160 L/min) as a mandatory criterion before extubation (see Section 3).

**Clinical implication:** Prolonged SBTs (≥8 hours) did **not** reduce the risk of reintubation in this cohort. Instead, the study suggests that waiting for disease recovery — especially recovery of cough strength and bulbar function — before attempting extubation may be more important than SBT duration.

> **Citation:** Thille AW, et al. Characteristics of ventilator weaning in patients intubated for Guillain-Barré syndrome or myasthenia gravis: a nationwide multicenter study. *Intensive Care Med*. 2025;51:2054-2064. PMID: 41114833

### 7.2 Risk Factors for Weaning Failure

| Risk Factor | Evidence | Source |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| **Ineffective cough** | 61% reintubation rate with ineffective cough vs far lower with effective cough (p<0.001) | Thille 2025 |
| **Male sex** | Males more likely to experience prolonged weaning (aOR 1.41, 95% CI 1.06-1.88, p=0.017) | Thille 2025 |
| **Pulmonary comorbidities** | 79% in failed group vs 36% in success group (p=0.008) | Nguyen 2006 |
| **Autonomic dysfunction** | 73% vs 27% (p=0.008) | Nguyen 2006 |
| **Bulbar weakness** | Impaired airway protection → aspiration risk; 43% of ventilated GBS in Thille 2025 | Leonhard 2019, Thille 2025 |
| **Pneumonia** | Incidence 30-50% in ventilated GBS; prolongs weaning | Busl 2023 |
| **Axonal variant (AMAN/AMSAN)** | Slower respiratory muscle recovery | Walgaard 2017 |
| **Early extubation** | Earlier extubation associated with higher reintubation risk (p=0.014) | Thille 2025 |

*[Level 2b — prospective cohort; Level 2 — multicenter retrospective cohort]*

> **Citation:** Nguyen TN, et al. *Neurocrit Care*. 2006. PMID: 17290095
> **Citation:** Leonhard SE, et al. Diagnosis and management of Guillain-Barré syndrome in ten steps. *Nat Rev Neurol*. 2019;15(11):671-683. PMID: 31541214
> **Citation:** Busl KM. Respiratory Care in Guillain-Barré Syndrome. *Neurol Clin*. 2023;41(3):493-510. PMID: 36964442
> **Citation:** Thille AW, et al. *Intensive Care Med*. 2025;51:2054-2064. PMID: 41114833

**Cross-reference:** For detailed management of autonomic dysfunction during weaning, see [Post_Tracheostomy_Care_GBS_Dysautonomia.md](Post_Tracheostomy_Care_GBS_Dysautonomia.md).

### 7.3 Management After Failed Weaning

If ≥2 failed SBTs within one week: *[Level 5 — expert consensus]*

1. Re-evaluate for reversible causes (pneumonia, atelectasis, secretion burden, pain, delirium)
2. Optimize mechanics: upright positioning, chest physiotherapy, bronchoscopy if atelectasis
3. Consider repeat immunotherapy if clinical plateau suggests ongoing disease activity
4. Return to full support; re-attempt SBT in 5-7 days

## 8. LONG-TERM OUTCOMES AFTER PROLONGED VENTILATION

### 8.1 Weaning Success and Functional Recovery

| Outcome | Rate | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| Successfully weaned from prolonged MV | 76% | Cheng 2021 |
| Not decannulated | 24% | Cheng 2021 |
| Long-term invasive ventilation | 14% | Cheng 2021 |
| Short-distance assisted ambulation post-weaning | 45% | Cheng 2021 |
| Mortality at 36 months | 17% | Cheng 2021 |

*[Level 4 — retrospective cohort, n=29]*

> **Citation:** Cheng MCF, et al. *Neurol Clin Pract*. 2021;11(1):18-24. PMID: 33968468

**ICU mortality in larger cohort:** In Thille 2025 (n=513 GBS), ICU mortality was **9.4%** — notably, mortality was higher in patients who required reintubation (7% GBS, 16% MG) than in those successfully extubated. This reinforces the importance of optimizing extubation readiness rather than attempting premature extubation. *[Level 2 — multicenter retrospective cohort]*

### 8.2 Ventilated vs Non-Ventilated GBS at 6 Months

Independent walking: **57% ventilated** vs **87% non-ventilated** (Walgaard 2017). *[Level 2b]*

### 8.3 Key Messages

- **Recovery continues up to 1-2 years** — ventilator-dependent patients at month 3 may still achieve independent breathing
- Rehabilitation should begin in the ICU; psychological support essential for alert ventilator-dependent patients
- Discharge planning should involve pulmonology, neurology, rehabilitation medicine, and home ventilation services *[Level 5 — expert consensus]*

> **Citation:** Leonhard SE, et al. *Nat Rev Neurol*. 2019;15(11):671-683. PMID: 31541214
