## 1. Why BBE-GBS Overlap Requires a Distinct ICU Approach

BBE-GBS overlap is not simply "severe GBS." The combination of **central nervous system involvement** (consciousness disturbance, brainstem dysfunction) with **peripheral nervous system devastation** (flaccid tetraplegia, respiratory failure) creates unique ICU challenges that neither pure GBS nor pure BBE protocols fully address. *[Level 4]*

| Challenge | Pure GBS | BBE-GBS Overlap |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Consciousness | Preserved | **Impaired** (drowsy → coma) |
| Ventilation cause | Peripheral respiratory muscle weakness | Peripheral weakness **+ central drive impairment** |
| Weaning limiting factor | Muscle strength | Strength **+ consciousness recovery** |
| Dysautonomia | Common (up to 75%) | Common **+ brainstem-mediated** |
| Communication | Possible (eye movements, blinks) | **May be impossible** (locked-in) |
| Steroid rationale | Not recommended (8 RCTs negative) | **Considered** for BBE/CNS component |
| EEG utility | Exclusion of seizures | **Essential** for consciousness assessment |
| Prognosis assessment | mEGOS, NfL, NCS | All of above **+ anti-GQ1b status** |

Key principle: **The BBE component determines the ceiling of recovery speed.** Peripheral nerve recovery may outpace consciousness recovery, or vice versa. Treatment must address both axes simultaneously.

## 2. Consciousness Assessment and Monitoring

### 2.1 The Spectrum

Consciousness disturbance in BBE ranges from drowsiness to coma. In Koga 2012 (n=37 BBE): mild 21%, moderate 46%, severe/unarousable 24%. *[Level 3]*

**The fundamental question:** Is the patient unconscious (BBE-related), sedated (iatrogenic), or locked-in (conscious but unable to signal)?

### 2.2 Tools

| Tool | What it shows | When to use |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| **Continuous EEG** | Cortical function, reactivity, seizures, sleep architecture | **All BBE-GBS patients on admission.** Normal reactive alpha = preserved consciousness. Spindle coma = brainstem dysfunction with intact cortex (Shimozono 2012). |
| **Blink reflex** | Pontine circuit integrity | Early — may be only abnormal NCS finding in BBE |
| **ABR** | Brainstem auditory pathway | Assists prognosis; preserved = better outcome (Kurihara 2020) |
| **SEPs** | Somatosensory cortical function | SEP-ABR dissociation supports immune nodopathy diagnosis |
| **ERPs** | Cognitive processing | Normal auditory ERPs = preserved consciousness in locked-in (Ragazzoni 2000) |
| **fMRI** | Command following | Gold standard for detecting covert awareness (Norton 2022) — but requires transport |

### 2.3 Anti-GQ1b-Positive = Faster Consciousness Recovery

Median consciousness recovery: **10 days** (anti-GQ1b+) vs 23 days (anti-GQ1b−) (Yoshikawa 2020). This informs when to expect improvement and when to worry. *[Level 3]*

### 2.4 Brain Death Mimicry

Anti-GQ1b syndrome can present with coma, apnea, complete eye paralysis, total body paralysis — indistinguishable from brain death on clinical exam. **EEG showing normal reactive alpha rhythm is definitive.** Do NOT proceed with organ donation evaluations without EEG in suspected GBS/BBE (Tian 2023). *[Level 4]*

## 3. Sedation Strategy — The Autonomic-Awareness Dilemma

### 3.1 The Problem

Deeper sedation reduces autonomic storm frequency but:
- Prevents detection of consciousness recovery (the key BBE prognostic marker)
- Delays weaning assessment
- Masks pain and distress in a potentially fully aware patient

### 3.2 Recommended Protocol

**Dexmedetomidine is the ideal sedative** for BBE-GBS overlap: central alpha-2 agonist, reduces sympathetic outflow, no respiratory depression, allows neurological assessment (Weiss 2025). *[Level 4]*

| Phase | Target RASS | Regimen | BBE-Specific Note |
|-------|------------|---------|-------------------|
| 0–12h post-trach | −4 to −3 | Propofol 1–4 mg/kg/hr + fentanyl 25–100 µg/hr | Minimize autonomic triggers |
| 12–24h | −3 to −2 | Reduce propofol 25% | Begin EEG monitoring |
| 24–48h | −2 to −1 | Add dexmedetomidine 0.2–0.7 µg/kg/hr | Daily sedation pause for consciousness assessment |
| 48–72h | −1 to 0 | Dex + gabapentin | Look for signs of awareness |
| 72h+ | 0 | Dex PRN, gabapentin scheduled | Attempt communication |

**Caution:** No dexmedetomidine bolus in dysautonomic patients (bradycardia risk). Dose 0.2–1.4 µg/kg/hr IV.

### 3.3 Daily Sedation Pause Protocol

1. Stop sedation infusions (keep analgesics)
2. Observe for 30–60 minutes
3. Assess: eye opening? tracking? response to command? any voluntary movement?
4. If autonomic instability → resume sedation at 50% previous dose
5. Document findings — even tiny changes may signal consciousness returning

## 4. Dysautonomia Management

### 4.1 Prevalence and Detection

BBE/GBS overlap: autonomic disturbance in 31% (Koga 2012) to 75% of ventilated patients (Weiss 2025). *[Level 3]*

**Detection:** Systolic BP variation **> 85 mmHg daily** suggests dysautonomia (more sensitive than the older 40–50 mmHg threshold). *[Level 4]*

### 4.2 Protocol

| Problem | Intervention | Caution |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Hypertension** (systolic > 180) | Labetalol 10–20 mg IV, esmolol infusion, clonidine | **Rebound hypotension** — titrate cautiously |
| **Hypotension** (MAP < 65) | Crystalloid bolus, phenylephrine, norepinephrine | Minimize volume in fluid-positive patients |
| **Tachycardia** (HR > 120) | Identify cause first (pain, fever, PE). Dexmedetomidine preferred. | Beta-blockers risky → may unmask bradycardia |
| **Bradycardia/asystole** | **IV atropine at bedside at all times.** Can be triggered by minimal stimulation (suctioning, mouth care) | External pacer on standby |
| **Ileus** | Erythromycin 250 mg IV q6h, maintain K+ > 4.0 | Stop anticholinergics, minimize opioids |
| **Urinary retention** | Catheterize, plan early removal | UTI risk; daily assessment |
| **PRES risk** | 7% of dysautonomic GBS; exacerbated by IVIg. MRI if new visual symptoms or seizures. | Aggressive BP control |
| **Takotsubo** | Echocardiography if persistent hemodynamic instability despite treatment (Kimura 2021) | May explain refractory hypotension |

### 4.3 Autonomic Storm During Procedures

Tracheal suctioning is the most common trigger. Protocol:
1. Pre-oxygenate
2. Limit suction pass to ≤10 seconds
3. Monitor ECG continuously during and 5 minutes after
4. If bradycardia develops → stop immediately → atropine 0.5 mg IV
5. If severe BP surge → short-acting agent only (esmolol)

## 5. Mechanical Ventilation and Weaning

### 5.1 Ventilation in BBE-GBS Overlap

- **34% of BBE patients** require MV (Ito 2008) vs 1% of pure MFS
- **16% of anti-GQ1b cohort** overall (Coly 2024)
- Median MV duration in GBS: 25 days (IQR 13–51, Thille 2025 n=513)
- BBE-GBS overlap may have prolonged courses: Chae 2018 case — tracheostomy still in place at 8 months

### 5.2 BBE-Specific Weaning Considerations

Standard GBS weaning criteria apply (see [Ventilator Weaning GBS Protocol](/doc/07-acute-icu-protocols/Ventilator_Weaning_GBS_Protocol.md)), with critical additions:

1. **Consciousness must recover** before meaningful weaning trials — in BBE, this may lag days to weeks
2. **Consciousness recovery in anti-GQ1b+ BBE:** median 10 days (Yoshikawa 2020)
3. **Foot flexion ability** absent at immune therapy conclusion → predicts prolonged MV (Weiss 2025)
4. **Cough effectiveness** is the strongest predictor of extubation success — 61% reintubation rate without effective cough (Thille 2025) *[Level 2]*

### 5.3 Tracheostomy Timing

- Consider at **10–14 days** if no improvement trajectory
- Delaying beyond 14 days increases VAP risk (OR 8.2)
- BBE patients may need earlier tracheostomy given prolonged consciousness disturbance

### 5.4 Weaning Readiness Criteria

All of the following must be met before weaning trial:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|----------|
| Consciousness | **At minimum: eye opening and response to stimuli** |
| FiO₂ | ≤ 40% |
| PEEP | ≤ 8 cmH₂O |
| Hemodynamics | Stable without high-dose vasopressors |
| Temperature | < 38.5°C |
| Sedation | Minimal / off |

## 6. Communication in Near-Locked-In and Locked-In States

### 6.1 The Critical Principle

GBS/BBE patients with complete paralysis **usually have fully intact consciousness, vision, and hearing.** The locked-in-like state is psychologically devastating. **Always address the patient directly — explain all procedures, assume full awareness.** *[Level 5]*

### 6.2 Communication Hierarchy

| Priority | Method | When |
|----------|--------|------|
| 1 | Eye movements, blinks | If any voluntary eye control remains |
| 2 | Communication cards, alphabet boards | If eye-pointing possible |
| 3 | Speaking valve (Passy-Muir) | After tracheostomy, if vocal cord function present |
| 4 | **cEEG interface** | When all visible voluntary movement is lost |
| 5 | fMRI command following | When other methods fail; requires transport |

### 6.3 cEEG Communication Protocol (Pisano 2025)

When even brow raises become invisible, standard bedside EEG can amplify **nonvisible** frontalis muscle activity:

- **Electrodes:** Fp2-F8, Fp1-F3, F3-C3, Fp2-F4 (standard frontal leads, AP bipolar montage)
- **Settings:** Sensitivity 70 µV/mm, time base 30 mm/s, 60 Hz notch, 1 Hz LF, 70 Hz HF
- **Code:** 2 peaks = "yes", 1 peak = "no"
- **Training:** Minimal — family described as "super easy"
- **Uses existing ICU equipment** — no additional hardware needed

**Clinical applications:** Pain level assessment, anxiety screening, dyspnea reporting, yes/no medical decisions, exclusion of non-convulsive status epilepticus. *[Level 4]*

### 6.4 Psychological Burden

- **60% hallucinations**, 70% delusions, 30% illusions in severe GBS ICU patients (Cochen 2005, n=139)
- 82% moderate-severe anxiety; 67% depression
- Visual hallucinations and paranoid delusions peak around day 9
- PTSD particularly prevalent in ventilated GBS patients
- **Consistent staff assignment** improves communication familiarity and trust

## 7. VTE Prophylaxis — The Hunter Lesson

### 7.1 Why This Is Section 7, Not an Afterthought

> **Citation:** Hunter G et al. BBE presenting to the ICU. *Neurocrit Care*. 2012;17:102-106. PMID: 22622843

An 80-year-old BBE patient survived 7 weeks of coma and was **actively recovering neurologically** — eye movements improving, beginning to speak — when he **died of pulmonary embolism.** A preventable death. *[Level 4]*

PE is responsible for approximately **half of all fatal GBS cases.**

### 7.2 Protocol

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Pharmacological** | Enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily (30 mg if <50 kg or CrCl <30). Start within 24h of ICU admission. |
| **Alternatives** | Dalteparin 5000 IU SC daily, tinzaparin 4500 IU SC daily, UFH 5000 IU SC q8-12h (if renal failure), fondaparinux 2.5 mg SC daily (if HIT) |
| **Mechanical** | IPC devices + graduated compression stockings. Use IPC alone during PE-induced coagulopathy. |
| **Duration** | **Entire period of immobility** — including rehabilitation phase |
| **Monitoring** | Weekly D-dimer is NOT useful for screening. Systematic duplex US screening if high clinical suspicion. |

### 7.3 Special Situations

- **During PE coagulopathy** (fibrinogen <2 g/L from plasmapheresis): maintain mechanical prophylaxis, hold LMWH until fibrinogen > 1.5 g/L
- **DVT incidence with screening:** 52% even with LMWH (Bersano 2016) — consider routine screening in prolonged immobilization *[Level 3]*

## 8. Infection Prevention and Steroid-Induced Immunosuppression

### 8.1 Baseline Infection Risk in GBS

| Complication | Incidence |
|-------------|-----------|
| VAP | 30–78% of ventilated patients |
| Septic shock | 22–24% |
| UTI | Common (catheter-related) |
| CLABSI | Risk increases with CVC duration |
| Any complication | **Two-thirds** of ICU GBS patients |

### 8.2 Steroid-Specific Risk in BBE

When IVMP is given for the BBE component:
- Further suppresses immune response on top of disease-related immunoparesis
- **Requires heightened infection surveillance** for the steroid duration + 2 weeks after

**Monitoring during/after IVMP:**
- Temperature continuous or q4h
- CRP + procalcitonin daily (procalcitonin distinguishes infection from GBS inflammation)
- WBC + differential daily during IVMP, then 3x/week
- Blood cultures with any new fever
- Low threshold for chest imaging with new respiratory symptoms

### 8.3 Prevention Bundles

**VAP prevention:**
- HOB elevation 30–45°
- Oral care with chlorhexidine q6h
- Daily sedation holiday
- Daily weaning assessment
- Subglottic drainage
- Air-stacking maneuvers + chest physiotherapy
- **Tracheostomy at 10–14 days** reduces VAP risk (OR 8.2 for delayed trach)

**Line management:**
- Daily assessment: can any line be removed?
- PE catheter removal as soon as PE sessions complete (reduces infection portal)
- Strict insertion bundles for any new lines

**Catheter management:**
- Daily assessment for Foley removal
- Intermittent catheterization preferred over indwelling when feasible

## 9. Pain Management

### 9.1 Prevalence and Types

Up to **89%** of GBS patients experience pain. In BBE-GBS overlap, neuropathic pain is less prominent than in classic GBS but still occurs (Bannai 2026). *[Level 3]*

| Type | Characteristics |
|------|----------------|
| Neuropathic | Burning, tingling, electric-shock, allodynia |
| Radicular | Low back, sciatica-like (often preceding weakness) |
| Musculoskeletal | Deep aching from immobility, contractures |
| Visceral | Abdominal — dysautonomia/ileus related |

### 9.2 Protocol

```
1. Baseline: Acetaminophen 1 g q6h (scheduled)

2. First-line neuropathic: Gabapentin 300 mg TID → titrate to 900 mg TID
   (15 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses)
   Evidence: RCT (Pandey 2002, n=18 ventilated): pain 7.22 → 2.06,
   fentanyl reduction −251 µg [Level 2]
   Alternative: Pregabalin 75 mg BID → 150-300 mg BID

3. Second-line: Carbamazepine 100 mg TID (effective from day 4)
   OR SNRI (duloxetine/venlafaxine)

4. Opioids: PRN with caution (ileus risk, respiratory depression during weaning)

5. Adjuncts: Lidocaine 5% patches (low risk), TENS, positioning, warm/cool compresses
```

**Avoid in dysautonomia:** Tricyclic antidepressants (worsen autonomic instability, urinary retention, ileus).

**Pain assessment in ventilated patients:** Use Behavioral Pain Scale (BPS) or Critical-Care Pain Observation Tool (CPOT).

## 10. Nutrition

### 10.1 Unique Challenges

BBE-GBS overlap patients face: gastroparesis, ileus (dysautonomia), dysphagia (bulbar palsy), high metabolic demands, risk of aspiration.

### 10.2 Protocol

| Component | Target/Protocol |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Timing** | Enteral nutrition within 24–48h of ICU admission |
| **Route** | Nasogastric if tolerated; post-pyloric (NJ tube) if gastroparesis |
| **Start** | 10–20 mL/hr, advance by 10–20 mL/hr q6h |
| **Calories** | 25–30 kcal/kg/day (indirect calorimetry preferred) |
| **Protein** | 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day (higher range in acute phase) |
| **Position** | HOB ≥ 30° during and 30 min after feeds |
| **Monitoring** | Gastric residual q4-6h (>500 mL → hold, prokinetics) |

### 10.3 Metabolic Monitoring

| Parameter | Frequency | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Phosphate | Daily first 5–7 days | >30% drop → IV repletion (refeeding syndrome) |
| K+, Mg2+ | Daily | Maintain K+ > 4.0 (arrhythmia + ileus prevention) |
| Glucose | q4–6h | Hyperglycemia worsens ICU outcomes |
| Pre-albumin | Weekly | Nutritional adequacy |
| Thiamine | Before feeding starts | If any refeeding risk |

## 11. Early ICU-Based Rehabilitation

### 11.1 Start Early

Begin physiotherapy as soon as hemodynamically stable — may be within 24h of ICU admission. **~40% of hospitalized GBS patients require inpatient rehabilitation.** *[Level 3]*

### 11.2 ICU Physiotherapy Protocol

| Phase | Focus | Frequency |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| Acute (week 1–4) | Passive ROM all joints, positioning, respiratory physio, contracture prevention | 2× daily, 30 min |
| Plateau | Active-assisted exercises where possible, sitting balance, tilt table | 2× daily |
| Recovery | Progressive strengthening, gait retraining, endurance | Increasing duration |

### 11.3 Key Interventions

- **Contracture prevention:** Passive ROM at least 2× daily. **Ankle dorsiflexion splinting** critical — foot drop is the most common residual deficit (Lombet 2021: ankle dorsiflexion was last to recover at 4.5 years)
- **Inspiratory muscle training (IMT):** Start after 7 days of MV. Threshold device, 5 sets of 6 breaths at ≥50% MIP, once daily. Reduces MV duration by ~3.2 days. *[Level 2]*
- **Respiratory physiotherapy:** Chest percussion, secretion mobilization, assisted cough (manual or mechanical insufflation-exsufflation)
- **DVT prevention dual role:** IPC devices also provide passive muscle stimulation

### 11.4 Recovery Pattern (BBE-GBS Overlap)

Based on Lombet 2021 (24-year-old BBE-GBS, MRI-positive, 4.5-year follow-up) and Chae 2018:

**Motor recovery:** Proximal → distal, upper limb → lower limb. Shoulder/elbow recover first; ankle dorsiflexion and toe extension last.

**Functional milestones (composite from literature):**

| Milestone | Typical timeline |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Consciousness recovery | Median 10 days (anti-GQ1b+) |
| First limb motor improvement | 2–3 weeks |
| Ventilator weaning | 3–4 weeks (range: days to months) |
| Sitting balance | 6–8 weeks |
| Standing with assistance | 3–5 months |
| Walking with aids | 6–12 months |
| Near-complete motor recovery | 1–4.5 years |

## 12. Post-Steroid Monitoring in BBE

### 12.1 When IVMP Is Given for BBE

No established post-IVMP protocol exists. Based on literature synthesis (Bannai 2026, Weiss 2025):

| Timepoint | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **During IVMP (3–5 days)** | Daily neurological assessment, glucose q6h, infection markers daily |
| **24–48h after last dose** | Assess objective response: consciousness? eye movements? strength? |
| **If improvement** | No oral taper. Monitor for relapse weeks 2–4. |
| **If no improvement at 48h** | Discontinue steroids. Consider IVIg (if possible), PLEX, or escalation to efgartigimod/rituximab. |
| **Week 2–4** | Repeat MRI if clinical trajectory stagnates or worsens |
| **Post-steroid infection window** | Heightened surveillance for 2 weeks (immunosuppression lag) |

### 12.2 Steroid-GBS Paradox

Corticosteroids are NOT effective in GBS (8 RCTs, Cochrane: Hughes 2016). Animal models show steroids **reduce macrophage infiltration needed for nerve debris clearance** → may delay peripheral nerve regeneration (Wang 2015). The rationale for IVMP in BBE-GBS overlap is strictly the **central/CNS component** — not the peripheral GBS. *[Level 1 for GBS inefficacy; Level 4–5 for BBE benefit]*

## 13. Long-Term Sequelae to Anticipate

### 13.1 Beyond Motor Recovery

BBE-GBS overlap recovery is not complete when the patient walks. Clinicians should actively screen for:

| Sequela | Prevalence | Details |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Cognitive impairment** | Documented | Attention, processing speed, dual-tasking deficient at 4.5 years despite near-complete motor recovery (Lombet 2021) |
| **Sleep dysregulation** | 80% (pediatric) | 75% met criteria for obstructive sleep apnea on polysomnography; hypothesized vagus/glossopharyngeal nerve + reticular formation injury (Santoro 2019) |
| **Autonomic dysregulation** | 40% (pediatric) | Orthostatic symptoms for ~1 year; resolves without pharmacotherapy (Santoro 2019) |
| **Fatigue** | Very common | Hallmark of GBS recovery; may persist years |
| **Neuropathic pain** | ~30% at 1 year | Chronic, may require long-term gabapentinoids |
| **Depression/anxiety** | 67% / 82% | 4.8× increased depression risk vs general population |
| **PTSD** | Prevalent | Especially in ventilated patients; screen at 3 and 6 months |
| **Persistent oscillopsia** | Minority | May outlast limb recovery (Bannai 2026) |

### 13.2 Screening Recommendations

- **3 months:** Neuropsychological screening, polysomnography if snoring/daytime sleepiness, orthostatic vitals
- **6 months:** Depression/anxiety screening (PHQ-9, GAD-7), PTSD screening, pain assessment
- **12 months:** Comprehensive neuropsychological testing if cognitive complaints persist
- **Vaccination:** Safe to resume standard vaccines ≥6 months post-diagnosis (Santoro 2019, n=5 pediatric, no complications)

## 14. Decision Algorithm: Daily ICU Checklist for BBE-GBS Overlap

```
DAILY ASSESSMENT — BBE-GBS OVERLAP
═══════════════════════════════════

□ CONSCIOUSNESS
  ├─ Sedation pause performed? (minimum 30 min)
  ├─ Any new voluntary movement? Eye opening? Tracking?
  ├─ EEG findings today?
  └─ Communication attempt? (hierarchy: eyes → cards → cEEG)

□ AUTONOMIC
  ├─ BP range past 24h? (>85 mmHg variation = dysautonomia)
  ├─ Heart rate range? Bradycardia episodes?
  ├─ Atropine at bedside? ✓
  ├─ Temperature?
  └─ Bowel function? (ileus is common)

□ VENTILATION
  ├─ Ready for weaning trial? (FiO₂ ≤40, PEEP ≤8, conscious)
  ├─ Cough effectiveness? (strongest extubation predictor)
  ├─ Tracheostomy indicated? (day ≥10-14 without improvement)
  └─ Inspiratory muscle training? (start after day 7 MV)

□ PREVENTION
  ├─ VTE prophylaxis given? (LMWH + IPC)
  ├─ Infection surveillance: CRP, procalcitonin, cultures PRN
  ├─ Lines needed? (remove any unnecessary → infection portals)
  ├─ Foley still needed? (assess for removal daily)
  └─ Post-steroid window? (if IVMP given: heightened surveillance ×2 weeks)

□ COMFORT
  ├─ Pain assessed? (BPS/CPOT)
  ├─ Gabapentin dose adequate?
  ├─ Patient addressed directly? (assume full awareness)
  └─ Family visit/communication facilitated?

□ REHABILITATION
  ├─ Passive ROM performed? (2× daily, all joints)
  ├─ Ankle splinting in place?
  ├─ Chest physiotherapy?
  └─ Nutrition target met? (25-30 kcal/kg/day, protein 1.2-2.0 g/kg/day)

□ TREATMENT RESPONSE
  ├─ Day since last immune therapy?
  ├─ Any clinical improvement? (document precisely)
  ├─ If IVMP given: objective response check at 24-48h
  └─ If no response at expected timeframe: escalation discussion
```
