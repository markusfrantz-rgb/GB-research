## 1. IVIg AFTER PLASMAPHERESIS

### 1.1 Can IVIg Be Given After PE? What Does the Evidence Say?

**Yes, IVIg can be given after PE, but the evidence for BENEFIT is weak.**

The 1997 Plasma Exchange/Sandoglobulin GBS Trial (n=379) randomized patients to PE alone, IVIg alone, or PE followed immediately by IVIg. The combination arm showed **no additional benefit** over either treatment alone. This finding led the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline to recommend **against** planned sequential PE-then-IVIg as a treatment strategy.

**However, there is a critical distinction that must be understood:**

- The 1997 trial and the 2023 guideline recommendation pertain to **planned combined sequential therapy** -- i.e., giving both treatments from the outset as a deliberate strategy
- There is **no RCT and no specific guideline recommendation** addressing the scenario of **switching to IVIg after PE has clearly failed**
- The 2025 Frontiers in Neurology review (Roe et al.) explicitly identified "insufficient evidence" regarding sequential therapy in either direction when the first treatment fails
- In clinical practice, switching modalities after treatment failure is a pragmatic decision driven by disease severity, despite limited formal evidence

**Important safety note for IVIg after PE:** When IVIg is given after PE (as opposed to PE after IVIg), there is NO risk of removing therapeutic IVIg -- the direction-specific risk only applies to PE washing out previously administered IVIg.

> **Citation:** Plasma Exchange/Sandoglobulin Guillain-Barre Syndrome Trial Group. Randomised trial of plasma exchange, intravenous immunoglobulin, and combined treatments in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Lancet*. 1997;349(9047):225-230.

> **Citation:** Frontiers in Neurology. Plasma exchange following immunoglobulins for Guillain-Barre Syndrome: a persisting problematic practice? *Front Neurol*. 2025. DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1712202

### 1.2 Optimal Timing: How Long After Last PE Session Before Starting IVIg?

**Minimum 48 hours after the last PE session is the widely cited practical recommendation.**

Rationale:
- PE removes plasma proteins non-selectively from the intravascular space
- Immediately after PE, the intravascular volume is replaced with albumin (not immunoglobulin)
- Over approximately 6 hours, IgG begins to redistribute from the interstitial compartment into the intravascular space via the thoracic duct and capillary pores
- Giving IVIg too soon (within 24 hours) risks partial removal of the infused IVIg by any residual PE effects and suboptimal distribution
- By 48 hours, hemodynamic redistribution is largely complete, and any IVIg administered will not be removed

**In practice:**
- **48-72 hours** after the last PE session is the most commonly recommended interval
- If clinical urgency demands earlier administration, IVIg can potentially be started 24 hours after the last PE, but with awareness that some degree of washout may occur
- The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline does not specify an exact interval, as it recommends against the combination entirely

### 1.3 Does PE Wash Out IVIg If Given Too Close Together?

**Yes, PE will remove IVIg if performed after IVIg administration.**

Key pharmacokinetic data:
- A single PE session processing approximately 1.0 plasma volume removes **60-65% of circulating IgG**
- If PE is performed shortly after IVIg, the infused therapeutic IgG is removed along with endogenous IgG
- The 2011 study by Haupt et al. found that PE after IVIg showed no association between timing interval and improved outcomes, suggesting the IVIg removal was clinically significant regardless of exact timing

**In the REVERSE direction (IVIg given AFTER PE), the washout concern does not apply** -- PE cannot remove IVIg that has not yet been administered. The concern is only relevant if PE is performed AFTER IVIg.

> **Citation:** Haupt WF, Rosenow F, van der Ven C, Borberg H, Pawlik G. Plasma exchange after initial intravenous immunoglobulin treatment in Guillain-Barre syndrome: critical reassessment of effectiveness and cost-efficiency. *J Neurol*. 2011;258:S255-S259.

### 1.4 The SID-GBS Trial: Does It Apply When Switching FROM PE to IVIg?

**No, the SID-GBS trial findings do NOT directly apply to the PE-to-IVIg switch scenario.**

The SID-GBS trial (Walgaard et al., *Lancet Neurology* 2021) demonstrated that a **second course of IVIg after an initial IVIg course** provided no benefit and increased serious adverse events in poor-prognosis GBS patients. Critically:

- **SID-GBS studied:** IVIg (2 g/kg) --> Second IVIg (2 g/kg) vs. placebo
- **The PE-to-IVIg switch is a fundamentally different scenario:** PE (failed) --> IVIg (first exposure to IVIg)
- In SID-GBS, patients had already received a full IVIg course, and the second course raised IgG levels even higher without clinical benefit while increasing thromboembolic risk
- In the PE-to-IVIg switch, the patient has never received IVIg, and PE has actually **depleted** IgG levels, creating a very different immunological starting point

**The 2025 pharmacodynamic analysis** of SID-GBS (van Tilburg et al., *Ann Clin Transl Neurol*) confirmed that the problem with a second IVIg dose was not about IgG levels per se but about the lack of additional immunomodulatory benefit when IVIg had already been given. This mechanism-based argument further supports that giving IVIg de novo after PE failure is a different clinical question.

**Key point:** The SID-GBS trial should NOT be cited as evidence against IVIg after PE failure. They address different clinical scenarios.

> **Citation:** Walgaard C, Jacobs BC, Lingsma HF, et al. Second intravenous immunoglobulin dose in patients with Guillain-Barre syndrome with poor prognosis (SID-GBS). *Lancet Neurol*. 2021;20(4):275-283.

> **Citation:** van Tilburg SJ, Huizinga R, Kuitwaard K, et al. If it does not help, it might hurt: Pharmacodynamics of a second IVIg course in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Ann Clin Transl Neurol*. 2025;12(5):966-975.

### 1.5 IgG Levels After PE: Depletion and Implications for IVIg Dosing

**PE causes profound IgG depletion:**

| Parameter | After PE Course (5 sessions) | Recovery Timeline |
|-----------|------------------------------|-------------------|
| Total IgG reduction | 80-85% of baseline | ~12 weeks to full recovery |
| IgG1 reduction | ~65% per session | Slow recovery; not within 20% of baseline at 3 weeks |
| IgG2 reduction | ~64% per session | Similar to IgG1 |
| IgG3 reduction | ~61% per session | Fastest recovery; near baseline by 3 weeks |
| IgG4 reduction | ~70% per session | Slowest recovery |
| IgA reduction | ~60-65% per session | Returns to ~80% of baseline by 3 weeks |
| IgM reduction | ~60% per session | Returns to ~80% of baseline by 3 weeks |

**After a full course of 5 PE sessions over 7-14 days, total IgG levels are typically reduced to 16-20% of baseline.**

**Implications for IVIg dosing after PE:**
- Standard IVIg dosing (0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days = 2 g/kg total) should be used regardless of prior PE
- The depleted IgG state does NOT require higher IVIg doses -- the SID-GBS trial demonstrated no benefit from additional IVIg
- However, the depleted state means that a larger fraction of infused IVIg may be catabolized rapidly (the IgG recycling system via FcRn is not saturated), potentially reducing the effective duration of action
- **IgG levels should be measured before starting IVIg** to document the degree of depletion and guide monitoring

> **Citation:** Miyasaka N, Hara M, Koike T, et al. Effect of Therapeutic Plasma Exchange on Immunoglobulins in Myasthenia Gravis. *Transfusion*. 2017;57(5):1132-1140. (PMC5440840)

> **Citation:** Warner A, et al. Use of immunoglobulin G homeostatic set point and recovery time in plasmapheresis donor safety monitoring. *Vox Sanguinis*. 2025. DOI: 10.1111/vox.13800

## 2. EFGARTIGIMOD AFTER PE AND/OR IVIg

### 2.1 Mechanism of Action -- Why It Is Relevant to GBS

Efgartigimod (Vyvgart, argenx) is a human IgG1 Fc fragment that competitively binds the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), blocking the normal IgG recycling mechanism. This accelerates IgG catabolism, reducing total serum IgG levels by:
- **~50% after a single dose** (10 mg/kg IV)
- **~75% after multiple doses** (4 weekly infusions)
- IgG levels return to baseline approximately **8 weeks** after the last dose

Efgartigimod does NOT affect IgM, IgA, IgE, or albumin -- it is **IgG-specific**.

This mechanism is directly relevant to antibody-mediated GBS subtypes where anti-ganglioside IgG antibodies drive nerve damage.

### 2.2 Can Efgartigimod Be Given After PE? Timing?

**Yes, efgartigimod can be given after PE, and the combination may be synergistic in IgG reduction.**

**Rationale:**
- PE removes IgG from the intravascular space (bulk removal)
- Efgartigimod prevents IgG recycling via FcRn (sustained catabolism)
- Together, they achieve deeper and more prolonged IgG suppression
- PE depletes IgG acutely; efgartigimod prevents rebound

**Timing:**
- A formal PLEX-to-efgartigimod study protocol (multicentre, three-arm, open-label, 48-week study in generalized MG) is currently being conducted, investigating sequential PLEX followed by efgartigimod
- Based on available evidence, efgartigimod can be started **as early as 24-48 hours after the last PE session**
- There is no pharmacological reason to wait longer -- efgartigimod works via a completely different mechanism than PE
- Preclinical studies showed efgartigimod effectively reduced IgG when given **3 days** post-IVIg (and would work even sooner post-PE, as there is no competing IVIg to consider)

**In the GBS context:**
- After PE failure (3+ sessions without improvement), starting efgartigimod within 48 hours is reasonable
- The depleted IgG state from PE means efgartigimod will work on the residual and rebounding IgG
- Monitor IgG levels before and during efgartigimod to ensure they do not drop to dangerously low levels (risk of infection)

> **Citation:** PMC12320067. Efgartigimod following plasma exchange in the treatment of subjects with generalised myasthenia gravis: study protocol for a multicentre, three-arm, open-label study. 2025.

### 2.3 If IVIg Was Given, Does Efgartigimod Remove the Therapeutic IVIg?

**YES -- this is a critical interaction.**

Efgartigimod accelerates catabolism of ALL IgG, without distinguishing between:
- Pathogenic autoantibodies (anti-ganglioside IgG)
- Therapeutic IVIg (exogenous IgG)
- Protective immunoglobulins (infection-fighting IgG)

**Therefore:**
- If efgartigimod is given shortly after IVIg, it will accelerate clearance of the therapeutic IVIg, potentially negating its benefit
- This is analogous to how PE removes IVIg -- but via a different mechanism (accelerated catabolism rather than physical removal)
- The key question is whether the IVIg has had time to exert its immunomodulatory effects before efgartigimod clears it

**Optimal sequencing if both are used:**

| Sequence | Timing | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| IVIg first, then efgartigimod | Wait at least **7-14 days** after completing IVIg before starting efgartigimod | Allows IVIg time to exert immunomodulatory effects (FcRn saturation, anti-idiotypic effects, Fc-gamma receptor modulation) before efgartigimod accelerates its clearance |
| PE first, then efgartigimod | Wait **24-48 hours** | No conflicting mechanism; synergistic IgG depletion |
| PE first, IVIg, then efgartigimod | IVIg immediately after PE (>48h gap); efgartigimod 7-14 days after IVIg | Most complex; allows each modality to work before the next |

**The IVIg-then-efgartigimod sequence is paradoxical:** IVIg works partly by raising IgG levels and saturating FcRn, while efgartigimod works by blocking FcRn and lowering IgG levels. These are mechanistically opposing strategies.

**A phase 4 study** (NCT06307626) is investigating the transition from IVIg to efgartigimod in CIDP, starting efgartigimod **1 week** after the last IVIg infusion, based on a balance between efficacy and safety.

> **Citation:** NeurologyLive. Phase 4 Trial to Test Transition From IVIg to Efgartigimod in Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy. 2025.

### 2.4 Optimal Sequencing: PE --> Efgartigimod vs PE --> IVIg --> Efgartigimod

**For a patient who has failed PE and has IgA deficiency, PE --> efgartigimod (skipping IVIg) may be the optimal sequence.**

Arguments FOR PE --> efgartigimod directly (bypassing IVIg):
1. **Eliminates IgA-related anaphylaxis risk entirely** -- efgartigimod contains no IgA
2. **Mechanistically synergistic** -- PE depletes, efgartigimod prevents rebound
3. **No paradoxical interaction** -- unlike the IVIg-then-efgartigimod conflict
4. **Faster to initiate** -- no need for the 7-14 day waiting period after IVIg
5. **Emerging evidence supports efficacy** -- multiple case reports show improvement in PE/IVIg-refractory GBS

Arguments for including IVIg in the sequence:
1. IVIg has multiple mechanisms beyond IgG level modulation (anti-idiotypic antibodies, complement scavenging, cytokine modulation)
2. These additional mechanisms are NOT replicated by efgartigimod
3. If an IgA-depleted IVIg product is available and the patient has tolerated SCIG, IVIg may be feasible

**Recommended approach for this patient:**
- Given IgA deficiency and PE failure: **PE --> efgartigimod** is the most logical and safest sequence
- If IgA-depleted IVIg (Gammagard S/D, IgA <1 mcg/mL) is available AND anti-IgA antibodies are negative, consider PE --> IVIg (IgA-depleted) --> efgartigimod (after 7-14 days)

### 2.5 Case Reports of Efgartigimod in GBS

**All published GBS case reports to date (2024-2026):**

#### Case 1: Ripellino et al., 2025 (*European Journal of Neurology*)
- **Patient:** 60-year-old woman, fulminant AIDP
- **Prior treatment:** 5 PE sessions + full IVIg course (2 g/kg) -- both FAILED
- **Status at efgartigimod start:** Severely tetraparetic, bedridden, mechanically ventilated, minimal MRC improvement. Day 30 post-admission.
- **Efgartigimod protocol:** 10 mg/kg IV weekly x 4 doses
- **Response:** Improvement in upper limb strength within HOURS of first dose. After 2nd-3rd infusions: upper limbs MRC grade 4. Tracheostomy removed at 2 months. Walking with aid at 2 weeks post-last infusion. Walking alone at 4 months from onset.
- **Key point:** This patient had failed BOTH PE and IVIg before efgartigimod was tried.

> **Citation:** Ripellino P, et al. Efgartigimod as Add-On Treatment in Refractory Guillain-Barre Syndrome: A Case Report. *Eur J Neurol*. 2025;32(8):e70308. DOI: 10.1111/ene.70308

#### Case 2: BMC Neurology, 2025 (MFS-GBS Overlap)
- **Patient:** GBS with poor IVIg response
- **Prior treatment:** Standard IVIg -- poor response
- **Efgartigimod protocol:** 800 mg (~16 mg/kg for a ~50 kg patient) x 4 doses
- **Response:** Safe, effective, shortened disease course

> **Citation:** BMC Neurology. Efgartigimod for treating Guillain-Barre syndrome with poor response to intravenous immunoglobulin: a case report. *BMC Neurol*. 2025. DOI: 10.1186/s12883-025-04412-0

#### Case 3: Chen et al., 2025 (*Therapeutic Advances in Neurological Disorders*)
- **Patients:** 3 patients with different GBS subtypes
- **Key findings:**
  - Case 1: After 3 weekly doses of efgartigimod (10 mg/kg), independent ambulation achieved 19 days post-onset. Serum ganglioside antibody titers and total IgG levels decreased.
  - Case 2: After 3 doses, symptom improvement within 1 month, IgG antibody reduction.
  - Case 3: 27-year-old male, MFS-GBS overlap, initially unresponsive to IVIg. After 2 doses of efgartigimod, significant improvement in ophthalmoplegia. Serum ganglioside antibodies eventually became undetectable.
- **Conclusion:** Efgartigimod effectively reduces anti-ganglioside antibody titers and shortens disease course.

> **Citation:** Chen S, Ou R, Wei Q, Zhao B, Chen X. Sequential administration of efgartigimod shortened the course of Guillain-Barre syndrome: a case series. *Ther Adv Neurol Disord*. 2025;18:17562864251314746. DOI: 10.1177/17562864251314746

#### Case 4: Deng et al., 2025 (*Frontiers in Immunology*)
- **Patients:** 4 patients with different acute GBS subtypes
- **Key finding:** 2 patients received efgartigimod **monotherapy** without IVIg, PE, or glucocorticoids
- **Response:** Favorable outcomes in all 4 patients
- **Significance:** First demonstration that efgartigimod monotherapy may be sufficient in some GBS patients

> **Citation:** Deng M, Kong Z, Wang Y, Wang X, Li T. Efgartigimod in the treatment of Guillain-Barre syndrome: case report. *Front Immunol*. 2025;16:1586663. DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1586663

#### Case 5: Cheng et al., 2026 (*Scientific Reports*)
- **Study design:** Retrospective analysis of 17 GBS patients (5 efgartigimod, 8 IVIg, 4 PE)
- **Key results:**
  - Efgartigimod: 1-point GBS-DS improvement in median **4.0 days** vs IVIg 7.0 days vs PE 11.5 days (p=0.033)
  - At 1 week: 80% of efgartigimod patients had INCAT <=2 vs 12.5% IVIg vs 25% PE (p=0.039)
  - Full MRC scores at 1 week: 80% efgartigimod vs 12.5% IVIg (p=0.045)
- **Adverse events:** Mild in 20% (efgartigimod) vs 50% (PE)
- **Conclusion:** Efgartigimod offers more rapid symptom relief and functional recovery compared to IVIg and PE

> **Citation:** Cheng Y, Li W, Xie S, et al. Rapid neurological recovery in Guillain-Barre syndrome treated with efgartigimod. *Sci Rep*. 2026;16:44163. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-44163-7

### 2.6 Phase 2 RCT in Progress

argenx is conducting a **Phase 2 randomized controlled trial** comparing efgartigimod to IVIg in GBS. Details are limited in the public domain as of March 2026, but this trial (noted in the 2025 Refractory GBS research document) could fundamentally change GBS treatment if positive.

### 2.7 Efgartigimod Dosing Protocol for GBS

Based on all published case reports:

| Parameter | Recommended |
|-----------|-------------|
| Dose | 10 mg/kg IV |
| Frequency | Weekly |
| Number of doses | 4 (may extend based on response) |
| Infusion duration | 1 hour |
| Loading dose | Not required |
| Route | IV (SC formulation with hyaluronidase [PH20] available but not tested in GBS) |

**Monitoring during treatment:**
- Serum total IgG: before first dose, then weekly (pre-infusion trough)
- Complete blood count
- Liver function tests
- Anti-ganglioside antibody titers (if available)
- Clinical: MRC sum score, GBS disability score, respiratory function

## 3. IMLIFIDASE AFTER PE

### 3.1 Mechanism: What Makes Imlifidase Unique

Imlifidase (Idefirix, Hansa Biopharma) is a cysteine protease derived from *Streptococcus pyogenes* that **enzymatically cleaves IgG** in a two-step process:

1. **First cleavage** (rapid, minutes to hours): One heavy chain is cleaved at the lower hinge region, creating "single-cleaved IgG" (scIgG)
2. **Second cleavage** (slower, hours): The second heavy chain is cleaved, yielding one F(ab')2 fragment and one Fc homodimer

**Key properties:**
- **Speed:** Complete IgG cleavage within **2-6 hours** of a single IV infusion
- **Specificity:** Cleaves ONLY IgG -- does NOT affect IgM, IgA, IgE, complement factors, clotting factors, fibrinogen, albumin, or any other plasma protein
- **Dose:** 0.25 mg/kg IV (single dose in GBS trial)
- **Duration:** Intact IgG remains absent for at least **7 days** after administration
- **IgG rebounds** around days 6-12 as new IgG is synthesized

### 3.2 Can Imlifidase Follow PE? Contraindications?

**Yes, imlifidase can follow PE. There are no specific contraindications to this sequence.**

**Advantages of imlifidase after PE:**

1. **Complementary mechanism:** PE removes IgG (and other proteins) by physical separation; imlifidase cleaves remaining/rebounding IgG enzymatically
2. **Speed after PE depletion:** If PE has reduced IgG to 16-20% of baseline, imlifidase can rapidly eliminate the remaining IgG that PE did not remove, achieving near-complete IgG ablation
3. **Critical advantage -- NO coagulopathy risk:**
   - PE depletes clotting factors (fibrinogen, factors V, VIII, XIII) proportionally with each exchange, potentially causing coagulopathy
   - Imlifidase cleaves ONLY IgG and does not affect any clotting factors
   - **For a patient developing coagulopathy from PE, switching to imlifidase for residual IgG elimination avoids further coagulation depletion**
4. **No complement depletion:** PE removes complement components C3, C4, C1q (35-40% per session); imlifidase does not affect complement
5. **No hemodynamic stress:** Unlike PE, imlifidase is a brief IV infusion without large-volume fluid shifts

### 3.3 Timing Considerations

- Imlifidase can be given **within 24 hours** of the last PE session
- No specific waiting period is required
- If PE was recently performed, IgG levels are already low; imlifidase will cleave whatever remains
- Consider measuring IgG level before imlifidase to document starting point

### 3.4 Imlifidase + IVIg Combination (The Phase 2 Trial Protocol)

The Phase 2 15-HMedIdeS-09 trial used imlifidase + IVIg in combination:

**Protocol:**
- Single dose of imlifidase 0.25 mg/kg IV
- Followed by standard IVIg (0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days)
- The rationale: imlifidase rapidly clears pathogenic IgG autoantibodies, then IVIg provides immunomodulation

**Results (n=30, open-label, severe GBS patients):**
- 37% independently walking by Week 1
- Mean MRC sum score improvement of 10.7 points by Week 1
- Median time to independently walk: **16 days**
- Median time to improve by >=1 GBS-DS grade: **6 days**
- By Week 8: 67% walking independently, 40.7% able to run
- At 6 months: 63% could run or had no functional disability (GBS-DS <=1)
- **Comparative analysis:** Patients returned to independent walking **6 weeks sooner** compared to IGOS real-world IVIg-only patients

**Safety:** Well tolerated. No new safety signals.

> **Citation:** Hansa Biopharma. Positive full results from 15-HMedIdeS-09 Phase 2 study. Press release, December 2024. Available at: https://www.hansabiopharma.com/media/press-releases/2024/

> **Citation:** Hansa Biopharma. Data at the 2025 PNS Annual Meeting. Press release, May 2025. Available at: https://www.hansabiopharma.com/media/press-releases/2025/

### 3.5 Can Imlifidase Be Combined with Other Therapies?

| Combination | Feasibility | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Imlifidase + IVIg | **YES (tested in Phase 2)** | Imlifidase clears pathogenic IgG first, then IVIg provides immunomodulation. The IVIg should be given AFTER imlifidase has cleared the pathogenic antibodies (within hours) |
| Imlifidase after PE | **YES (no contraindication)** | Complementary mechanisms; imlifidase eliminates residual IgG that PE missed |
| Imlifidase + efgartigimod | **Theoretically possible but untested** | Both reduce IgG by different mechanisms. Could achieve very deep IgG depletion. Risk of severe hypogammaglobulinemia and infection |
| Imlifidase + tanruprubart | **Theoretically possible but untested** | Different targets (IgG vs C1q). No mechanistic conflict |
| Imlifidase + rituximab | **Theoretically synergistic** | Imlifidase clears existing IgG; rituximab prevents production of new pathogenic antibodies by depleting B cells |

**IMPORTANT: Imlifidase IgG cleavage also affects therapeutic monoclonal antibodies.** Any IgG-based monoclonal antibody (rituximab, efgartigimod, tanruprubart) given before imlifidase will be cleaved. Therefore, **imlifidase should be given FIRST**, and any monoclonal antibody therapy should follow after the imlifidase effect wanes (>7 days).

### 3.6 Availability and Access

- **EU conditional approval (Idefirix):** For kidney transplant desensitization only
- **FDA BLA submitted:** January 2026 for kidney transplant; PDUFA date December 19, 2026
- **NOT approved for GBS** in any jurisdiction
- **Compassionate use for GBS:** Hansa Biopharma has stated they are "unable to offer a compassionate use program at this time" as it could jeopardize ongoing clinical trials
- **Phase 3 GBS trial:** Not yet publicly announced; Phase 2 data are being used to plan next steps
- **Hansa Biopharma is based in Lund, Sweden** -- proximity may facilitate academic/clinical collaboration

> **Citation:** Hansa Biopharma. PDUFA Action Date for Imlifidase BLA Set for December 19, 2026. Press release, 2026.

## 4. TANRUPRUBART (Anti-C1q) SEQUENCING

### 4.1 Mechanism of Action

Tanruprubart (ANX005, Annexon Biosciences) is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds C1q, the initiating molecule of the classical complement cascade. By blocking C1q, it prevents:
- C1q binding to antibody-antigen complexes on nerve surfaces
- Activation of the classical complement cascade (C1 --> C4 --> C2 --> C3 --> MAC)
- Formation of the membrane attack complex (MAC) that damages myelin and axons

**Dosing:** Single 30 mg/kg IV infusion (the 75 mg/kg dose was NOT superior in Phase 3)
**Onset:** Near-immediate complement inhibition following infusion

### 4.2 Can Tanruprubart Be Given After PE?

**This is problematic due to trial design, not pharmacology.**

**Pharmacological considerations:**
- Tanruprubart targets C1q, a complement protein
- PE removes complement components temporarily (C3, C4 drop 35-40% per session but recover to baseline within **24 hours**)
- C1q specifically recovers rapidly after PE (within 24-48 hours)
- Therefore, from a mechanistic standpoint, tanruprubart COULD be given after PE once complement levels have recovered (24-48 hours)

**Clinical trial exclusion issue:**
- The Phase 3 pivotal trial (NCT04701164) studied tanruprubart as **monotherapy** -- patients did NOT receive IVIg or PE
- The FORWARD study (NCT07020819, ongoing) **explicitly excludes** patients who have received previous or intended treatment with either PE or IVIg for GBS
- This means there is **NO clinical data** on tanruprubart after prior PE or IVIg
- The trial exclusion criteria were chosen to demonstrate tanruprubart's standalone efficacy, not because of a pharmacological contraindication

**In theory:** If tanruprubart becomes available (FDA/EMA approval expected mid-2026 to 2027), using it after PE failure would require off-label use without supporting data from the registration trial.

### 4.3 Phase 3 Trial Results

**NCT04701164 (Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled, n=242):**
- Patients randomized 1:1:1 to tanruprubart 30 mg/kg, 75 mg/kg, or placebo
- **Primary endpoint met** for 30 mg/kg: OR 2.4 (95% CI 1.29-4.50; p=0.0058) for improved GBS-DS at Week 8
- Patients walked independently **31 days earlier** (p=0.0211)
- **28 fewer days** on mechanical ventilation (p=0.0356)
- Patients were **14 times more likely** to show improved mobility at Week 1 vs placebo
- Quality of life improvements sustained through Week 24
- Infusion-related reactions in 35% (transient)
- The 75 mg/kg dose did NOT show superiority over 30 mg/kg

> **Citation:** NeurologyLive. Complement-Targeting Agent ANX005 Demonstrates Therapeutic Efficacy in Phase 3 Study of Guillain-Barre Syndrome. 2025. https://www.neurologylive.com/view/complement-targeting-agent-anx005-demonstrates-therapeutic-efficacy-phase-3-study-guillain-barr-syndrome

> **Citation:** Neurology. Efficacy and Safety of Targeted Immunotherapy with ANX005 in Treating Guillain-Barre Syndrome: A Phase 3 Multicenter Study (PL5.006). 2025. DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000212033

### 4.4 Does PE Affect Complement Levels That Tanruprubart Targets?

**PE temporarily depletes C1q, but it recovers rapidly.**

| Complement Component | Depletion per PE Session | Recovery to Baseline |
|----------------------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| C1q | ~35-40% | **24-48 hours** |
| C3 | ~35-40% | **24 hours** |
| C4 | ~35-40% | **24 hours** |
| Total hemolytic complement (CH50) | ~35-40% | **24 hours** |

**Key implication:** PE does not create a sustained complement-depleted state. Unlike IgG (which takes 12 weeks to recover fully), complement components are rapidly resynthesized. This means:

- Tanruprubart given 48+ hours after PE would encounter normal or near-normal C1q levels to bind
- The target for tanruprubart is not depleted long-term by PE
- However, there remains NO clinical evidence supporting this sequence

> **Citation:** Schein PF, et al. Changes in coagulation factors, complement, immunoglobulins, and immune complex concentrations with plasma exchange. *Transfusion*. 1982;22(1):54-58.

> **Citation:** Sutton DMC, et al. The effect of serial therapeutic plasmapheresis on platelet count, coagulation factors, plasma immunoglobulin, and complement levels. *J Clin Apher*. 1986;3(2):124-128.

### 4.5 Regulatory Status and Access

- **FDA:** Fast Track and Orphan Drug designations. BLA submission anticipated mid-2026. Potential approval mid-2026 to early 2027.
- **EMA:** MAA submitted January 2026. Orphan Drug designation. Decision expected late 2026 to 2027.
- **FORWARD study** (open-label, US/Canada/Europe): Currently enrolling, but excludes patients who have received PE or IVIg
- **Compassionate use:** No formal program announced by Annexon. In theory, eligible for Article 83 compassionate use request in EU since MAA has been submitted.

## 5. DECISION ALGORITHM FOR TREATMENT SEQUENCING

### 5.1 When PE Fails: What Should Come Next?

**There is no guideline-endorsed algorithm for PE-refractory GBS.** The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline explicitly identifies this as a gap: "No specific recommendation exists for what to do when either first-line treatment fails completely."

The following decision framework is derived from available evidence, mechanism of action analysis, and expert opinion:

### 5.3 How to Decide Between IVIg, Efgartigimod, Imlifidase, and Tanruprubart

| Factor | IVIg (IgA-depleted) | Efgartigimod | Imlifidase | Tanruprubart |
|--------|---------------------|--------------|------------|--------------|
| **IgA deficiency safety** | Requires IgA-depleted product; residual risk | **SAFE** (no IgA content) | **SAFE** (no IgA content) | **SAFE** (no IgA content) |
| **Prior PE compatibility** | Wait >48h after last PE | Wait 24-48h | No waiting needed | Wait 24-48h (for C1q recovery) |
| **Coagulopathy concern** | No impact on coagulation | No impact | **ADVANTAGE: No coagulation depletion** | No impact |
| **Speed of action** | Days to weeks | Hours to days (first dose) | **2-6 hours** (fastest) | Hours (complement block is immediate) |
| **Level of evidence in GBS** | Level 1 (as first-line); Level 4-5 after PE failure | Level 4 (case reports/series) | Level 2 (Phase 2 open-label) | Level 1 (Phase 3 RCT) -- but only as monotherapy, not after PE |
| **Availability in Sweden (March 2026)** | Available (request IgA-depleted product) | Available off-label (approved for MG) | Available for transplant (Idefirix); NOT approved for GBS | **NOT available** (EMA review ongoing) |
| **Cost/Access barriers** | Low (standard product) | Moderate (off-label) | High (off-label, no compassionate use for GBS) | Very high (not yet approved) |

### 5.4 What Lab Values Guide the Decision?

**Essential labs before second-line therapy:**

| Lab Test | Purpose | Action Threshold |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| **Total serum IgG** | Quantify PE-induced depletion; guide IVIg vs efgartigimod choice | If <2 g/L: severe depletion; efgartigimod may push too low; IVIg preferred if safe. If 3-5 g/L: efgartigimod feasible |
| **IgG subclasses** (1-4) | Identify which subclasses are most depleted | IgG1 and IgG4 slowest to recover after PE |
| **Anti-IgA antibodies** (IgG and IgE class) | Determine IVIg safety | IgE anti-IgA positive: IVIg CONTRAINDICATED even with IgA-depleted product. IgG anti-IgA only: IgA-depleted IVIg may be used cautiously |
| **Anti-ganglioside antibodies** (GM1, GD1a, GQ1b panel) | Confirm antibody-mediated pathology; monitor response | If positive: supports efgartigimod or imlifidase. If negative: complement pathway (tanruprubart) may be more relevant |
| **Complement C3, C4, CH50** | Assess complement consumption; guide tanruprubart | Low levels suggest active complement-mediated damage -- tanruprubart may be most targeted |
| **C1q level** | Specific target for tanruprubart | If depleted by PE, wait for recovery before tanruprubart |
| **Fibrinogen** | Assess coagulopathy from PE | <1.5 g/L: significant coagulopathy; AVOID further PE; imlifidase preferred for IgG clearance |
| **PT/INR, aPTT** | Coagulation status | Prolonged: document PE-induced coagulopathy |
| **Factor V, VIII levels** | Specific clotting factor depletion from PE | If depleted: supports switching from PE to non-PE modality |
| **CBC with differential** | Infection risk assessment | Lymphopenia may increase infection risk with further immunosuppression |
| **CRP, procalcitonin** | Rule out infection before immunosuppression | Elevated: investigate infection before adding immunotherapy |

### 5.5 2023 EAN/PNS Guideline Key Recommendations for Refractory GBS

The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline (van Doorn et al., *Eur J Neurol* 2023) provides the following relevant recommendations:

1. **Strong recommendation AGAINST** a second IVIg course in poor-prognosis GBS (based on SID-GBS)
2. **Strong recommendation AGAINST** combined PE followed by IVIg as planned strategy
3. **Good Practice Point:** IVIg may be effective up to 2-4 weeks from onset of weakness
4. **For TRF (treatment-related fluctuation):** A repeated course of IVIg or PE CAN be considered
5. **If no improvement and >8 weeks:** Consider reclassification to A-CIDP, which has different treatment algorithms (including rituximab, cyclophosphamide, maintenance IVIg or PE)
6. **CRITICAL GAP:** No recommendation for what to do when either first-line therapy completely fails in acute GBS

**The guideline explicitly does NOT address:**
- Efgartigimod for GBS
- Imlifidase for GBS
- Tanruprubart for GBS (Phase 3 results came after guideline publication)
- Any algorithm for PE-refractory GBS with IgA deficiency

> **Citation:** van Doorn PA, Van den Bergh PYK, Hadden RDM, et al. European Academy of Neurology/Peripheral Nerve Society Guideline on diagnosis and treatment of Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Eur J Neurol*. 2023;30(12):3646-3674. DOI: 10.1111/ene.16073

## 6. IgG LEVEL MONITORING PROTOCOL

### 6.1 When to Measure IgG During Sequential Therapy

| Timepoint | What to Measure | Why |
|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| Before first PE session | Total IgG, IgG subclasses, IgA, IgM | Establish baseline |
| After last PE session (24-48h) | Total IgG | Document degree of depletion |
| Before starting second-line therapy | Total IgG, complement (C3, C4, CH50), fibrinogen | Guide choice of second-line agent |
| During efgartigimod (weekly, pre-infusion) | Total IgG | Ensure IgG does not drop below 1-2 g/L (infection risk) |
| After imlifidase (6h, 24h, Day 7) | Total IgG, F(ab')2 fragments if available | Confirm complete cleavage; monitor rebound |
| After IVIg (24-48h post-completion) | Total IgG | Confirm IVIg has raised levels; guide further dosing |
| Weekly during recovery | Total IgG | Monitor natural recovery; assess need for IgG replacement |

### 6.2 Target IgG Levels

| Situation | Target IgG | Rationale |
|-----------|------------|-----------|
| After PE, before second-line therapy | Document level; no target | Expected to be 1-3 g/L; informs decision |
| During efgartigimod | >1.0 g/L trough | Below 1.0 g/L: significant infection risk; may need to hold dose |
| After imlifidase | Expected to be <0.1 g/L for 7 days | Acceptable for short duration; provide IVIg after to restore |
| Post-IVIg | >7 g/L (approximate normal) | Confirms adequate IVIg dosing |
| During recovery (>4 weeks) | >5 g/L | May need IgG replacement if persistently low |

### 6.3 Infection Prophylaxis During Deep IgG Depletion

When IgG levels drop below 2 g/L (from PE, efgartigimod, or imlifidase):
- Strict infection control measures
- Consider prophylactic antibiotics (institution-dependent)
- Monitor for signs of infection daily
- Low threshold for blood cultures and empiric antibiotics if febrile
- Avoid invasive procedures if possible
- Meningococcal vaccination/prophylaxis if complement inhibitors used (tanruprubart)
