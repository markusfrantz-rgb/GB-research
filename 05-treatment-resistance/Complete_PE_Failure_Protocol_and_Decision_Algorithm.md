## 1. DEFINITION OF PE FAILURE AFTER FULL 5-SESSION COURSE

### 1.1 The Problem: No Consensus Definition Exists

There is **no universally agreed-upon definition** of treatment failure after a complete PE course in GBS. This represents a significant gap in clinical guidelines, including the 2023 EAN/PNS guideline, which provides recommendations against combining treatments but does not explicitly define when PE has "failed" (Doorn et al., Eur J Neurol, 2023).

### 1.2 Working Definition of PE Failure

Based on available evidence, PE failure can be practically defined as:

**Primary failure (non-response):**
- Patient remains at Hughes grade >= 4 (bedridden/wheelchair-bound) or grade 5 (ventilator-dependent) AND
- No improvement of >= 1 grade on the GBS disability scale OR no improvement of > 5 points on MRC sum score AND
- Assessment performed **at least 2 weeks** after completing the 5th PE session AND
- Alternative diagnoses have been considered and excluded

**Important distinctions:**

| Category | Definition | Clinical Action |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Non-responder** | No improvement at all after full course | Reassess diagnosis; consider next-line therapy |
| **Partial responder** | Some improvement but still severely affected (Hughes >= 4) | May still improve; reassess at 4 weeks |
| **Treatment-related fluctuation (TRF)** | Initial improvement followed by worsening within 2 months | Consider repeat PE or switch modality |
| **Progressing despite treatment** | Continuing to worsen during or after PE | Urgent reassessment; consider diagnosis error or hyperacute variant |

> **Citation:** Alessandro L, et al. Treatment-related fluctuations in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Arq Neuropsiquiatr*. 2022;80(5):516-522.

### 1.3 Why "Failure" Is Hard to Define: The Time Factor

PE's effect in GBS is to **accelerate recovery rather than fundamentally change the final outcome**. The Cochrane review of PE in GBS (Chevret et al., 2017; PMID: 28241090) *[Level 1 — Cochrane systematic review]* demonstrated:

- PE significantly reduced **time to improve one disability grade** (median 16.5 days PE vs longer for supportive care)
- PE significantly reduced **time to walk unaided**
- PE reduced the proportion unable to walk at 4 weeks and at 6 months
- However, PE does NOT guarantee improvement in all patients

**Therefore, "PE failure" at day 7 post-completion is premature. "PE failure" at week 4 is meaningful.**

> **Citation:** Chevret S, Hughes RA, Annane D. Plasma exchange for Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*. 2017;2:CD001798.

## 2. EXPECTED IMPROVEMENT TIMELINE AFTER PE

### 2.1 When Clinical Improvement Typically Begins

| Time Point | Expected Response | Assessment |
|------------|-------------------|------------|
| **During PE (sessions 2-5)** | Some patients show initial improvement; most do not yet | Too early to assess |
| **Day 1-7 after session 5** | Nadir may not yet be reached; ongoing deterioration possible | Too early to declare failure |
| **Day 7-14 after session 5** | Earliest meaningful assessment point; clinical plateau or initial improvement expected | First reliable assessment window |
| **Week 2-4 after session 5** | Improvement should be detectable if PE was effective | Key decision point |
| **Week 4 (from symptom onset)** | mEGOS prediction target; most responders show improvement by now | Formal failure assessment |
| **Week 8** | Maximum expected benefit from acute treatment | Late responders may still improve |

### 2.2 Data from Major Trials

**North American GBS Study Group (1985, 245 patients):** *[Level 1 — RCT]*
- Median time to improve 1 grade: 19 days (PE) vs 40 days (supportive care)
- At 4 weeks: 59% of PE patients improved >= 1 grade vs 39% supportive care

**French Cooperative Group (1997, 556 patients):** *[Level 1 — RCT]*
- At 1 month: 71% of PE patients improved >= 1 grade
- Severe patients (unable to walk): 4 PE sessions superior to 2

**Key finding:** If a patient has not improved by **4 weeks** after completing PE (approximately 6 weeks from symptom onset), the probability of a treatment-attributable response is low, and the clinical picture should be reassessed.

> **Citation:** French Cooperative Group on Plasma Exchange in Guillain-Barre syndrome. Efficiency of plasma exchange in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Ann Neurol*. 1987;22:753-761.

### 2.3 How Long to Wait After Session 5 Before Declaring Failure

**Recommended waiting period:**

```
SESSION 5 COMPLETED
       |
       v
[Day 0-7] -- Too early to assess. Allow physiological recovery.
       |
       v
[Day 7-14] -- First clinical assessment. Look for ANY sign of improvement:
              - Slight increase in MRC sum score (even 1-2 points)
              - Return of any reflexes
              - Improved respiratory mechanics (FVC trend)
              - Subjective improvement reported by patient
              - NfL trend (if serial measurements available)
       |
       v
[Day 14-21] -- If NO improvement at all: reassess diagnosis.
               Consider electrophysiology (NCS/EMG), ganglioside Ab,
               nodal/paranodal Ab, repeat LP.
       |
       v
[Day 21-28] -- FORMAL FAILURE DECLARATION if:
               - No improvement >= 1 Hughes grade, AND
               - No improvement > 5 MRC sum score points, AND
               - Diagnosis confirmed as GBS (not A-CIDP or nodopathy)
       |
       v
[INITIATE NEXT-LINE THERAPY]
```

**Critical caveat:** In a deeply sedated, ventilated patient (as Madeleine is), clinical assessment is severely limited. **Biomarker-based assessment becomes essential** (see Section 5).

## 3. DECISION ALGORITHM AFTER 5 PE SESSIONS FAIL

### 3.1 Step 1: Reassess Diagnosis (Critical First Step)

Before declaring PE failure, the following diagnostic reassessment MUST occur:

**Mandatory investigations if not already done:**

| Investigation | Purpose | Findings That Change Diagnosis |
|--------------|---------|-------------------------------|
| **NCS/EMG** | Subtype classification, axonal vs demyelinating, prognosis | Demyelinating with conduction block suggests CIDP; pure sensory suggests alternative |
| **Ganglioside antibodies** (anti-GM1, GD1a, GQ1b, GT1a panel) | Subtype confirmation, targeted therapy potential | Specific patterns inform treatment choice |
| **Anti-neurofascin antibodies** (NF155, NF186, CNTN1) | Autoimmune nodopathy (masquerading as GBS) | Positive = rituximab-responsive, NOT IVIg/PE-responsive |
| **Repeat lumbar puncture** | Cell count, protein, cytology | >50 cells/mm3 = consider infectious, lymphomatous, sarcoid cause |
| **Anti-IgA antibodies** | Safety for future IVIg consideration | Positive = higher anaphylaxis risk with IVIg |
| **NfL (serum)** | Axonal damage quantification, trend | Rising = ongoing damage; falling = stabilization |
| **TSH, fT4, fT3** | Thyroid function during critical illness | Hypothyroidism impairs nerve recovery |
| **MRI spine (with contrast)** | Nerve root enhancement, alternative pathology | Enhancement suggests active inflammation; mass lesion changes diagnosis |

**Red flags for misdiagnosis:**

| Red Flag | Consider Instead |
|----------|-----------------|
| >= 3 treatment-related fluctuations | Acute-onset CIDP (A-CIDP) |
| Symptoms progressing beyond 8 weeks | A-CIDP |
| Prominent sensory ataxia, tremor | Anti-NF155 autoimmune nodopathy |
| Lack of preceding infection | Autoimmune nodopathy or A-CIDP |
| Asymmetric weakness | Vasculitic neuropathy, multifocal motor neuropathy |
| CSF pleocytosis (>10 cells) | HIV, Lyme, sarcoid, lymphoma |
| Normal NCS at presentation | Consider neuromuscular junction disorder |

> **Citation:** Bellanti R, Rinaldi S. Guillain-Barre syndrome: a comprehensive review. *Eur J Neurol*. 2024;31:e16365.
> **Citation:** Doorn PA, et al. EAN/PNS Guideline on diagnosis and treatment of GBS. *Eur J Neurol*. 2023;30:3646-3674.

**FOR A PATIENT WITH IgA DEFICIENCY WHO HAS FAILED PE:**

```
PRIORITY 1 (Most relevant IgA-safe option) [Level 4 — fallrapporter/fallserier]:
------------------------------------------
EFGARTIGIMOD (Vyvgart)
- 10 mg/kg IV weekly x 4 doses
- Can start 24-48 hours after last PE session
- Mechanism: FcRn blockade, accelerates IgG catabolism
- Evidence: 11 published GBS cases (7 publications, 2024-2026).
  Most were IVIg-refractory. Only 1 PE-refractory case (Ripellino 2025):
  fulminant GBS, day 30, improvement in upper limbs within hours,
  tracheostomy removed 1 week post-last dose, walked with aid 2 weeks later.

PRIORITY 2 (Strong Phase 2 data, Swedish company) [Level 2 — prospektiv fas 2]:

- Complete IgG cleavage within 2-6 hours
- Phase 2: independently walking 6 weeks sooner than IVIg-only
- IgA-SAFE: cleaves only IgG
- ADVANTAGE: does NOT cause coagulopathy (unlike PE)

PRIORITY 3 (Phase 3 positive, but access limited) [Level 1 — RCT fas 3, men ENBART monoterapi]:

-----------------------------------------

- 0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days, start >= 48 hours after last PE

- SID-GBS does NOT apply (that trial was 2nd IVIg, not 1st)
- REQUIRES: anti-IgA antibody testing BEFORE administration

PRIORITY 5 (Very limited evidence) [Level 4 — fallrapporter]:
------------------------------------
RITUXIMAB (off-label)
- Consider if anti-NF155/NF186 antibodies positive

- IgA-SAFE: monoclonal antibody
```

## 4. EFFECT OF INTERRUPTIONS IN PE COURSE ON EFFICACY

### 4.1 Standard PE Schedule

The standard protocol for PE in GBS is:
- **5 exchanges** (or 4 in moderate GBS; 6 not superior to 4 in severe GBS)
- **Over 7-14 days** (typically every other day)
- **1-1.5 plasma volumes per session** (approximately 40-50 mL/kg/session)
- Sessions spaced **every other day** to allow redistribution of pathogenic mediators from extravascular to intravascular compartment

> **Citation:** French Cooperative Group. Appropriate number of PE in GBS. *Ann Neurol*. 1997;41:298-306.

### 4.2 Madeleine's Scenario: Sessions 1-3, Pause, Sessions 4-5

**Scenario:** 3 PE sessions performed on days 1-4 (roughly every other day). Tracheostomy performed on day 5. Sessions 4-5 planned for days 6-7 (or as soon as clinically stable post-tracheostomy).

**Impact analysis:**

| Factor | Standard Protocol | Madeleine's Schedule | Impact |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Total sessions | 5 over 7-14 days | 5 over ~7-8 days | Equivalent |
| Spacing between sessions | Every 48h | ~48h gap for tracheostomy | Minimal impact |
| Gap length | None >48h | 1-2 extra days between session 3 and 4 | Unlikely to affect efficacy |
| Cumulative volume exchanged | 5-7.5 plasma volumes total | Same (if sessions 4-5 completed) | Equivalent |

### 4.3 Evidence on Interrupted PE Courses

**Direct evidence is extremely limited.** No RCT has specifically studied the effect of interruptions within a PE course on GBS outcomes. However, the following indirect evidence is informative:

1. **Pharmacokinetics of IgG redistribution:** After each PE session, IgG redistributes from the interstitial compartment to the intravascular space over approximately 24-48 hours (equilibration time). A 1-2 day pause between sessions 3 and 4 falls within this normal redistribution window and may actually **improve** the efficiency of session 4 by allowing more complete IgG redistribution.

2. **The French Cooperative Group data (1997):** Showed that 4 sessions were equivalent to 6 in severe GBS. The critical factor is **total volume exchanged**, not the precise schedule. As long as all 5 sessions are completed within the overall 14-day window, efficacy should be preserved.

3. **Analogy from other conditions:** In myasthenia gravis and TTP, PE courses are routinely interrupted for procedures, with no documented loss of efficacy from brief (1-3 day) gaps.

4. **Coagulopathy consideration:** A pause after session 3 allows partial recovery of clotting factors (fibrinogen, factor V, factor VIII), which may actually be **beneficial** for hemostasis during tracheostomy and for sessions 4-5.

### 4.4 Clinical Recommendation

**A 1-2 day pause between PE sessions 3 and 4 for tracheostomy is unlikely to reduce the efficacy of the overall PE course.** The key priorities are:

1. **Complete all 5 sessions** within 14 days of the first session
2. **Resume PE as soon as hemostasis permits** after tracheostomy (typically 24-48 hours post-procedure if no active bleeding and fibrinogen > 1.5 g/L)
3. **Check coagulation** (PT, fibrinogen, platelets) before sessions 4 and 5
4. **Replace fibrinogen** if < 1.5 g/L before the next PE session (cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate)

## 5. BIOMARKER-BASED TREATMENT RESPONSE ASSESSMENT DURING SEDATION

### 5.1 The Clinical Challenge

Madeleine is deeply sedated post-tracheostomy. Standard clinical assessments (MRC sum score, Hughes grade, grip strength, FVC) **cannot be reliably performed**. This creates a critical monitoring gap during a period when treatment decisions must be made.

### 5.2 Biomarkers That Can Substitute for Clinical Assessment

#### Serum Neurofilament Light Chain (NfL)

**The most valuable biomarker for monitoring during sedation.** *[Level 2 — prospektiv kohortstudie, IGOS]*

| Parameter | Value | Clinical Meaning |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| **Baseline (admission)** | Should be drawn ASAP if not done | Establishes starting point |
| **At 1 week** | Compare to baseline | Rising = ongoing axonal damage |
| **At 2 weeks** | Key prognostic value | sNfL > 85.5 pg/mL: 62% ICU transfer, 78 day median hospitalization |
| **At 4 weeks** | Strongest prognostic value | Week-4 sNfL: OR 2.79 for inability to walk at 6 months |
| **Peak timing** | Average 22.8 days from onset | Peak NfL does not mean peak clinical severity |
| **Falling trend** | Indicates axonal damage is stabilizing | Favorable sign even if absolute level is high |
| **Persistently rising** | Ongoing nerve destruction | Unfavorable; consider treatment switch |

**Clinical application in Madeleine's case:**
- Draw serum NfL NOW (day 4-5 from onset) as baseline
- Repeat at day 10-14 (2 weeks from onset) -- this is the key decision-making data point
- If NfL is rising between days 5 and 14: treatment is not halting nerve damage; escalate
- If NfL is stable or falling: biological evidence that the disease process is stabilizing, even if clinical improvement cannot yet be assessed

> **Citation:** van Tilburg SJ, et al. Dynamics and prognostic value of serum NfL in GBS. *eBioMedicine*. 2024;102:105072.
> **Citation:** Altmann P, et al. Increased serum NfL indicates poor outcome in GBS. *J Neuroinflammation*. 2020;17:86.

#### Total Serum IgG Levels

| Time Point | Expected | Interpretation |
|------------|----------|---------------|
| Pre-PE | Baseline (normal 7-16 g/L) | Document starting point |
| After 5 PE sessions | 16-20% of baseline (typically 1.5-3 g/L) | Expected depletion; confirms PE effectiveness |
| Rebound at 1-3 weeks | Gradual rise toward baseline | IgG recovery; if too rapid, pathogenic Ab also returning |
| After efgartigimod (if given) | Further reduction | Confirms drug effect |

**Clinical application:** IgG levels document that PE achieved its pharmacological goal (IgG depletion). If IgG was NOT adequately depleted, PE sessions may have been technically suboptimal.

#### Complement Markers (C3, C4, sC5b-9)

- **C3 levels** at baseline independently predict 1-month and 3-month disability
- Higher C3 = more complement substrate = worse prognosis
- Serial C3 changes parallel clinical course
- sC5b-9 has limited utility for monitoring (does not change significantly between acute and remission phases)
- C3 and C4 are depleted by PE (35-40% per session) but recover within 24 hours
- **Most useful for:** baseline prognostication and monitoring response to complement inhibitors (tanruprubart, if used)

> **Citation:** Kleyweg RP, et al. Serum C3 complement levels predict prognosis in GBS. *J Neurol Sci*. 2023;444:120510.

#### Anti-Ganglioside Antibody Titers

- Detectable in 40-60% of GBS patients
- **Serial monitoring has prognostic value:** *[Level 3 — retrospektiv analys]*
  - Persistently high anti-GM1 IgG titers (> 1,600) = slow/incomplete recovery
  - Falling titers = favorable response to treatment
  - HR = 2.11 for poor outcome with high vs low anti-GM1 IgG
- PE should reduce titers (physical removal of antibodies)
- **If titers remain high after 5 PE sessions:** suggests high antibody production rate; supports adding efgartigimod (FcRn blockade to accelerate IgG/antibody clearance)

> **Citation:** Thomma RCM, et al. High and persistent anti-GM1 antibody titers predict poor recovery in GBS. *Neurol Neuroimmunol Neuroinflamm*. 2023;10(3):e200107.

### 5.3 Biomarkers That CANNOT Substitute for Clinical Assessment

| Biomarker | Limitation |
|-----------|-----------|
| **CRP** | Non-specific; elevated by infection, surgery (tracheostomy), inflammation |
| **CSF protein** | Repeat LP not recommended for routine monitoring; invasive |
| **Procalcitonin** | Infection marker, not disease activity marker |
| **sC5b-9** | Does not change reliably between phases |
| **Cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha)** | Research tools; not clinically standardized |

### 5.4 Recommended Biomarker Monitoring Protocol During Sedation

```
BIOMARKER SCHEDULE FOR SEDATED GBS PATIENT POST-TRACHEOSTOMY:

IMMEDIATE (Day 0-1 post-tracheostomy):
- Serum NfL (baseline or second measurement)
- Total IgG
- Anti-ganglioside antibody panel (if not already sent)
- Anti-IgA antibodies (if not already sent)
- C3, C4
- CRP, procalcitonin (infection baseline)
- Coagulation panel (PT, fibrinogen, platelets) -- for PE safety

DAY 7 (1 week post-tracheostomy, ~2 weeks from onset):
- Serum NfL (KEY measurement -- compare to baseline)
- Total IgG (recovery from PE depletion)
- Anti-ganglioside titers (if positive at baseline)
- C3

DAY 14 (2 weeks post-tracheostomy, ~3 weeks from onset):
- Serum NfL (trend analysis: rising, stable, or falling)
- Total IgG
- Anti-ganglioside titers

DAY 21-28 (3-4 weeks from onset):
- Serum NfL (should be approaching peak, then declining)
- Full biomarker panel reassessment
- NCS/EMG (if not yet performed -- essential for prognosis)
```

## 6. NEUROLOGICAL ASSESSMENT AFTER TRACHEOSTOMY AND DEEP SEDATION

### 6.1 When to Lighten Sedation

**One of the key benefits of tracheostomy over endotracheal intubation is the ability to reduce sedation.** Published evidence shows:

- **Significant decrease in propofol and fentanyl utilization** from 24 hours pre-tracheostomy to 24 hours post-tracheostomy (Breen et al., Crit Care Med, 2022)
- **Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS), Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), and CAM-ICU scoring improve** as early as 24 hours post-tracheostomy
- Tracheostomy reduces the need for deep sedation because the tube is better tolerated and causes less gagging/discomfort

**Recommended sedation lightening protocol for GBS:**

| Timeframe | Action | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **0-6 hours post-tracheostomy** | Maintain current sedation | Surgical site stabilization |
| **6-12 hours** | Reduce propofol/midazolam by 25-50% | Tracheostomy better tolerated than ETT |
| **12-24 hours** | Target RASS -2 to -1 (light sedation) | Allow purposeful responses to stimulation |
| **24-48 hours** | Daily sedation interruption (sedation vacation) | Assess neurological status |
| **48+ hours** | Maintain lightest sedation compatible with comfort | Ongoing clinical assessment |

### 6.2 What Can Be Assessed and When

**IMPORTANT CAVEAT FOR DYSAUTONOMIC PATIENTS:** Sedation lightening MUST be done cautiously in patients with confirmed severe autonomic dysfunction, as withdrawal of sedation can trigger autonomic storms (tachycardia, hypertension, diaphoresis). Lighten gradually with hemodynamic monitoring.

| Assessment | When Possible | Minimum Sedation Level | Notes |
|------------|---------------|----------------------|-------|
| **Pupillary responses** | Immediately (even while sedated) | Any | Anisocoria already documented; monitor for changes |
| **Corneal reflex** | 12-24 hours | RASS -3 or lighter | Cranial nerve assessment |
| **Eye opening to command** | 24-48 hours | RASS -1 to 0 | Confirms cortical function |
| **Eye tracking** | 24-48 hours | RASS -1 to 0 | Madeleine's primary communication channel |
| **Purposeful movement** | 48-72 hours | RASS 0 | Even minimal: eye blink, shoulder shrug |
| **MRC testing (upper limbs)** | 48-72 hours | RASS 0, cooperative | May detect proximal strength |
| **MRC testing (full)** | 72+ hours | RASS 0, alert | Full assessment possible |
| **FVC (via tracheostomy)** | 48-72 hours | RASS 0, cooperative | Attach spirometer to tracheostomy |

### 6.3 First Reliable Clinical Assessment Timepoint

**The first reliable clinical assessment of GBS severity and treatment response can typically occur 48-72 hours after tracheostomy**, assuming:

1. Sedation has been successfully lightened to RASS 0 (alert and calm)
2. No autonomic storm during lightening
3. Patient is hemodynamically stable
4. Residual effects of sedation agents have cleared (propofol: 30-60 min; midazolam: 2-6 hours; fentanyl: 1-2 hours for sedation reversal, though ICU patients with prolonged use may take 24-48 hours)

**In Madeleine's case,** with confirmed severe dysautonomia, the timeline may be longer (72-96 hours) because:
- Sedation lightening must be extremely gradual to avoid autonomic destabilization
- Autonomic storms triggered by sedation withdrawal would necessitate re-deepening sedation
- Her communication is limited to right eye blink (yes) and right shoulder movement (no) -- these can be assessed at relatively light sedation levels

### 6.4 Interim Assessment During Deep Sedation

Even while deeply sedated, the following GBS-relevant clinical observations provide information:

| Observation | Method | Significance |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| **Spontaneous limb movements** | Observation during care | Any movement = residual motor function |
| **Withdrawal to pain** | Gentle nailbed pressure | Tests spinal reflex arc + some motor function |
| **Deep tendon reflexes** | Standard testing | Present = better prognosis; absent = expected in GBS |
| **Plantar responses** | Babinski testing | Upgoing = unexpected in GBS (consider additional diagnosis) |
| **Respiratory drive** | Pressure support/CPAP trial | Any triggered breaths = diaphragm function present |
| **Cough reflex during suctioning** | Observation | Present = vagal function + some expiratory muscle function |
| **Pupil size and reactivity** | Pupillometry | Trends in autonomic function |

```
- FORMAL TREATMENT RESPONSE ASSESSMENT
- Full biomarker panel + clinical assessment

  IF no improvement (Hughes still 5, MRC unchanged):
    --> PE HAS FAILED
    --> START efgartigimod (if not already started)
    --> Request anti-IgA result to evaluate IVIg as backup
    --> Contact Hansa Biopharma re: imlifidase (academic collaboration)
    --> Contact Annexon re: tanruprubart (named patient program)

  IF some improvement but still severely affected:
    --> Partial response; may still improve
    --> Consider efgartigimod to consolidate response
    --> Reassess at week 6-8

  IF >= 3 fluctuations or progression beyond 8 weeks:
    --> Reclassify as A-CIDP
    --> Start chronic immunotherapy (IVIg maintenance or corticosteroids)
```
