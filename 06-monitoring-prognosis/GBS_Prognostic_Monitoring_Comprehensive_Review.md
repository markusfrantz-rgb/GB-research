## 1. BIOMARKERS FOR DISEASE MONITORING IN ACUTE GBS

### 1.1 Neurofilament Light Chain (NfL)

NfL is a structural protein of myelinated axons released into blood and CSF upon axonal damage. It has emerged as the most promising prognostic biomarker in GBS, reflecting the degree of acute axonal injury regardless of electrophysiological subtype. *[Level 2 — prospektiv kohortstudie, IGOS n=281]*

#### Serum Levels and Reference Values

- **Median sNfL at admission in GBS:** 85.5 pg/mL (IQR: 41.5-229.7 pg/mL)
- **Healthy controls:** 9.1 pg/mL (IQR: 6.9-11.2 pg/mL)
- **Elevation:** Median NfL at admission is approximately 5-fold higher than age-matched controls; individual patients range from normal to 100-fold increased levels
- **Normal reference range in adults (US population):** age-dependent; generally <10-20 pg/mL in those under 60

> **Citation:** Altmann P, et al. Increased serum neurofilament light chain concentration indicates poor outcome in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *J Neuroinflammation*. 2020;17(1):86. PMID: 32183837

#### Correlation with Axonal Damage and Disability

- **Correlation with Hughes Functional Score (HFS) at admission:** rs = 0.52 (p = 0.005)
- **Correlation with HFS at nadir:** rs = 0.59 (p = 0.001)
- **Correlation with HFS at discharge:** rs = 0.41 (p = 0.04)
- **Correlation with hospitalization duration:** rs = 0.69 (p < 0.0001)
- **Correlation with CSF albumin index:** rs = 0.57 (p = 0.002)
- High NfL is strongly associated with AMAN subtype and inexcitable nerves

> **Citation:** Altmann P, et al. *J Neuroinflammation*. 2020;17(1):86.

#### Prognostic Cutoffs and Outcome Prediction

Using the **median of 85.5 pg/mL** as a threshold:

| Parameter | sNfL <= 85.5 pg/mL | sNfL > 85.5 pg/mL |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Discharged with unimpaired walking (HFS=1) | **93%** | **54%** |
| Median hospitalization | 13.5 days | 78 days |
| ICU transfer rate | 14% | 62% |
| Median HFS at nadir | 2 | 5 |

**Odds ratios per two-fold sNfL increase:**
- Unfavorable discharge (HFS >= 2): OR = 2.71 (95% CI: 1.10-6.67, p = 0.03)
- ICU transfer: OR = 2.37 (95% CI: 1.14-4.95); AUROC for ICU transfer = 0.78 (95% CI: 0.60-0.96)

> **Citation:** Altmann P, et al. *J Neuroinflammation*. 2020;17(1):86.

#### Serial Monitoring and Dynamics (IGOS Cohort Data, 2024)

A large 2024 study (n = 281 patients) from the International GBS Outcome Study (IGOS) characterized serial NfL dynamics:

- **sNfL measured at:** baseline, 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 12 weeks
- **Average time from onset to sNfL peak:** 22.8 days
- **sNfL returns to normal:** after approximately 1 year
- **Week 2 sNfL and 4-week walking ability:** OR = 1.74 (95% CI: 1.27-2.45) for inability to walk unaided
- **Week 4 sNfL and 26-week walking ability:** OR = 2.79 (95% CI: 1.72-4.90) for inability to walk unaided
- **Highest quartile at week 2:** significantly longer time to walking recovery (p < 0.0001)
- **No significant difference** in sNfL dynamics between patients receiving a second IVIg course vs. placebo

**Clinical implication:** Serial sNfL measurement (at admission, 2 weeks, and 4 weeks) provides increasingly accurate prognostic information. The 2-week and 4-week values are stronger predictors than baseline.

> **Citation:** van Tilburg SJ, et al. Dynamics and prognostic value of serum neurofilament light chain in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *eBioMedicine*. 2024;102:105072. PMID: 38518653

#### NfL and Prognostic Models

A 2025 study demonstrated that adding NfL to existing clinical prognostic models (mEGOS) improved prediction of both respiratory failure and inability to walk in GBS.

> **Citation:** Vermeij FH, et al. Neurofilament light chain improves clinical prognostic models for Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Neurology*. 2025. PMID: 40316442

### 1.2 Anti-Ganglioside Antibodies

Anti-ganglioside antibodies (anti-GM1, anti-GD1a, anti-GQ1b, etc.) are found in 40-60% of GBS patients. Their role in serial monitoring is now better defined. *[Level 3 — retrospektiv kohortstudie]*

#### Prevalence and Subtypes

- **Anti-GM1 positive:** 20.7% (78/377) of GBS patients in a large Dutch cohort
  - IgG only: 38.5%
  - IgM only: 30.8%
  - Both IgG and IgM: 30.8%
- **Anti-GD1a:** strongly associated with AMAN subtype
- **Anti-GQ1b:** associated with Miller Fisher syndrome and Bickerstaff brainstem encephalitis

#### Titer Levels at Entry

- **Anti-GM1 IgG median titer:** 1,600 (IQR: 800-12,800; range: 100-51,200)
- **Anti-GM1 IgM median titer:** 200 (IQR: 100-1,200; range: 100-25,600)
- **High IgG titers (>1,600):** 61.5% also positive for IgM, more often associated with inexcitable nerves (p = 0.041)

#### Serial Monitoring: Persistence and Prognostic Value

**Antibody persistence rates:**

| Time Point | Still Positive | Persistent High IgG (>1,600) |
|------------|---------------|-------------------------------|
| 3 months | 62.8% (27/43) | 12.9% (4/31) |
| 6 months | 46.3% (19/41) | 17.2% (5/29) |

**Association with poor outcomes:**
- High IgG titers at entry: slower recovery (p = 0.015) and more frequent inexcitable nerves (p = 0.041)
- Consistently higher IgG titers in poor outcome group at 2 weeks (p < 0.001), 4 weeks (p = 0.013), and 3 months (p = 0.012)
- **Slow titer decline** associated with poor outcome at 4 weeks (p = 0.003) and 6 months (p = 0.032)
- **Persistent high IgG at 6 months** independently associated with inability to walk unaided (p = 0.004)
- **Independent prognostic factor:** High vs. low IgG titers: HR = 2.11 (95% CI: 1.015-4.388, p = 0.046) after correcting for known prognostic factors

**Effect of treatment on titers:**
- At 3 months, titers differed by treatment (p = 0.027): plasma exchange patients had highest titers; IVIg + methylprednisolone had lowest
- IgG1 and IgG3 subclasses are most frequent and pathogenic; IgG1 associated with more severe course

**Clinical recommendation:** Serial anti-ganglioside monitoring (at diagnosis, 2-4 weeks, 3 months, 6 months) has prognostic value. Persistent high anti-GM1 IgG titers (>1,600) predict slow or incomplete recovery.

> **Citation:** Thomma RCM, et al. High and persistent anti-GM1 antibody titers are associated with poor clinical recovery in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Neurol Neuroimmunol Neuroinflamm*. 2023;10(3):e200107. PMC10119811

### 1.3 Complement Markers (C3, C4, CH50, sC5b-9)

Complement activation via the classical pathway is central to GBS pathophysiology, particularly in axonal variants where autoantibodies bind gangliosides and fix complement at nodes of Ranvier.

#### C3 as a Prognostic Biomarker

A 2022 study of 76 GBS patients found:

- **Higher serum C3 (not C4) at baseline** was independently associated with poorer outcomes:
  - Lower MRC sum scores
  - Higher GBS disability scores (GBSDS)
  - Longer hospitalization
  - More frequent treatment-related fluctuations
- **Independent predictor:** Age, MRCSS at admission, and baseline serum C3 were significant independent indicators of 1-month and 3-month GBSDS
- **Longitudinal correlation:** Change in C3 levels coincided with clinical severity over the disease course
- **Mechanistic rationale:** C3 correlated positively with C3a (r = 0.32) and C5a (r = 0.37), indicating active complement consumption

**Note:** Higher C3 reflects more substrate available for complement activation (consumption generates C3a, C5a, and MAC), correlating with greater complement-mediated tissue damage.

> **Citation:** Kleyweg RP, et al. Serum C3 complement levels predict prognosis and monitor disease activity in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *J Neurol Sci*. 2023;444:120510. PMID: 36462224

#### sC5b-9 (Soluble Membrane Attack Complex)

- sC5b-9 levels in GBS patients did not change significantly between acute and remission phases in one study
- **Ba and CFI remained elevated** in remission compared to healthy controls, suggesting persistent low-grade complement activation
- sC5b-9 may have limited utility as a phase-differentiating marker

#### Clinical Application

C3 levels are readily available in standard clinical labs and can be used for:
1. Baseline prognostication (higher C3 predicts worse outcome)
2. Serial monitoring of disease activity (changes parallel clinical course)
3. Potential monitoring of complement inhibitor therapy (e.g., ANX005)

### 1.4 CSF Protein

#### Diagnostic vs. Monitoring Value

- **Albuminocytologic dissociation (ACD)** is present in 70% of GBS patients overall
- ACD rate increases with time from onset to lumbar puncture: 57% at <= 4 days; 84% at > 4 days
- **High CSF protein** is associated with demyelinating subtype, proximal/global weakness, and reduced walking ability at weeks 2 and 4
- Elevated or rising CSF protein with <= 10 mononuclear cells/mm3 strongly supports the diagnosis
- CSF protein correlates with serum NfL (rs = 0.48, p = 0.016)

#### Role of Serial LP in Monitoring

- **Repeat lumbar puncture is generally NOT recommended** for routine monitoring in established GBS
- A repeat LP may be useful when:
  - Diagnosis is uncertain (e.g., cell count elevated suggesting alternative diagnosis)
  - Clinical deterioration raises concern for superimposed infection (e.g., meningitis)
  - Differentiating GBS from A-CIDP in patients with > 8 weeks of symptoms
- Normal protein at first LP does not exclude GBS (may not rise until 1-2 weeks after onset)
- Age-adjusted reference limits should be used, as older patients have higher normal CSF protein

> **Citation:** Geurts YM, et al. CSF findings in relation to clinical characteristics, subtype, and disease course in patients with Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Neurology*. 2023;101(5):e488-e498.

### 1.5 Emerging Biomarkers (2024-2026)

#### Cytokine Profiles

Recent proteomics studies have identified multiple cytokines elevated in GBS:

- **Pro-inflammatory cytokines significantly elevated in acute GBS:** TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, IL-1beta, IL-6, IL-17, IL-23, IL-8
- **Anti-inflammatory:** IL-4, IL-10 also elevated
- **Novel findings (2023):** Extensive cytokine biomarker analysis identified 61 upregulated and 6 downregulated proteins in GBS serum
  - **CD23** validated as diagnostic marker: sensitivity 0.818, specificity 0.800, AUC 0.824
  - **CCL20 and IL-5** identified as potential independent predictors for GBS diagnosis
  - **IL-9** significantly elevated (p < 0.01)
- **IL-6:** stands out as a near-term candidate for clinical integration due to availability in standard labs; elevated IL-6 may identify patients likely to benefit from targeted immunotherapy

> **Citation:** Bu X, et al. Extensive cytokine biomarker analysis in serum of Guillain-Barre syndrome patients. *Sci Rep*. 2023;13:8585. PMC10205034

#### NfL Combined with Cytokine Panels

A 2025 narrative review highlights the synergistic prognostic value of combining NfL (reflecting axonal damage) with cytokine profiles (reflecting inflammatory activity) for a more comprehensive GBS biomarker panel.

> **Citation:** New insights into neurofilament and cytokine biomarkers. *J Neuroimmunol*. 2025.

## 2. CLINICAL SCALES FOR MONITORING GBS PROGRESSION AND RECOVERY

### 2.1 GBS Disability Scale (Hughes Functional Grading Scale)

The most widely used outcome measure in GBS clinical trials and practice.

| Grade | Description | Clinical Significance |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| **0** | Normal | Full recovery |
| **1** | Minor symptoms, able to run | Good outcome |
| **2** | Able to walk 10 m without assistance, unable to run | Good outcome (independent) |
| **3** | Able to walk 10 m with assistance | Poor outcome (dependent) |
| **4** | Bedridden or chair-bound | Poor outcome |
| **5** | Requiring assisted ventilation (part or full day) | Severe - ICU |
| **6** | Dead | |

**Clinical application:**
- Outcomes typically dichotomized: **Good (0-2)** vs. **Poor (3-6)**
- Primary outcome measure in most GBS trials
- Assessed at: admission, nadir, weekly during acute phase, then at 4 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year
- **Meaningful change:** >= 1 grade change is considered clinically significant

> **Citation:** Hughes RA, et al. Immunotherapy for Guillain-Barre syndrome: a systematic review. *Brain*. 2007;130:2245-2257.

---

### 2.2 MRC Sum Score

#### Assessment Technique

**12 muscles tested** (6 muscle groups bilaterally):

| Muscle Group | Upper Limb | Lower Limb |
|-------------|------------|------------|
| Proximal | Shoulder abduction | Hip flexion |
| Middle | Elbow flexion | Knee extension |
| Distal | Wrist extension | Ankle dorsiflexion |

**MRC grading per muscle:**

| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0 | No contraction |
| 1 | Flicker of contraction |
| 2 | Active movement with gravity eliminated |
| 3 | Active movement against gravity |
| 4 | Active movement against gravity and resistance |
| 5 | Normal power |

**Total score range:** 0 (quadriplegic) to 60 (normal)

#### Interpretation and Thresholds

| MRC Sum Score | Interpretation |
|---------------|---------------|
| 60 | Normal |
| 48-59 | Mild weakness |
| < 48 | Significant weakness (ICU-acquired weakness threshold) |
| < 40 | Severe weakness; key mEGOS threshold |
| < 36 | Very severe weakness |
| 0-30 | Near-complete paralysis |

#### Meaningful Change Thresholds

- **> 5 points change** is the accepted threshold for clinically meaningful change in GBS (used in TRF definition)
- Improvement of > 5 MRC sum score points corresponds roughly to 1 grade improvement on the Hughes scale
- **Assessment frequency:** At admission, daily in rapidly progressing patients, then at least weekly until stable, then at standard outcome time points

> **Citation:** Kleyweg RP, et al. The natural history of the Guillain-Barre syndrome in 18 children and 50 adults. *J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry*. 1991;54:1064-1067.

---

### 2.3 Modified Erasmus GBS Outcome Score (mEGOS)

Predicts the probability of being unable to walk independently at 4 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months.

#### Scoring System

**At hospital admission (score range 0-9):**

| Variable | Category | Points |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Age at onset** | <= 40 years | 0 |
| | 41-60 years | 1 |
| | > 60 years | 2 |
| **Preceding diarrhea** | Absent | 0 |
| (within 4 weeks) | Present | 1 |
| **MRC sum score at admission** | 51-60 | 0 |
| | 41-50 | 2 |
| | 31-40 | 4 |
| | 0-30 | 6 |

**At week 1 of admission (score range 0-12):**

| Variable | Category | Points |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Age at onset** | <= 40 years | 0 |
| | 41-60 years | 1 |
| | > 60 years | 2 |
| **Preceding diarrhea** | Absent | 0 |
| (within 4 weeks) | Present | 1 |
| **MRC sum score at week 1** | 51-60 | 0 |
| | 41-50 | 3 |
| | 31-40 | 6 |
| | 0-30 | 9 |

**Note:** MRC sum score is weighted more heavily in the week-1 version.

#### Predictive Accuracy

| Timing | Predicting outcome at | AUC |
|--------|----------------------|-----|
| Admission | 4 weeks | 0.69 (95% CI: 0.63-0.74) |
| Admission | 26 weeks | 0.67 (95% CI: 0.62-0.72) |
| Week 1 | 4 weeks | **0.78 (95% CI: 0.71-0.85)** |
| Week 1 | 26 weeks | 0.70 (95% CI: 0.64-0.75) |

**International validation** (IGOS cohort) confirmed good discriminative ability.

**Clinical use:**
- **mEGOS >= 6** at admission = poor prognosis (used as inclusion criterion for SID-GBS trial)
- Calculate at admission AND at day 7 for improved accuracy
- Recommended by EAN/PNS 2023 guideline for routine use

> **Citation:** Walgaard C, et al. Prediction of respiratory insufficiency in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Ann Neurol*. 2010;67:781-787.
> **Citation:** Doorn PA, et al. European Academy of Neurology/Peripheral Nerve Society Guideline on diagnosis and treatment of Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Eur J Neurol*. 2023;30:3646-3674.

---

### 2.4 EGRIS (Erasmus GBS Respiratory Insufficiency Score)

Predicts probability of mechanical ventilation within the first week of admission.

#### Scoring System

| Variable | Category | Points |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Days from onset to admission** | > 7 days | 0 |
| | 4-7 days | 1 |
| | <= 3 days | 2 |
| **Facial and/or bulbar weakness** | Absent | 0 |
| | Present | 1 |
| **MRC sum score at admission** | 51-60 | 0 |
| | 41-50 | 1 |
| | 31-40 | 2 |
| | 21-30 | 3 |
| | <= 20 | 4 |

**Total score range:** 0-7

#### Risk Stratification

| EGRIS Score | Risk Category | Estimated MV Risk |
|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| 0-2 | Low | ~4% |
| 3-4 | Intermediate | ~24% |
| 5-7 | High | ~65-90% |

#### Validation Performance

- **Original AUC:** 0.84
- **International validation (IGOS):** AUC = 0.86 (95% CI: 0.80-0.91)
  - Europe/North America: 0.86 (0.80-0.93)
  - Asia: 0.80 (0.62-0.91)
- **mEGRIS (simplified version):** AUC = 0.84 (0.80-0.88) - requires less information, can be used at multiple time points

**Clinical use:**
- Calculate at admission for ALL patients with suspected GBS
- EGRIS >= 5: high risk - transfer to ICU for monitoring
- Recommended by EAN/PNS 2023 guideline and NCS neuroprognostication guidelines

> **Citation:** Walgaard C, et al. Prediction of respiratory insufficiency in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Ann Neurol*. 2010;67:781-787.
> **Citation:** Galassi G, et al. International validation of the Erasmus Guillain-Barre syndrome respiratory insufficiency score. *Eur J Neurol*. 2022;29:2690-2697. PMC9306880

### 2.5 R-ODS (Rasch-built Overall Disability Scale)

A linearly weighted scale that captures **activity and social participation limitations** in GBS, CIDP, and MGUSP.

- **24 items** ranging from easy ("reading a newspaper," "eating") to difficult ("standing for hours," "running")
- Each item scored 0-2 (0 = unable, 1 = with difficulty, 2 = easily performed)
- **Total range:** 0 (most disabled) to 48 (no limitations)
- Provides a **wider range of item difficulties** than the Hughes scale, better targeting patients at different ability levels
- Particularly useful in the **recovery phase** (months 3-12) when patients are between Hughes grades 1-3
- Used in recent clinical trials (e.g., ANX005 Phase 3) as a secondary endpoint
- Also called **I-RODS** (Inflammatory RODS) in some publications

> **Citation:** van Nes SI, et al. Rasch-built Overall Disability Scale (R-ODS) for immune-mediated peripheral neuropathies. *Neurology*. 2011;76:337-345. PMID: 21263135

---

### 2.6 Grip Strength Dynamometry

- Measured with **Vigorimeter** (validated in GBS/CIDP) or **JAMAR hydraulic dynamometer** (gold standard)
- Provides **continuous, quantitative** measure of distal upper limb strength
- **Validated for use** in GBS, CIDP, and gammopathy-associated polyneuropathy
- Test-retest reliability: ICC > 0.80 (good to excellent)
- **Minimum detectable change (95%):** variable, ranging 14.5-98.5% depending on population
- Useful for serial monitoring but requires relatively **large percentage changes** to confirm real change
- Best used as **adjunct** to MRC sum score, not as standalone measure

> **Citation:** Merkies ISJ, et al. Assessing grip strength in healthy individuals and patients with immune-mediated polyneuropathies. *Muscle Nerve*. 2000;23:1393-1401.

---

### 2.7 Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) Trends

FVC is a critical bedside monitoring tool (see Section 5 for detailed ICU protocol).

- **> 20 mL/kg:** generally safe, ward monitoring may suffice
- **< 20 mL/kg:** ICU-level monitoring required
- **< 15 mL/kg:** consider elective intubation
- **< 10 mL/kg:** intubation strongly indicated
- **Decline > 30% in 24 hours:** urgent concern regardless of absolute value
- **Trend is more important than single value** - serial measurements (at minimum every 4-6 hours in progressive patients) essential

## 3. ELECTROPHYSIOLOGICAL MONITORING

### 3.1 Timing of Nerve Conduction Studies

Timing critically affects both diagnostic yield and prognostic accuracy.

#### Recommended Protocol

| Study | Timing | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **First NCS** | Within 10 days of onset | Baseline classification; may be normal or equivocal |
| **Early follow-up** | Within 3 weeks of onset | Reclassification; captures demyelinating features |
| **Late follow-up** | 3-10 weeks after onset | Definitive subtyping; axonal degeneration evident |
| **Optional 3-month NCS** | ~12 weeks | Recovery monitoring; persistent axonal damage assessment |

**Key finding:** Two NCS (first within 2 weeks, second at 3-8 weeks) is generally sufficient for accurate electrophysiological classification in GBS.

#### Diagnostic Yield

- **First NCS:** 75% classifiable (Hadden criteria), 68% (Rajabally criteria)
- **Second NCS adds:** 19% additional classification (Hadden), 14% (Rajabally)
- **Delay from onset does NOT influence first NCS yield** - clinical severity (GBS disability at nadir) is the main determinant

> **Citation:** Uncini A, et al. Nerve conduction studies in Guillain-Barre syndrome: influence of timing and value of repeated measurements. *J Neurol Sci*. 2021;420:117261. PMID: 33352506

---

### 3.2 Serial NCS: Pattern Changes and Reclassification

**Between first and early follow-up NCS (within 3 weeks):**
- 33.3% showed significant classification changes (Hadden criteria)
- 18.3% showed significant changes (Rajabally criteria)

**Between first and late follow-up NCS (3-10 weeks):**
- 23.3% showed changes (Hadden criteria)
- 21.7% showed changes (Rajabally criteria)

**Key reclassification pattern:** 71.4% (Hadden) and 46.2% (Rajabally) of all late changes were already evident at the early follow-up.

**Equivocal NCS at admission** shows the greatest instability; maximum shift is toward AIDP on repeat testing.

**Conditions associated with delayed classification changes:**
- Very early initial NCS (within first few days)
- Mild symptoms at baseline
- Persistent electrophysiological deterioration at 3 weeks post-onset

> **Citation:** Rajabally YA, et al. Serial nerve conduction studies in Guillain-Barre syndrome: its usefulness and precise timing. *J Clin Neurophysiol*. 2024;41(3):254-260. PMID: 38436391

---

### 3.3 Prognostic Value of Electrophysiological Patterns

#### AIDP vs. Axonal Subtypes

| Feature | AIDP | AMAN/AMSAN |
|---------|------|------------|
| Mechanism | Demyelination | Axonal degeneration |
| Recovery pattern | Generally faster | Bimodal: rapid or very slow |
| Walking at 6 months | ~80-90% | Variable (rapid recovery in 11-41% of AMAN) |
| Relation to prognosis | Demyelination burden predicts good outcome | Axonal burden at 1 and 3 months predicts poor outcome |

**Key prognostic finding:** Electrophysiological sub-typing at admission alone does not reliably predict 6-month outcome. The **burden of axonal change at 1 month** is a stronger predictor than admission subtype.

> **Citation:** Gupta A, et al. Prognostic significance of serial nerve conduction in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Neurol India*. 2022;70(5):2086-2091.

#### What to Look For on Serial NCS

| Finding | Prognostic Implication |
|---------|----------------------|
| Progressive conduction block | Active demyelination; potentially reversible |
| Improving conduction block | Recovery of demyelination; favorable sign |
| Falling CMAP amplitudes | Axonal degeneration developing; poor sign |
| Mean CMAP < 20% of LLN | Poor functional outcome predictor |
| Improving CMAP amplitudes | Axonal recovery or reversible conduction block |
| F-wave absence evolving to presence | Recovery sign |

---

### 3.4 Inexcitable Nerves: Dose-Response Prognosis

Inexcitable motor (IM) nerves (no recordable CMAP with supramaximal stimulation) represent severe nerve injury.

**Prevalence:** 1.4-19% have at least one IM nerve; 52.3% in a hospitalized GBS cohort (n = 88)

#### Dose-Response Relationship with Outcome (6 months)

| Number of IM Nerves | Complete Recovery | Poor Outcome |
|---------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| 0 | 81.2% (26/32) | 18.8% |
| 1 | 60.0% (6/10) | 40.0% |
| 2 | 78.6% (11/14) | 21.4% |
| 4 | 42.9% (3/7) | 57.1% |
| 6 | **23.1% (3/13)** | **76.9%** |

*(p < 0.01 for trend)*

**Optimal prognostic cutoff:** >= 4 IM nerves
- Sensitivity: 52%
- Specificity: 87.8%
- PPV: 70%, NPV: 76.8%

**Correlation with mechanical ventilation:**
- 0 IM nerves: 19% MV
- 1 IM nerve: 45.5% MV
- 6 IM nerves: **92.3% MV** (p < 0.01 for trend)

**Subtype-specific prognosis with IM nerves:**
- Axonal GBS with all recordable CMAPs: 75% poor recovery
- AIDP with all recordable CMAPs: 9.1% poor recovery (p = 0.01)
- When IM nerves are present: difference between AIDP and axonal GBS is **no longer significant** (p = 0.40)

> **Citation:** Gupta S, et al. Dose-effect relationship of motor nerve inexcitability on outcome in Guillain-Barre syndrome: a prospective cohort study. *J Neurol*. 2024. PMC10789402

## 4. MONITORING TREATMENT RESPONSE

### 4.1 Treatment-Related Fluctuation (TRF) vs. Treatment Failure

#### Definitions

**Treatment-related fluctuation (TRF):**
Clinical deterioration within 8 weeks of onset (median 18 days, range 10-54 days) following initial improvement or stabilization, defined as:
1. Improvement of >= 1 Hughes grade OR > 5 MRC sum score points after treatment, followed by worsening of >= 1 grade or > 5 points; OR
2. Stabilization > 1 week post-treatment, followed by same degree of worsening

**Incidence:** 5-26% of GBS patients (recent IGOS data: ~5%)

**Treatment failure (no consensus definition):**
- No improvement of >= 1 Hughes grade after completing standard therapy in patients with Hughes >= 3
- Poor prognosis defined as mEGOS >= 6

**A-CIDP must be considered** when: >= 3 fluctuations occur, or symptoms progress beyond 8 weeks

> **Citation:** Alessandro L, et al. *Arq Neuropsiquiatr*. 2022;80(5):516-522.

---

### 4.2 Timeline for Expected Improvement

#### After Plasma Exchange (PE)

- PE typically consists of **5 exchanges over 7-14 days** (1-1.5 plasma volumes per session)
- **Expected improvement timeline:** Clinical improvement often begins during or shortly after the PE series
- **Median time to improve 1 disability grade:** 16.5 days
- Improvement may begin as early as after the 2nd-3rd exchange
- PE speeds recovery more than it changes final outcome

#### After IVIg

- Standard dose: **0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days** (total 2 g/kg)
- **Median time to improve 1 disability grade:** 14 days
- Should be started within 2 weeks of symptom onset for maximum benefit
- Therapeutic effects notably diminished when treatment delayed > 2 weeks
- IVIg is equivalent to PE in efficacy

#### When to Assess Treatment Response

- **Day 7-14 after completing treatment:** earliest meaningful assessment point
- **Week 4:** key time point for outcome assessment (mEGOS prediction target)
- **Important:** Some patients continue to worsen for days after starting treatment before improvement begins; this does NOT necessarily indicate treatment failure

---

### 4.3 IgG Level Monitoring During and After Treatment

#### During IVIg

- Standard IVIg (2 g/kg over 5 days) produces highly variable IgG rise between patients
- **Mean increase in IgG (deltaIgG) at 2 weeks:** 7.8 g/L (SD: 5.6 g/L)
- **Low deltaIgG** (below mean) is independently associated with poor outcome
- Only **33% of severely affected and mechanically ventilated patients** achieve target deltaIgG > 7.3 g/L, vs. 74% of mildly affected patients
- **Pharmacokinetic explanation:** more severely ill patients have higher IgG catabolism (possibly through FcRn-mediated clearance or third spacing)

**Clinical recommendation:** Measuring serum IgG before and 2 weeks after IVIg may identify patients with rapid IVIg clearance who might benefit from dose adjustment (though second IVIg course is NOT recommended by guidelines).

> **Citation:** Kuitwaard K, et al. Pharmacokinetics of intravenous immunoglobulin and outcome in Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Ann Neurol*. 2009;66:597-603.
> **Citation:** Fokkink WJR, et al. Population pharmacokinetic modelling of intravenous immunoglobulin treatment in patients with Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Clin Pharmacokinet*. 2022;61:1409-1420. PMC9439991

#### During PE

- PE removes approximately 50-70% of IgG per session (depending on volume exchanged)
- **Monitor total IgG** before and after PE series
- Post-PE IgG rebound occurs over 1-3 weeks as endogenous production resumes
- Also monitor: coagulation factors (PT, PTT), fibrinogen, calcium (citrate-related), platelets

---

### 4.4 When to Consider Treatment Switch

The **EAN/PNS 2023 guideline** makes the following key recommendations:

1. **Do NOT combine IVIg + PE** (no benefit demonstrated in any sequence)
2. **Do NOT give a second IVIg course** in patients with poor prognosis (SID-GBS trial negative)
3. **FOR TRF:** Repeated IVIg or PE may be effective (observational data)
4. **FOR A-CIDP:** If >= 3 fluctuations or progression > 8 weeks, consider reclassification and chronic immunotherapy (corticosteroids, maintenance IVIg/PE)

**Practical considerations for PE after IVIg failure (or vice versa):**
- No RCT evidence supports switching
- PE after IVIg will remove recently administered immunoglobulins
- Anecdotal/case series evidence exists but guidelines do not recommend it
- If considering switching: wait until TRF can be excluded (> 1-2 weeks stability assessment)

> **Citation:** Doorn PA, et al. EAN/PNS Guideline on diagnosis and treatment of Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Eur J Neurol*. 2023;30:3646-3674.

---

### 4.5 ANX005 (C1q Complement Inhibitor) - Emerging Treatment with Monitoring Implications

The ANX005 Phase 3 trial (2024-2025) demonstrated that complement biomarkers can serve as treatment response indicators:

- **Single 30 mg/kg infusion** produced 2.4-fold improvement on GBS-DS at week 8 (p = 0.0058)
- **MRC sum score improvement at day 8:** p < 0.0001 (earliest detected treatment benefit)
- **NfL reduction:** 11.2% relative to placebo between weeks 2-4 (p = 0.03) - NfL decline as a biomarker of reduced axonal damage under treatment
- **Ventilation reduction:** median 28 fewer days on artificial ventilation (p = 0.0356)
- **Time to walk independently:** 31-day reduction (p = 0.0211)

**Monitoring implication:** If/when complement inhibitors become available, serial NfL may serve as a pharmacodynamic biomarker to confirm treatment effect.

> **Citation:** Annexon Biosciences. Phase 3 ANX005 topline results. Press release, 2024. BLA submission expected H1 2025.

## 5. ICU MONITORING PROTOCOLS FOR GBS

### 5.1 Respiratory Monitoring

#### FVC (Forced Vital Capacity)

| FVC Threshold | Action |
|--------------|--------|
| **> 20 mL/kg** | Ward monitoring, serial FVC |
| **< 20 mL/kg** | Transfer to ICU; close monitoring |
| **< 15 mL/kg** | Consider elective intubation |
| **< 10 mL/kg** | Intubation strongly indicated |
| **Decline > 30% in 24 hours** | Intubation irrespective of absolute value |
| **FVC > 15 mL/kg** (ventilated patient) | May suggest readiness for extubation |
| **Decline > 50% of VC** | Predictive of intubation within 24 hours |

**Monitoring frequency:**
- **Rapidly progressive GBS:** Every 2-4 hours
- **Moderate progression:** Every 4-6 hours
- **Stabilized patient:** Every 6-12 hours
- **Enter all values on a trend chart** for easy visualization

#### MIP/NIF (Maximal Inspiratory Pressure / Negative Inspiratory Force)

| MIP Threshold | Significance |
|---------------|--------------|
| **> -30 cmH2O** (i.e., less negative) | ICU admission indicated |
| **-25 cmH2O** | Consider intubation |

#### MEP (Maximal Expiratory Pressure)

| MEP Threshold | Significance |
|---------------|--------------|
| **< 40 cmH2O** | ICU admission indicated; impaired cough |

#### The "20-30-40 Rule" (with caveats)

Traditional rule: intubate if FVC < 20 mL/kg, MIP < -30 cmH2O, or MEP < 40 cmH2O.

**Important limitations (current evidence):**
- Multivariable analysis shows only FVC is an independent predictor; MIP and MEP do not add independent information
- MIP and MEP are more effort-dependent and less reproducible than FVC
- FVC alone with trend analysis is preferred by recent authorities
- **Pulse oximetry and blood gases are INADEQUATE for early detection** - hypoxemia and hypercarbia are very late manifestations

#### Bedside Assessment (when spirometry unavailable)

- **Single breath count (SBC):** Count to the highest number possible in one breath. Normal > 25; < 15-20 suggests FVC < 20 mL/kg
- **Shoulder shrug and head lift:** Loss correlates with diaphragm weakness
- **Cough strength:** Weak cough suggests MEP < 40 cmH2O
- **Paradoxical breathing:** Inward abdominal movement during inspiration = diaphragm paralysis

---

### 5.2 Autonomic Monitoring

Autonomic dysfunction occurs in **up to 65%** of GBS patients and is a significant cause of death.

#### Cardiovascular Monitoring

| Parameter | Method | Frequency | Alert Thresholds |
|-----------|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Heart rate** | Continuous telemetry | Continuous | Bradycardia < 50 or sudden tachycardia |
| **Blood pressure** | Arterial line or frequent NIBP | Every 1-2 hours (ICU) | Systolic variation > 85 mmHg/day |
| **ECG rhythm** | Continuous cardiac monitor | Continuous | Any new arrhythmia |

**Specific dysautonomia manifestations to monitor:**

1. **Tachycardia** (most common, ~50%): usually sinus; may alternate with bradycardia
2. **Hypertension** (often episodic): may be severe; avoid aggressive treatment (risk of rebound hypotension)
3. **Hypotension** (orthostatic or sustained): volume challenge first; vasopressors cautiously
4. **Bradycardia and asystole** (most dangerous):
   - Can be triggered by benign procedures (IV insertion, suctioning, turning)
   - **Daily systolic BP variation > 85 mmHg is a sensitive predictor** of dangerous bradyarrhythmias
   - Temporary pacing may be required; rare cases need permanent pacemaker
   - Have atropine immediately available at bedside
5. **Reduced heart rate variability:** Loss of normal respiratory-related HR variation is an early sign

**Autonomic monitoring should continue even after motor recovery has begun**, as dysautonomia can persist or emerge in the recovery phase.

> **Citation:** Peric S, et al. Autonomic involvement in Guillain-Barre syndrome: an update. *Clin Auton Res*. 2019;29:289-299.

---

### 5.3 Nutrition Monitoring in Ventilated GBS Patients

GBS patients on mechanical ventilation have unique nutritional challenges including gastroparesis, ileus, and high metabolic demands from critical illness.

#### Feeding Protocol

| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Within 24-48 hours of ICU admission** | Initiate enteral nutrition if hemodynamically stable |
| **Start low, increase gradually** | Begin at 10-20 mL/hr, advance by 10-20 mL/hr every 6-8 hours |
| **Target energy delivery** | 25-30 kcal/kg/day (use indirect calorimetry if available) |
| **Protein target** | 1.2-2.0 g/kg/day (higher range for acute phase) |

#### GI Tolerance Monitoring

- **Gastric residual volume (GRV):** check every 4-6 hours initially; GRV > 500 mL concerning
- **Abdominal examination:** daily assessment for distension, tenderness, absent bowel sounds
- **Vomiting/aspiration:** elevate head of bed 30-45 degrees
- **Prokinetics:** consider erythromycin or metoclopramide for gastroparesis
- **Post-pyloric feeding:** if gastroparesis persists despite prokinetics

#### Metabolic Monitoring

| Parameter | Frequency | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Phosphate** | Daily (first 5-7 days) | Refeeding syndrome risk; 30% drop triggers intervention |
| **Potassium, magnesium** | Daily | Refeeding risk; arrhythmia prevention |
| **Glucose** | Every 4-6 hours | Stress hyperglycemia; insulin resistance common |
| **Pre-albumin** | Weekly | Nutritional adequacy marker |
| **Nitrogen balance** | Weekly | Protein adequacy assessment |
| **Thiamine** | Before feeding starts | High-dose thiamine if malnourished or at refeeding risk |

> **Citation:** Singer P, et al. ESPEN practical and partially revised guideline: clinical nutrition in the intensive care unit. *Clin Nutr*. 2023;42:1671-1689.

---

### 5.4 Infection Surveillance

Ventilated GBS patients have prolonged ICU stays (median 30-50 days) with high infection risk.

#### Monitoring Protocol

| Infection Type | Prevention | Surveillance |
|---------------|------------|--------------|
| **VAP** (Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia) | HOB elevation, oral care, subglottic drainage, daily sedation holidays | Daily sputum assessment; cultures with clinical suspicion; CXR for new infiltrates |
| **CAUTI** (Catheter-Associated UTI) | Minimize catheter use; daily assessment for removal | Urinalysis/culture if fever or clinical suspicion |
| **CLABSI** (Central Line Infection) | Strict insertion bundles; daily line assessment; remove when no longer needed | Blood cultures with fever; exit site inspection |
| **Skin/pressure ulcers** | Turning schedule (every 2 hours); pressure-relieving mattress | Daily skin assessment |
| **DVT/PE** | Pharmacologic prophylaxis; sequential compression devices | Clinical assessment; D-dimer/duplex US if suspected |

**Tracheostomy timing:**
- Consider at **10-14 days** of intubation if weaning unlikely soon
- Delaying beyond 14 days increases VAP risk
- GBS patients may require prolonged ventilation (weeks to months)

**General infection monitoring:**
- **Temperature:** continuous or every 4 hours
- **WBC count + differential:** daily in acute phase, then 2-3x/week
- **Procalcitonin:** consider for distinguishing infection from GBS-related inflammation
- **CRP:** daily in acute phase, then 2-3x/week

---

### 5.5 Laboratory Monitoring Schedule

#### Daily (Acute Phase)

| Test | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| CBC with differential | Infection surveillance; PE-related thrombocytopenia |
| Electrolytes (Na, K, Cl, CO2) | Dysautonomia-related SIADH; feeding; PE complications |
| Glucose | Stress hyperglycemia; autonomic dysfunction |
| Calcium (ionized) | Citrate anticoagulation during PE |
| Phosphate, magnesium | Refeeding risk; arrhythmia prevention |
| PT/PTT (if on PE) | Coagulopathy monitoring after PE |
| Fibrinogen (if on PE) | Depletion from PE |

#### Every 2-3 Days (After Stabilization)

| Test | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| CBC | Infection; transfusion needs |
| Renal function (BUN, creatinine) | Drug dosing; hydration |
| Liver function | Drug-related hepatotoxicity; critical illness cholestasis |
| CRP | Inflammatory trend |

#### Weekly

| Test | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| Total IgG | Post-PE recovery; post-IVIg monitoring |
| Pre-albumin | Nutritional status |
| Thyroid function | Critically ill euthyroid syndrome |
| Vitamin levels (D, B12) | Prolonged ICU stays |

#### At Specific Time Points

| Test | Timing | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|
| sNfL | Admission, week 2, week 4 | Axonal damage monitoring and prognosis |
| Anti-ganglioside panel | Diagnosis, 3 months, 6 months | Titer trajectory predicts recovery |
| C3, C4 | Admission, weekly during treatment | Complement activation monitoring |
| CSF analysis | At diagnosis only (repeat only if diagnostic uncertainty) | Albuminocytologic dissociation |
| NCS/EMG | Weeks 1-2, weeks 3-8, optional month 3 | Subtype classification and prognosis |

## 6. PROGNOSTIC FACTORS SPECIFIC TO RECURRENT GBS

### 6.1 Epidemiology of Recurrent GBS

- **Recurrence rate:** 2-5% of all GBS patients
- Recurrences can occur from months to decades after the initial episode
- **Younger patients at higher risk:** Recurrent patients mean age 34.2 years vs. non-recurrent 46.9 years (p = 0.001)
- More common in patients with MFS (p = 0.049) or milder initial symptoms (p = 0.011)
- **Preceding respiratory and gastrointestinal infections** associated with recurrence (p = 0.015 and p = 0.007, respectively)
- Distinct from A-CIDP: each episode must meet GBS criteria with complete or near-complete recovery between episodes (minimum 2-month interval if fully resolved; 4 months if partially resolved)

> **Citation:** Das A, et al. Recurrent Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Electromyogr Clin Neurophysiol*. 2008;48:95-102. PMID: 18931012

---

### 6.2 Does Prior Episode Predict Current Episode Severity?

**Key findings from the literature:**

- **Similar symptom pattern:** Clinical symptoms in a first episode are similar to following episodes in individual patients (e.g., GBS or MFS but never both) - suggesting a consistent immunological target
- **Severity varies between episodes:** While the pattern of symptoms is similar, the severity and the nature of the preceding infection can differ substantially between episodes
- **First episode comparable to monophasic GBS:** No difference in course or outcome between the first episode of recurrent GBS and non-recurrent GBS
- **First and second episodes similar:** In recurrent cases, the first and second episodes were similar in course and outcome
- **No clear escalation pattern:** There is no consistent evidence that successive episodes become progressively more severe
- **Full recovery is typical:** The majority of recurrent GBS patients undergo full recovery

**Mechanistic insight:** Genetic or immunological host factors likely play a dominant role, as patients can develop similar symptoms after different preceding infections.

> **Citation:** Das A, et al. *Electromyogr Clin Neurophysiol*. 2008;48:95-102.
> **Citation:** Imam YZ. Recurrent Guillain-Barre syndrome: clinical profile and outcome. *Cureus*. 2023;15(7):e42429. PMID: 37505408

---

### 6.3 Age as a Prognostic Factor (Data Relevant to Patients ~50 Years)

Age is one of the three variables in the mEGOS prognostic score and is consistently identified as an independent predictor of GBS outcome.

#### Age-Stratified Outcome Data

| Age Group | Hughes >= 3 at Admission | Independent Walking at 3 Months | Required MV |
|-----------|-------------------------|--------------------------------|-------------|
| **< 60 years** | 69.1% | 66% achieved | 32.9% |
| **60-69 years** | ~96% | **76.2%** | ~42% |
| **>= 70 years** | ~96% | **33.4%** | 41.6% |

**For patients aged 41-60 years (the relevant range for a 53-year-old patient):**
- mEGOS assigns **1 point** for age (vs. 0 for <= 40, 2 for > 60)
- This age range carries intermediate risk
- Age 41-60 is NOT an independent risk factor for mortality in the available data
- The major age-related prognostic breakpoint is at **>= 70 years** where outcomes deteriorate sharply

#### Independent Prognostic Factors by Multivariate Analysis

- **Age >= 70 years:** OR = 10.3 (95% CI: 1.3-77, p = 0.023) for unfavorable 3-month outcome
- **Invasive mechanical ventilation:** OR = 7.3 (p = 0.044) for unfavorable outcome
- **Axonal subtype:** independent predictor of poor outcome
- **Lower nadir MRC score:** strongest predictor across all studies

#### Specific Considerations for a 53-Year-Old Patient with Recurrent GBS

1. **Age per se** is a moderate but not major risk factor at 53 (1 mEGOS point)
2. **Recurrent GBS** does not systematically worsen prognosis compared to monophasic disease
3. **Prior good recovery** from childhood episode suggests capacity for nerve regeneration
4. **Current episode severity** (nadir MRC, ventilation need, axonal involvement) is the dominant prognostic factor
5. **Electrophysiology at 1 month** will provide the best prognostic information about recovery trajectory
6. **Serial NfL** at 2 and 4 weeks will quantify axonal damage burden

> **Citation:** Briseno-Godinez ME, et al. Prognostic factors in elderly patients with Guillain-Barre syndrome: does age matter? *J Clin Neuromuscul Dis*. 2021;23:18-25. PMC8442146
> **Citation:** Wen P, et al. Risk factors for the severity of Guillain-Barre syndrome and predictors of short-term prognosis of severe Guillain-Barre syndrome. *Sci Rep*. 2021;11:11578.

## 7. FORMAL NEUROPROGNOSTICATION GUIDELINES (Busl 2023)

The Neurocritical Care Society published the first GRADE-based systematic review dedicated to neuroprognostication in adult GBS patients. The guideline synthesizes evidence on clinical, electrophysiological, and biomarker predictors and provides a framework for goals-of-care discussions. *[Level 1 — GRADE systematic review]*

### 7.1 Validated Clinical Predictors

The following clinical variables are validated for neuroprognostication in GBS:

- **Age:** older age independently predicts worse functional outcome
- **Preceding diarrhea:** associated with axonal subtypes (anti-GM1/GD1a) and poorer short-term outcome
- **GBS disability score at admission:** higher disability at presentation correlates with worse outcome at 6 months
- **MRC sum score:** the single strongest bedside predictor of both respiratory failure and walking ability at 6 months

### 7.2 mEGOS for Outcome Prediction

The modified Erasmus GBS Outcome Score (mEGOS), calculated at admission and at day 7, predicts inability to walk independently at 6 months:

- **mEGOS at admission:** AUC 0.78 for inability to walk at 6 months
- **mEGOS at day 7 (week-1 version):** more accurate than admission version due to incorporation of observed disease trajectory
- mEGOS is recommended by both EAN/PNS 2023 and NCS neuroprognostication guidelines as a standard prognostic tool

### 7.3 Electrophysiology as Prognostic Tool

- **Inexcitable motor nerves in >= 4 nerves:** probability of walking at 6 months drops from 81% to 23%
- This represents the single most powerful electrophysiological prognostic finding
- NCS should be performed within the first 2 weeks and repeated at 3-8 weeks for definitive subtyping

### 7.4 Neurofilament Light Chain (NfL) as Prognostic Adjunct

NfL adds meaningful prognostic value beyond clinical models alone:

- **C-statistic improvement when NfL is added to clinical models:** +0.109 (van Tilburg 2024)
- Serial NfL measurements (admission, week 2, week 4) provide increasing prognostic accuracy
- Week-4 NfL is the strongest single biomarker predictor (OR 2.79 for inability to walk at 26 weeks)

### 7.5 Goals-of-Care: Avoiding Premature Prognostic Nihilism

The guideline strongly emphasizes:

- **Recovery continues up to 1-2 years** after GBS onset — premature prognostic conclusions are inappropriate
- Even severely affected patients (ventilated, axonal subtype) have meaningful recovery rates
- Goals-of-care discussions should be **informed but cautious**, presenting realistic expectations while acknowledging the potential for late improvement
- **Prognostic nihilism** — concluding that recovery is impossible based on early severity — should be actively avoided
- Family/patient communication should be staged: early discussions focus on short-term trajectory, with long-term prognosis refined over weeks to months

> **Citation:** Busl KM, et al. Guidelines for Neuroprognostication in Adults with Guillain-Barré Syndrome. *Neurocrit Care*. 2023;38:791-812. PMID: 36964442

## 8. EGRIS INTERNATIONAL VALIDATION (Doets 2022)

The Erasmus GBS Respiratory Insufficiency Score (EGRIS) was originally developed in a Dutch cohort. A 2022 study validated EGRIS in the large international IGOS cohort (n=1500), confirming its predictive value for mechanical ventilation and providing recalibrated risk estimates for European/North American populations. *[Level 2 — prospektiv kohortstudie, IGOS n=1500]*

### 8.1 International Validation Performance

- **Original EGRIS AUC:** 0.84 (Dutch derivation cohort)
- **International validation AUC:** 0.86 (95% CI 0.80-0.91)
- The original EGRIS slightly **overestimates MV risk** in European/North American patients
- A **recalibrated EGRIS-Eu/NA** was developed that provides more accurate estimates for this population

### 8.2 EGRIS Components

EGRIS can be calculated at admission using three bedside variables — no additional tests are needed:

| Variable | Category | Points |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Days between onset and admission** | > 7 days | 0 |
| | 4-7 days | 1 |
| | <= 3 days | 2 |
| **Facial and/or bulbar weakness** | Absent | 0 |
| | Present | 1 |
| **MRC sum score at admission** | 51-60 | 0 |
| | 41-50 | 1 |
| | 31-40 | 2 |
| | 21-30 | 3 |
| | <= 20 | 4 |

**Total score range:** 0-7

### 8.3 Recalibrated EGRIS Risk Stratification (Eu/NA)

| EGRIS Score | Risk Category | Estimated MV Risk |
|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| 0-2 | Low | ~4% |
| 3-4 | Intermediate | ~25% |
| 5-7 | High | ~65% |

### 8.4 Clinical Implications

- EGRIS should be calculated for **every GBS patient at admission**
- Score >= 5: high risk — transfer to ICU for close respiratory monitoring
- The recalibrated version (EGRIS-Eu/NA) is preferred for European and North American populations
- EGRIS complements mEGOS: EGRIS predicts **respiratory failure**, mEGOS predicts **functional outcome**

> **Citation:** Doets AY, et al. International Validation of the Erasmus Guillain-Barré Syndrome Respiratory Insufficiency Score. *Ann Neurol*. 2022;91(1):105-116. PMID: 35106830

## 9. STRUCTURED PROGNOSTIC DECISION POINTS

This section provides a practical timeline with if/then logic for the treating team, integrating biomarkers, electrophysiology, and clinical scales into actionable decision points.

### 9.1 Day 7 (from symptom onset)

```
ASSESSMENTS:
- Calculate mEGOS (week-1 version) — more accurate than admission mEGOS
- Assess deltoid MRC bilaterally
- Request NCS/EMG (first study, if not already done)
- First NfL measurement (if not done at admission)
- EGRIS score for respiratory risk (if not calculated at admission)

DECISION LOGIC:
  IF deltoid MRC ≤2 bilaterally
    → 95% probability of needing >14 days mechanical ventilation (Walgaard 2017)
    → Plan tracheostomy if not already done
  IF EGRIS ≥5 and patient not yet in ICU
    → Transfer to ICU immediately
```

### 9.2 Day 14

```
ASSESSMENTS:
- Second NfL measurement — KEY decision data point
- NCS results: subtype classification
- First meaningful clinical assessment post-tracheostomy (if applicable)
- Assess for TRF (treatment-related fluctuation)

DECISION LOGIC:
  NfL trajectory:
    IF NfL rising: ongoing axonal damage → consider treatment escalation
    IF NfL stable/falling: biological stabilization → continue current management

  NCS results:
    IF inexcitable nerves ≥4 motor
      → Walking at 6 months drops to 23%
      → Inform family: prolonged rehabilitation expected
      → Begin planning for long-term rehabilitation facility
    IF demyelinating pattern (AIDP)
      → Better prognosis — allow time for remyelination
      → Recovery may continue for months

  TRF assessment:
    IF worsening after initial improvement/stabilization
      → Repeat IVIg or PE may be considered
    IF no improvement and ≥3 fluctuations
      → Consider reclassification as A-CIDP
```

### 9.3 Day 28 (4 weeks)

```
ASSESSMENTS:
- Third NfL (strongest prognostic value — OR 2.79 for inability to walk at 26 weeks)
- mEGOS formal outcome prediction
- Repeat NCS for definitive subtyping
- Goals-of-care discussion with family, informed by ALL accumulated data

DECISION LOGIC:
  IF NfL at week 4 in highest quartile
    → Poor prognosis for independent walking at 6 months
    → Intensive rehabilitation planning
  IF NCS shows progressive axonal degeneration (falling CMAPs)
    → Prolonged recovery expected (months to years)
  IF NCS shows improving conduction block
    → Favorable sign — demyelination recovering

  Weaning assessment (for ventilated patients):
    → Cross-reference: Ventilator_Weaning_GBS_Protocol.md
    → FVC > 15 mL/kg may suggest readiness for weaning trials
```

### 9.4 Mortality Stratification

Understanding mortality risk by clinical status helps frame goals-of-care discussions:

| Clinical Status | Estimated Mortality | Source |
|----------------|--------------------:|--------|
| Non-ventilated GBS | 1-2% | Chakraborty 2020 |
| Mechanically ventilated, no dysautonomia | 2-4% | Chakraborty 2020 |
| Mechanically ventilated + dysautonomia | ~6% | Chakraborty 2020, PMID 31297663 |
| Overall ventilated GBS (large cohort) | 9.4% | Thille 2025, PMID 41114833, n=886 |

**Key interpretation:** Even in the highest-risk group (ventilated + dysautonomia), >90% of patients survive. This reinforces the message from Section 7: **avoid premature prognostic nihilism**.

> **Citation:** Walgaard C, et al. Early recognition of poor prognosis in Guillain-Barré syndrome. *Neurology*. 2017;89(3):228-236. PMID: 27538676
> **Citation:** Chakraborty T, et al. Guillain-Barré syndrome in the ICU. *Neurocrit Care*. 2020;32:708-722. PMID: 31297663
> **Citation:** Thille AW, et al. Outcomes of mechanically ventilated patients with Guillain-Barré syndrome. *Crit Care Med*. 2025. PMID: 41114833

## SUMMARY: PRACTICAL MONITORING CHECKLIST FOR ACUTE GBS

### On Admission (Day 1)

- [ ] Hughes functional grade
- [ ] MRC sum score (all 12 muscles)
- [ ] Calculate **EGRIS** (ventilation risk)
- [ ] Calculate **mEGOS** (outcome prediction)
- [ ] FVC, MIP, MEP baseline
- [ ] Serum NfL (send to lab)
- [ ] Anti-ganglioside panel (GM1, GD1a, GQ1b IgG and IgM)
- [ ] Serum C3, C4
- [ ] Total serum IgG
- [ ] Baseline bloods (CBC, electrolytes, LFT, coag, glucose, phosphate)
- [ ] Lumbar puncture (CSF protein, cell count, glucose)
- [ ] First NCS (if within 10 days of onset)
- [ ] Initiate continuous cardiac monitoring if any autonomic symptoms

### Daily (During Active Phase)

- [ ] Hughes grade
- [ ] MRC sum score
- [ ] FVC trends (every 2-6 hours depending on trajectory)
- [ ] Vital signs including BP variability assessment
- [ ] Continuous telemetry review
- [ ] Abdominal/nutritional assessment
- [ ] Infection surveillance (temperature, line sites, respiratory)
- [ ] Labs: CBC, electrolytes, glucose, calcium/phosphate (if on PE: add PT, PTT, fibrinogen)

### Day 7

- [ ] Recalculate **mEGOS** (week-1 version, more accurate)
- [ ] Serum IgG (if IVIg given, assess deltaIgG)
- [ ] Reassess treatment response: improved? stable? worsening?

### Week 2

- [ ] Serum NfL (second measurement - stronger predictor)
- [ ] Reassess for TRF (has initial improvement reversed?)
- [ ] First follow-up NCS (if initial NCS was within days of onset)

### Week 4

- [ ] Serum NfL (third measurement)
- [ ] Hughes grade, MRC sum score
- [ ] Anti-ganglioside titers (trend)
- [ ] Second NCS (definitive subtyping; axonal burden assessment)

### Month 3 and Month 6

- [ ] Hughes grade, MRC sum score, R-ODS
- [ ] Grip strength dynamometry
- [ ] Anti-ganglioside titers (persistence assessment)
- [ ] Optional third NCS (recovery monitoring)
- [ ] sNfL (should be normalizing)
- [ ] Assess for A-CIDP if any progressive or relapsing course
